# The surviving all-integer quantifier after the new constructions

8 October 2026. This audit isolates a remaining geometric question; it
does not prove any additional nonexistence theorem. In particular, **14430
is not excluded**. The complete class `15m²` and the positive class
`4830m²` are now settled in this project, but Erdős 634 is not.

## 1. An infinite branch-isolated test family

For every integer `h>=13` with `h=13 mod 600`, set

```
a=(h²−1)/3,  b=(2h+1)/3,  c=(h²+h+1)/3,
N_h=3(a+b)(a+2b)=h(h+2)(h²+4h+1)/3.
```

**Proposition.** These are distinct positive integer counts, with a
primitive ordered F3 arithmetic candidate at multiplier one. Every
tiling of `N_h`, if one exists, must belong to F3. The displayed candidates
have `a/b` tending to infinity. None is supplied at multiplier one by the
new mixed-corner criterion, the earlier nested-corner criterion, or the
uniform positive sectors and tails listed below.

**Proof.** The congruence `h=1 mod 3` makes the three sides integral.
Expansion gives `c²=a²+ab+b²`. If the unnormalized sides are
`a0=h²−1, b0=2h+1`, then

```
4a0=(2h−1)b0−3.
```

Their gcd is exactly 3 (it is odd, divides 3, and both sides are
divisible by 3). Thus `gcd(a,b)=1`.

Because `h=5 mod 8`, direct reduction of the displayed formula gives
`N_h=14 mod 16`. More explicitly, the unnormalized sides have residues
8 and 11 modulo 16, so `3(a0+b0)(a0+2b0)=14 mod 16`; division by 9
preserves that residue. Since `h=13 mod 25`, the factor `h+2` has
5-adic valuation one, whereas `h` and `h²+4h+1` are nonzero modulo 5.
Therefore `v5(N_h)=1`. The exhaustive branch-isolation theorem in
[the global-overlap note, Theorem 1.1](../../../docs/f3-global-overlap.md)
now excludes every branch except F3. In particular, new constructions
in W cannot resolve these counts. The classification/rationality inputs
and exact branch normalization are those identified in that theorem;
no proposed prime-case or W-scale-one exclusion is used.

The ratio `(h²−1)/(2h+1)` is increasing, starts at `56/9`, and tends to
infinity. For the new [mixed-corner theorem](../f3-general/MIXED_CORNER_THEOREM.md),

```
9D=9(b²+2ab−a²)=−h⁴+4h³+8h²−2<0       (h>=13).
```

For example, `h²−4h−8>0` throughout this range, making the last
inequality immediate. Therefore **no choice** of `q>=1,A,B>=0` can
satisfy `D=qc+Aa+Bb`. This rules out the entire stated method, not
only a selected decomposition.

The more flexible [nested-corner criterion](../../group2-nested-corners/PROOF.md)
requires, at multiplier one, some integer `k>=1` with
`bc−a²k` in the nonnegative semigroup generated by `a,b,c`. But

```
9(bc−a²)=−h⁴+2h³+5h²+3h<0              (h>=13),
```

so no such `k` exists. The negative inequality follows, for instance,
from `2/13+5/13²+3/13³<1` and monotonic decrease of these three terms.
Thus varying the nested-corner parameters cannot repair this family
at multiplier one either. Finally all four positive factors of `N_h`
increase with `h`, proving distinctness. ∎

## 2. Check against the other positive recipes

The theorem concerns the **displayed tile and multiplier**, except for
its separate global branch-isolation assertion. The following checks
avoid treating a sufficient threshold as a necessary one.

| Existing recipe | Exact reason it does not supply these displayed scale-one candidates |
| --- | --- |
| Five-region/gamma F3 construction, `0<a<2b` | Here `a/b>=56/9>2`. |
| New uniform F3 cone, `a/b<2725/1139` | Here `a/b>=56/9>2725/1139`. |
| New asymptotic semigroup sector, `a/b<1+sqrt(2)` | The ratio is already greater than 6. |
| Staircase F3 sector, `b<a<=5b/2`, `m>=2` | Both the ratio condition and multiplier condition fail. |
| Balanced corner sector, `3c>=4a`, `m>=2` | Both conditions fail; the more general nested-corner method is already excluded above at `m=1`. |
| All-integer Harries–Zhang F3 tail | The guaranteed bound is `m>=ceil(3(h+3)/4)`, which is at least 12. |
| W constructions, including adjacent parameters | Every W realization at these **counts** is excluded by branch isolation. |

For the tail calculation, `floor(a/b)=(h−1)/2`. Substitute this into
the safe sufficient bound
`ceil((3/2)(floor(a/b)+2))` in
[square-class tails, Appendix A](../../../docs/square-class-tails.md#appendix-a-every-integer-tails-for-all-norm-rows).
These results do supply higher square multiples; they do not supply
the original displayed scale-one instance. The balanced sector fails
because `4(h²−1)>3(h²+h+1)` for `h>=13`.

This is an audit of the stated repository recipes and their published
inputs, not a claim of exhaustive knowledge of every construction in
the literature. It also does not assert uniqueness of the F3 candidate
for every `h`: other primitive F3 tiles or scales at the same count
would require a separate arithmetic gate.

## 3. A complete gate at the first member: 14430

At `h=13` the count and target are

```
N=14430=2·3·5·13·37,
(a,b,c)=(56,9,61),
target=(3721,4514,1755).
```

This count is squarefree. Branch isolation leaves only F3, and the
integral-square scale formula forces `m=1`. Put `V=a+b`, `U=a+2b`.
Then

```
UV=14430/3=4810,       V<U<2V.
```

The complete divisor list has exactly one pair satisfying these
inequalities: `(V,U)=(65,74)`. It gives
`a=2V−U=56`, `b=U−V=9`, and `c²=3721=61²`.
Thus **any tiling of 14430 must use this ordered primitive F3 candidate
at scale one**, up to similarity/reflection and the normalizations in
the exhaustive classification. Changing the branch, the primitive
tile, or the residual scale cannot bypass this particular question.

Here `D=−2047` and `bc−a²=−2587`, so both semigroup constructions
fail outright. The existing tail starts at `m=12`.
The standalone checker independently enumerates the divisor gate and
compares it with the complete repository candidate enumerator; both
give the sole candidate above. It does **not** claim that the target
is impossible, and it is not a completed positional search.

## 4. What remains to close, and the attempted shortcut

The all-integer question still asks whether at least one geometric
realization exists among the complete finite arithmetic candidates for
**each** count. The new constructions eliminate large infinite sectors
but do not give that answer for the unbounded-ratio F3 front above.
This is a surviving mathematical quantifier, not a resource estimate.

One actual proposed shortcut has now been checked completely at 14430:
replace the difficult primitive candidate by a different tile or an
already covered branch at the same count. The exact arithmetic gate
shows that this is impossible. A second shortcut, merely changing the
integer parameters in the two strongest corner recipes, is excluded
on the entire displayed infinite family by their negative residuals.

Neither obstruction rules out a new dissection of the F3 target. Nor
do they prove infinitely many globally undecided counts: branch
isolation for every `h` is proved, whereas complete candidate uniqueness
is proved here only for 14430. A universal positive theorem for the
remaining F3 scale-one targets, or a new necessary-and-sufficient
geometric criterion, would address this gap; neither is established
by this audit. Other general small-scale branches also remain in the
[full-solution roadmap](../../../docs/full-solution-roadmap.md).

Reproduce the finite checks with `python check_all_n_gap.py`. The JSON
report records exact arithmetic and the limited scope of the result.
