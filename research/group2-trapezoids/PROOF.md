# A universal smaller ideal-trapezoid construction

Working proof, 3 October 2026. This is a constructive sufficient theorem;
the necessity of the resulting semigroup conditions is not asserted.

Let `(a,b,c)` be a primitive positive integral 120-degree triangle:

`c²=a²+ab+b²`.

Put `d=a+b−c`, `K=a²+b²`, `L=ab`. Then `0<d<min(a,b)`.
Use the 60-degree oblique coordinates `(u,v) -> (u+v/2,sqrt(3)v/2)`.
The ideal trapezoid `T(x,L)` has vertices
`(0,0),(x+L,0),(x,L),(0,L)`.

## The construction theorem

**Both `T(K−bd,L)` and `T(K−ad,L)` are tiled by the original tile.**
The first has `K+c²−2bd` tiles. Interchanging `a,b` gives the second.

Consequently, for either `s=K−bd` or `s=K−ad`, every ideal trapezoid
`T(x,nab)` with `n>=1` and `x−s in <a,b>` is tiled. Here `<a,b>` is the
nonnegative integer semigroup generated by `a,b`.

### A direction-changing trade inside a c-fold triangle

Set `z=((b/c),(a/c))` in the oblique basis. This is a Euclidean unit
vector, because `b²+ab+a²=c²`. It is the rotation through alpha of the
horizontal unit vector. Consider the c-fold tile with vertices

```
F=(0,0), A=(c²,0), B=(b²,ab).
```

Define

```
C=(bd,ad), E=(bc,0), D=(b(c+d),ad),
Q=C+b² z, R=E+b(c−b) z.
```

The norm equation implies

```
b(c−b)−cd = ad.
```

The c-fold triangle `FAB` is partitioned into these four regions:

| Region | Geometry | Tile count | Short directions |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `F E D C` | parallelogram of sides `bc,dc` and included angle alpha | `2cd` | height 0 |
| `C D Q` | b-fold tile | `b²` | height 1 |
| `E A R` | (c−b)-fold tile | `(c−b)²` | height 1 |
| `Q B R D` | 60-degree parallelogram of sides `ad,ab` | `2ad` | height 1 |

For the first parallelogram, take the vectors `U=(b,0)`, `V=(b,a)`.
Its sides are `cU,dV`; each elementary U,V cell has two original tiles
along its diagonal of length `a`. For the last parallelogram use width-a
strips and height-b cells in its rotated short-edge basis. Thus all four
regions have explicit positive tilings.

The coordinates show the partition directly: `Q` lies on `CB`, the
segment `CD` separates the first large triangle from the low
parallelogram, `ER` separates the other triangle from it, and the
remaining region is precisely `QBRD`. Alternatively, their oriented
areas and pairwise intersections can be checked by rational clipping.

The uniform c²-tiling of `FAB` can therefore be replaced, with unchanged
boundary and count, by `2cd` tiles at height 0 and

```
b²+(c−b)²+2ad = c²−2cd = c(3c−2a−2b)
```

tiles at height 1. No arbitrary-shape island removal is used.

### Turning the trade into a smaller trapezoid

Put

```
O=(bd,0), H=(bd,ab), J=(K,ab).
```

Remove the d-fold corner triangle `FOC` from the first parallelogram.
This removal is compatible with its explicit U,V grid: `FOC` has
vertices `0,dU,dV`, so the ordinary triangular subset of its unit grid
contains exactly d² tiles. The remaining quadrilateral `O E D C`
therefore has `2cd−d²` tiles.

Attach the two additional triangles `C B H` and `B A J`. They are
respectively `(b−d)`-fold and `a`-fold copies of the tile. Together with
the other three regions in the preceding table, the six regions form
the trapezoid `O A J H`. Its leg is `ab` and its shorter base is
`K−bd`. Translation by `(-bd,0)` gives the asserted standard trapezoid.

Every piece count is nonnegative, because `0<d<b<c`. The total is

```
(2cd−d²) + b² + (c−b)² + 2ad + (b−d)² + a²
 = K+c²−2bd.
```

This proves the first construction. Reflection/interchanging the two
short sides gives the second.

For `x=s+ra+tb`, attach the corresponding short-grid parallelogram of
width `ra+tb` and height `ab`. For leg `nab`, stack n bands; the
successive shorter bases are `x,x+ab,...,x+(n−1)ab`, all having the same
semigroup property. This proves the final assertion. □

## The (3,5,7) one-leg family is positive by formula

Here `d=1`, `K=34`, `L=15`; the two seeds are `29` and `31`.

```
(29+<3,5>) union (31+<3,5>)
 = {29,31,32} union {x integers : x>=34}.
```

Thus all positive cases in Harries' one-leg `L=15` classification have
a single symbolic construction from these two seeds. The 73-tile seed
has high-height blocks of counts `25,4,6` and low-height blocks of
counts `13,16,9`: it contains the same 35-tile pentagon geometry found
in the source search certificate. This does not replace the separate
negative proof for smaller excluded bases.

## A general shaving parameter, and a counterexample to two-seed necessity

The same geometry works with any integer `t` in place of `d`, provided

```
1 <= t < b,
r_t = b(c−b)−ct belongs to <a,b>.
```

In this version the high parallelogram has width `r_t`, rather than `ad`.
Its count is `2r_t`; the other five counts are
`2ct−t², b², (c−b)², (b−t)², a²`. The resulting seed is

```
T(K−bt,ab), with K+c²−2bt tiles.
```

The special choice `t=d` works for every tile because `r_d=ad`.
Larger admissible t can give strictly smaller seeds.

For example, take `(a,b,c)=(32,45,67)`, with `K=3049` and `d=10`.
The two universal deficit seeds are `2599` and `2729`. But `t=12` gives

```
r_12 = 45*22−67*12 = 186 = 3*32+2*45,
K−45*12 = 2509.
```

Thus `T(2509,1440)` is tiled. Since `2509<2599`, its base is absent from
both `2599+<32,45>` and `2729+<32,45>`. This is an explicit
counterexample to the proposed universal exact spectrum consisting only
of the two deficit seeds plus their semigroups. The macroregion counts
are `1464,2025,484,372,1089,1024`, totaling 6458.

## A clean improved equilateral tail

Let `A=max(a,b)`, `B=min(a,b)`. Every equilateral multiplier

```
m >= 3 ceil(c/B)
```

is constructible by the usual cyclic three-trapezoid assembly. For the
deficit seed `s=A²+B²−Ad`, write `c=qB+r`, where `0<r<B` by coprimality.
The canonical semigroup representation is

```
kab−s = A(B−r) + B[A(k−q)−B].
```

It is nonnegative if and only if `k>=q+1=ceil(c/B)`: reducing any
representation modulo B forces its coefficient of A to be congruent to
`B−r`; increasing that coefficient only decreases the other coefficient.
Take cyclic parameters `k0,k0,m−2k0`, where `k0=ceil(c/B)`, and stack
bands to the required leg lengths.

The general t-family can sometimes improve this further, so this clean
tail is itself not asserted to be optimal. For instance, `(65,88,133)`
admits `t=25`, which permits a trapezoid at short base `2ab` even though
`ceil(c/min(a,b))=3`.

## Scaled reflected-corner theorem

The four-region construction also proves a useful scaled statement.
Keep a positive integer t with `0<t<b`, set
`r=b(c−b)−ct`, and let m be positive. If

```
mr belongs to <a,b>,
```

then an `mc`-fold triangle admits removal of the `mt`-fold reflected
triangle at its alpha corner, with the remaining quadrilateral tiled
by original unit tiles. In the coordinates above the remaining target
is `m(O A B C)`, where `O=(bt,0)` and `C=(bt,at)`.

The high triangles now have integer scales `mb` and `m(c−b)`. The
low parallelogram consists of an `mc` by `mt` grid of the b,c cells;
its corner subtraction removes `(mt)²` unit tiles. The high
parallelogram has sides `mr` and `mab`, so it is tiled by the semigroup
representation of mr. The four respective counts are

```
m²(2ct−t²), m²b², m²(c−b)², 2m²r,
```

and sum to `m²(c²−t²)` as required. This argument requires membership
of mr; requiring membership of r would be an unnecessarily stronger
condition.

For a reflected beta corner, interchange the short sides. In particular,
if `a>b` and `t=a−b`, the required remainder is `r=bc−a²`. It is never
itself in `<a,b>`: if `a=qb+r0` with `0<r0<b`, the canonical
coefficient of a is `(q+1)b−a`, leaving coefficient
`c−(q+1)a<0` for b. Nevertheless, its multiples can belong to the
semigroup and the scaled construction can work.

For `(a,b,c)=(8,7,13)`, r=27, and

```
2r=2*7+5*8,
3r=7*7+4*8.
```

The corresponding reflected-corner quadrilaterals therefore have
tilings with 672 and 1512 unit tiles. Their exact macrocoordinates and
checks are in `certificates/beta_corner_8_7_13_m2.json` and
`certificates/beta_corner_8_7_13_m3.json`. This supplies the
geometric ingredient for the balanced-ratio transfer proved in the
[reversed-F4 companion](BALANCED_F4.md); it does not assert a classification
of all reflected-corner quadrilaterals.

**Balanced-corner corollary.** If `b<a<=4b/3`, then the reflected beta
corner with `t=a−b` can be removed at every multiplier `m>=2`. Indeed,
with `r=bc−a²`,

```
2r=(3b−2a)a+(2c−3a)b,
3r=(4b−3a)a+(3c−4a)b.
```

All coefficients are nonnegative. The first coefficients follow from
the ratio bound. Also `4c²−9a²=−5a²+4ab+4b²` is decreasing over this
interval and is at least `4b²/9>0`, so `2c>3a`; this also implies
`3c>4a`. Every integer m at least two is a nonnegative combination of
two and three, hence mr belongs to `<a,b>`. This corollary settles all
those positive scales, while making no assertion that another
construction cannot work at scale one.

## Application and limitation

The theorem improves the sufficient trapezoid seeds for every
primitive 120-degree tile. In a three-trapezoid equilateral assembly,
one can test `kab−(K−bd)` or `kab−(K−ad)` for membership in `<a,b>`,
instead of using only `kab−K`. A statement of a sharp universal
equilateral cutoff would additionally require necessity; it does not
follow from this construction.

The local c²-triangle move reduces the height-one inventory, but its
remaining triangular blocks have scales `b` and `c−b`, both less than
c. Therefore repeated application to those blocks alone stops
immediately. An arbitrary maximal component need not contain any
c-fold triangular subpatch in the first place. These two geometric
facts are the precise gap between this positive surgery and a
normal-form theorem for all tilings.

The accompanying exact verifier checks the six macroregions, their
side lengths, containment, disjointness and total area, and expands the
(3,5,7) seed into congruent tiles. Its finite checks support, rather
than replace, the algebraic construction above.
