# The next gate length after unit-charge saturation

Let an exterior-free branch of a complete tiling have signed parity populations $E,O$, with $|E-O|=c$. If its gate has $c+a-b+2$ unmatched owners, its total far population is at most **2923 at 3431** or **2451 at 5063**. This permits arbitrary heights, reflected tiles and T-junctions throughout the branch. In particular a unit-charge branch carrying at least half of a 5063 board needs **at least 69 whole unmatched owners** across its own gate. On the 3431 board a unit-charge branch with more than 2923 tiles needs at least 67. These conclusions use the [minimum-length bounds](SATURATION.md) as well and apply without fixing the global unmatched total.

## Cutting at the unique reversed step

The circuit theorem gives one positive Jordan circuit: a second neutral circuit already costs ten or twelve owners, and additional nonzero charges cost still more. Orient the local chart so that the alternating character is positive, and label the six saturation vectors by $v_i$, using low at even headings and high at odd headings as in [SATURATION.md](SATURATION.md). Exactly one of the $m$ letters has the opposite character because their sum is $m-2$. Write it as $-v_j$. Rotate cyclically so this step is last. Rotation by a multiple of two headings leaves just $j=0,1$ to test.

The preceding $m-1$ vectors all belong to the ordinary six-letter alphabet. Their displacement must be $v_j$. Their product in each boundary group must equal the product of that single missing positive letter $v_j$. Closing by its opposite adds zero to the doubled axial area. This gives a word ending in a specified cyclic factor, with an exact target power. Deleting the opposite step without that endpoint constraint would lose part of the physical condition.

Fix the number of low letters in the prefix. Enumerate their three counts; their sum determines their displacement. The three high counts are determined by their sum and the remaining two displacement coordinates. For each cyclic order, subtract the target letter before checking the three abelian residues. This yields all 54 prefixes *as inventories* at 3431 and all 24 at 5063. Their orders are bounded by the factor parser. A product in the target factor with the target abelian residues has exactly the required value in that factor.

At 3431 the [original triangle profile](ALL_CUTS.md) already gives $n<2948$ at perimeter $65\cdot37$. The population is an odd multiple of 37. At 5063 the scalar parsing bounds leave a few profiles able to carry $59c$ tiles. For a recorded finite action of the other boundary group, the full prefix has to act as the target letter. Its slope is fixed by the inventory. Take the maximum at the **translation coefficient of that letter**, which may be nonzero. The test for the mirror pair transfers by the word symmetry proved in [SATURATION.md](SATURATION.md); after even rotation to normalize the exceptional axis its index map is $i\mapsto 1-i\pmod6$. Every resulting bound is below $59c$ tiles. The largest remaining odd multiple is $57c=2451$.

## When both signed parities carry charge

At 5063 a stronger length floor holds even for small enclosed populations. If $|E-O|=43$ and **both $E$ and $O$ are nonzero**, the gate costs at least **73 owners**. On the 3431 board, $|E-O|=37$ and $|E+O|\ge5\cdot37$ require at least **67**.

Here is an independent displacement argument. Normalize the two bank sums in their respective unit meshes to $x,y\in\mathbb Z[\rho]$. The cut closes in the plane, so $Dx+cy=0$ and $|x|=|y|$. If $x$ were invertible modulo the prime $c$, the equation would imply $D=0\pmod c$, contrary to its coordinates. Thus $c$ divides the norm squared of $x$ (otherwise the conjugate divided by its norm is a modular inverse). This also holds for $y$. A bank with heading character $s$ has displacement coordinates $(u,v)$ satisfying $u-v\equiv s\pmod3$, checked on each of the six unit headings. If $3$ does not divide $s$, the common squared norm is therefore at least $c$.

For $k$ steps and absolute character $s$, their two character classes have counts $(k+s)/2$ and $(k-s)/2$. Any vectors of opposite character have dot product at most $1/2$ in their bank's unit mesh. Expanding the squared norm gives the upper bound $(3k^2+s^2)/4$.

Both parity charges are multiples of $c$. If their difference has magnitude $c$ and both are nonzero, their sum has magnitude at least $3c$ and its quotient by $c$ is odd. At 5063, magnitude at least $5c$ already costs at least $5(c-a+b)=105$ by the ordinary character. At magnitude $3c$ the absolute character sum and difference on the two banks give individual magnitudes $64,1$ in some order, from $c+a-b=65$ and $3(c-a+b)=63$. A bank of character magnitude one with at most seven owners has squared norm at most 37, strictly below 43. It costs at least nine by parity, giving $64+9$. At 3431 a sum of magnitude $5c$ gives bank magnitudes $59,4$. Six owners in the smaller bank give squared norm at most 31, below 37. Eight are required by parity. A sum of magnitude at least $7c$ already costs at least 77. This argument uses only a physical cut and its two signed characters.
