# A tree of joint cells supported by the actual tiling

Use the standard vertex-phase frame, $R=\mathbb Z[\rho]$, $\rho=e^{i\pi/3}$ and $z=(a+b\rho)/c$. Write $K_h(p)=(h,p+z^hR)$. A tile has one short height $h$ and one long height $h+s$, $s=\pm1$. The two shorter sides and all three vertices belong to its short cell. A side at physical height $H$ has an edge cell $K_H(p)$ determined by either endpoint. On each connected collinear run all side endpoints are separated by integer physical offsets. In particular the two banks of every positive-length contact have the same physical edge cell, also at a T-junction. These are the [joint-cell and signed-current inputs](../../../2026-11-20/semiprimes/continuation/common-obstruction/joint/JOINT_CELLS.md).

## Why a tree is available

Form the undirected graph of joint cells by joining cells at consecutive heights if their translates intersect. The sequence of vertices $K_h(p)$, over all integer $h$, is a simple path for each fixed point $p$. The joint-cell graph itself can contain cycles. A tiling supplies a consistent lift to its tree of reduced walks, rooted at the axial exterior cell. In this tree, append an edge to a walk and cancel an immediate backtrack. Each projected neighbor of a vertex has exactly one adjacent lift at that vertex.

To construct the lift, use the disk formed by the actual tiling. In its dual, crossing a common side takes the short cell of the first tile to the common edge cell and then to the short cell of the second. Omit stationary steps. Subdivide sides at all tile vertices. A circuit around an interior subdivision vertex $p$ has trivial reduced walk. For each incident tile that has $p$ as a corner, its short and both incident edge cells are on the single path $K_h(p)$. A tile through whose side $p$ passes is entered and exited through that same side cell, an immediate backtrack through its short cell. Once these excursions are erased, the whole closed walk lies on the single path through $p$. This also proves that two neighboring markers of one physical side give the same lift when reached from the tile center. All dual loops in the disk are generated by the interior vertex circuits, with conjugations by connecting paths. Thus the lifted cell reached at a tile center or side marker is independent of the route.

An alternative disk construction makes the boundary case explicit. Map each tiling vertex $p$ to $K_0(p)$. Along a subdivided side $pq$ of height $H$, walk from $K_0(p)$ to $K_H(p)$ along the path through $p$, identify $K_H(p)=K_H(q)$, and walk to $K_0(q)$ along the path through $q$. Inserting a T-vertex adds inverse paths. At an original triangle corner the two incident edge cells and the short cell lie on that corner's path. The boundary of a triangle therefore reduces to excursions from its short cell to each edge cell and back. Fill these null loops in the graph and lift the resulting disk map. A center-to-side step is precisely the one in the dual construction. This argument allows any number of real contacts on a side.

Along a straight outer side its marker lift is constant. At a board corner the path from one exterior marker to the next reduces to the subpath of $K_h(p)$ between them. The two axial walls meet with the same height-zero lift $C$. At $X=A$ and $Y=B\rho$, $A=b(2a+b)$ and $B=a(a+2b)$, the slant connects the two corners. Its displacement is $Y-X=c^2\rho z^2$. Hence $K_i(X)=K_i(Y)$ for $i=0,1,2$. The exterior lifts form a path $C,B_1,T$ of heights $0,1,2$, with $T$ carrying the complete slant. In particular an off-path lift may project to an already used physical class. No short populations have been identified merely because their projections agree.

## One physical cut per edge

Delete an edge of the lifted tree. Let $S$ be either component, $U_S$ the union of the tiles whose short lift lies in it, and $\Gamma_S$ the sum of the CCW exterior sides whose marker lift lies in it. Let $P,Q$ denote the signed counts of tiles crossing the deleted edge from inside to outside and from outside to inside, respectively, by their short-to-long step. Write $A_e,B_e$ for the corresponding chains of whole CCW long sides. Then
$$
\partial U_S=\Gamma_S+A_e-B_e.
$$
Indeed short sides have the tile's own lift as their edge lift. At every generic contact point the two banks have the same edge lift and opposite orientations. Subtracting this cancellation from the boundary of the selected tiles leaves precisely the two indicated long-side banks. A physical half-turn pair uses the same edge and short lift, since its route across the common long side projects to a step and its inverse. Its two signs and its oriented long sides cancel. The identity holds before any subdivision of its displayed long edges.

Root the tree at $C$ and take $S$ beyond a directed tree edge. Its exterior term is empty or the complete slant. Both endpoints of every crossing long edge belong to *both* of the two physical joint classes represented by the incident lifts. This statement concerns points, independently of which triangles own them. Normalize the lower physical height to zero. The intersection of the two translates is a translate of $R\cap zR$, which has axial index $c$ in $R$. Indeed $w\in R$ belongs to $zR$ iff $(a+b\bar\rho)w=0\pmod{cR}$. Multiplication by $a+b\bar\rho$ modulo the prime $c$ has rank one, by its norm $c^2$ and its nonzero coordinates. When the slant is present the cut edge lies on $C,B_1,T$; both slant endpoints belong to its same intersection translate. The displayed chain is endpoint-balanced. The shoelace sum on every closed walk in the intersection translate is a multiple of $c$, by translating its start to zero. The sum over all walks is $ab\,\#U_S$. Since $\gcd(ab,c)=1$, this proves $c\mid\#U_S$. This proof applies when the cut has no area and when its support has holes or several components.

Subtract direct-child subtree populations from their parent's subtree population. Each lifted short-node population is divisible by $c$ except at $C$, where it is congruent to $N$. At a fixed lift $v$, the usual oriented short-edge character on each physical supporting line gives $(a-b)G_v$ after summation; complementary long sides have length $c$ and cancel or contribute multiples of $c$. The slant contributes a multiple of $c$. The axial sides contribute the original exceptional residue at $C$. The localization is legitimate because every entire physical edge has a single marker lift. Thus $G_v\equiv0\pmod c$ except at $C$, where $G_C\equiv-(a-b)\pmod c$. Since $c$ is odd, both sign counts in an ordinary lift are separately divisible by $c$. The [one-block line-incidence exclusion](../../../2026-11-20/semiprimes/LINE_COUNTS.md) applies to the same line equations for an ordinary lift of population $c$, whose signs would all agree. This rules out that population. A nonempty ordinary lift needs at least $2c$ tiles, and an ordinary lift of charge $\pm c$ needs at least $3c$. Summation over the lifts of any given Euclidean cell proves the same congruences for that cell. The one-block exclusion also rules out a monochromatic cell of size $c$ after summation.

## Contact and tile budgets

Let $k$ be the physical height of the endpoint inside $S$ at a cut, and let $E,O$ be the signed even- and odd-height tile populations in $S$. Put $d=a-b$ and $b_S=C(\Gamma_S)$ for the ordinary exterior character. Its height-alternating value agrees because all exterior heights are even. The two [tile characters](../../../2026-11-20/semiprimes/continuation/charge-transport/flux/SIGNED_CUT_TRANSPORT.md) give
$$
c(P-Q)=(c-d)(E+O)+b_S,\qquad
c(-1)^k(P+Q)=(c+d)(E-O)-b_S .
$$
The physical height $k$ may increase or decrease along a rooted tree edge. These signs follow from the CCW long edge having ordinary character $-c\epsilon$ for both hands. If $m_e$ is the number of unmatched owners on the edge then $m_e\ge |P|+|Q|$ and $M=\sum_e m_e$ for the whole board. Each unmatched tile has a unique short-to-long edge in this one tree.

An occupied branch cut off from all exterior markers satisfies
$$
c^2m_e^2\ \ge\ \pi\sqrt3\,ab\,\#U_S .
$$
Its perimeter is at most $cm_e$ by the physical cut identity; use the planar isoperimetric inequality. A nonempty such branch contains at least $2c$ tiles. If its root is empty, the only bank in the boundary is the opposite bank of whole long edges of one physical height. Each endpoint-connected tour then lies in one translate of the triangular mesh of step $c$. Positive total area forces a tour with nonzero area and hence a simple supported mesh cycle: unit edges of this triangular mesh do not cross each other in their interiors. This contradicts the [pure-mesh cycle exclusion](../../../2026-11-20/semiprimes/continuation/vertex-phase/pure_cycles/NO_REMOTE_GAPS.md). Every occupied branch away from the exterior path consequently has an occupied root, even if some other lift of its physical cell is occupied elsewhere.

With $j=N\bmod c>0$, let $R_*$ count **all** occupied ordinary lifts. The core costs at least $j$. Total odd sign is $-c$, so at least one odd lift has odd block count and needs at least $3c$. Thus $j+2cR_*+c\le N$, giving $R_*\le45,58$ respectively. Writing $G_v=cg_v$ on ordinary lifts gives
$$
N\ge n_C+\sum_{v\ne C,\ n_v>0} c\max(2,|g_v|+2\,\mathbf1_{|g_v|=1}).
$$
This charge and tile bound and the cut inequalities use the same occupied vertices and actual gate owners. They can constrain a common physical population without adding overlapping tile budgets.

## Unit-charge terminal cost at arbitrary heights and odd populations

Take an occupied vertex $v$ off the exterior path, with no occupied descendant. For every outward edge at $v$ the child side has no short tiles and no exterior markers. Its cut identity says that the sum of the CCW long sides from $v$ on that bank is the zero physical chain. All these tiles have the same short height, hand and long-edge length. On each oriented supporting line, write the bank as intervals of length $c$. If their signed sum is zero, the signed multiplicity of interval starts is a finite measure equal to its translate by $c$, hence is zero. Consequently each whole interval occurs with both orientations. The two owners have fixed hand and opposite long directions, so they are actual half-turn mates. Disjoint interiors prevent extra copies on one bank. This proof also works for outward edges of the same physical height change as the entering edge, and when other lifts project to the same Euclidean cell.

It follows that every unmatched tile of $v$ uses the one joint gate toward its parent. Other tiles of $v$ are genuine pairs. Its short-side endpoints satisfy the period-$c$ identities localized to this lift, with zero exterior contribution. These are exactly the single-gate hypotheses of the [isolated-gate duals](../../../2026-11-21/semiprimes/charged-branches/ISOLATED_GATE_TERMINAL_COST.md). Translate to gate residue zero and, if necessary, conjugate to exchange the two hands; a half-turn exchanges charge signs. Their integer column inequalities give
$$
n_v\ge\tfrac{91}{22}|G_v|\quad(3431),\qquad
n_v\ge\tfrac{83}{13}|G_v|\quad(5063).
$$
Together with $n_v/c\equiv G_v/c\pmod2$, these yield **185 and 301 actual tiles**, respectively, for a terminal lift of unit charge. At 5063 charge magnitude $2c$ costs at least **602**. The previous geometric transfer was limited to single-access remote even classes in a special odd-population regime. The lifted-tree argument gives single access for every exterior-free terminal lift. It does not identify an arbitrary terminal node of only one prime tree with one lifted vertex. These costs can replace the corresponding terms in the global population reserve above.
