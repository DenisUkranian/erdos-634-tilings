# Physical cancellation determines the entire native interface

For either target F2 board, if the bank owned by the middle lift B on either native cut cancels as an oriented chain, the slant-side tiles fill **the canonical triangle in its exact position**. After removal of half-turn pairs, the opposite bank has exactly 40 whole long edges at 3431 or 48 at 5063. In the reflected path frame the seam runs from the left slant endpoint along the middle direction E for a steps and then along S for b steps. Each step has physical length c. Every one of those owners belongs to the same even lift, C for the axial cut and T for the slant cut.

This upgrades the [population conclusion](ROOTED.md) from area to an interface with prescribed tile directions. Earlier work [mentions canonicality for pure paths](../../../2026-11-20/semiprimes/continuation/global-endpoints/V1_CLOSURE_GAP.md). We give a replayable two-modulus proof and apply it to the individual banks of the positioned tree. Both original counts remain **OPEN**.

## From a cancelling bank to a single path

Use the [positioned cut-chain identity](../joint-cell-tree/TREE.md) on the side containing the slant. Its boundary is the slant chord plus one bank of middle-height whole long sides after the B bank cancels. Opposite whole intervals on the remaining bank have fixed hand and short height and are actual half-turn pairs. Equal directed copies would have overlapping interiors. Remove the pairs. The endpoint incidence of the remaining bank has just the two slant endpoints as source and sink. Following directed edges and removing closed walks leaves a simple path between them. Every closed walk so removed would use distinct whole edges in one translate of the middle mesh. Those edges form an embedded mesh graph, so the walk would contain a supported simple cycle, excluded on the target boards by the [pure-cycle theorem](../../../2026-11-20/semiprimes/continuation/vertex-phase/pure_cycles/NO_REMOTE_GAPS.md). Thus all unmatched edges of this bank form the single path. This derivation uses whole-edge chains, which already cancel subdivided physical contacts. It assumes no missing global height.

Use middle mesh units in the reflected path frame, writing a point as x+yρ after dividing by cz. The board vertices are O=(0,0), D=(a+b,−b) and H=a(a+2b)(a,b)/c². The candidate path runs from O to D. Its steps lie in the integer unit triangular mesh, with E=(1,0) and S=(1,−1). The open chord OD contains no mesh vertex because gcd(a+b,b)=1. The line is supporting and is not a mesh direction. The path and chord therefore bound a tiled disk, even when the path visits another outer wall. The canonical path EᵃSᵇ with that chord also bounds a tiled disk: it is the c-fold enlargement of the tile, filled by the ordinary subdivision into c² copies. Hence these two paths have the same boundary word at each modulus below.

## Recovering individual edges from two word projections

Use three independent cyclic generators of order m in their free product, indexed by the three axes, with opposite headings inverse. For m=a label a directed physical unit step at height h and heading j by the generator of axis-heading j+h with exponent sʰ, where s=b/c modulo a. For m=b use heading j with exponent tʰ, where t=a/c modulo b. Both multipliers are units of square one by the tile norm. In either hand the tile boundary is trivial: the side of length m vanishes, and the other short side has the same word as the long side directed along the vector sum of the shorts. Subdivision into physical units and disk cancellation justify the word even at T-junctions as in the pure-cycle theorem. On the fixed middle mesh we may normalize the headings and invert the common unit exponent, so whole steps have their ordinary generator labels.

For modulus a repeatedly erase adjacent inverses or a identical horizontal steps. Each erasure shifts the subsequent lift in the plane by a multiple of aE. In the remaining word no run of a identical nonhorizontal letters is possible. Its original endpoints would be board vertices differing by a multiple of a in both coordinates with nonzero vertical component. The [integer mesh check](seam_cancellation.py) finds only the differences 0 and ±aE. The maximal runs remaining therefore give a free-product normal form, with one sign per run and nonzero exponent modulo a. Since the canonical word is Sᵇ modulo a, only one S-axis run remains. Its vertical displacement fixes it to Sᵇ. On the horizontal cylinder of period a, erasing inverse pairs or full horizontal circles preserves the signed chain of nonhorizontal edges. Accordingly this chain agrees there with the projection of the canonical path.

Index the two nonhorizontal edge types by their upward vector (0,1) or (−1,1), lower row y and lower abscissa x. Simplicity bounds the signed multiplicity by one and precludes an edge traversed in both directions. If only one edge of this type and row has a given x modulo a, its coefficient is fixed. In any other residue the path can use only edges whose residue is shared or occurs in the canonical chain. The finite rational board gives the following bounds. An ambiguous row means that the allowed support on some heading extends beyond the canonical support.

| Tile | Ambiguous lower rows | Maximum span of x in any one row and heading | a+b−1 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| (33,7,37) | 0 | 33 | 39 |
| (35,13,43) | −2,−1,0,1 | 41 | 47 |
| (5,3,7), control | none | 4 | 7 |

Outside the indicated layers the nonhorizontal edges are exactly canonical. Indeed if the only allowed edge is canonical its signed coefficient must equal the projected coefficient. Below the lowest ambiguous row L the path therefore ends with the canonical diagonal tail from row L to D. Each separating row interface there is crossed exactly once and consecutive crossings share their prescribed endpoint, leaving no horizontal detour on a simple path. Above the highest ambiguous layer there are no crossings. The prefix before this tail stays in a strip of vertical span one or four respectively. In the control the first projection already suffices.

Cancel the common suffix in the word of modulus b. The prefix has the same word as Eᵃ followed by the canonical diagonal down to row L. Erase inverses and b identical horizontal letters. A remaining run of b identical nonhorizontal letters would span b rows in the original prefix, impossible in this strip. Its reduced syllables thus agree with the canonical prefix modulo b. The horizontal syllable may differ by a multiple of b, and the S syllable, when present, has its exact exponent fixed by vertical displacement. The nonhorizontal oriented chain agrees with the canonical prefix modulo horizontal translation by b as well. Reattach the common tail.

For each heading and row take the Laurent polynomial whose coefficient at x is the signed edge discrepancy. The two cylinder projections give divisibility by both Xᵃ−1 and Xᵇ−1. Their least common multiple has degree a+b−1 since gcd(a,b)=1. After translating the least exponent to zero, the span bound in the table forces the polynomial to vanish. Independently the checker verifies that the incidence graph linking each allowable x modulo a to x modulo b is a forest: deleting leaves successively forces every discrepancy coefficient to zero. Thus all the nonhorizontal edges are the canonical diagonal, with their orientations. The remaining portion must be the unique simple horizontal segment from O to (a,0). This proves the asserted order and position, including for paths longer than the initially known shortest-path threshold.

By the cut identity the slant-side union has exactly this oriented triangle boundary. Winding number off the tile skeleton gives its indicator function, so its tiles fill the canonical cap. The complement is the quadrilateral with vertices O,(a,0),D,H and has N−c² tiles. In particular a tiling with even one cancelling middle bank would imply a tiling of that specific quadrilateral. Conversely any tiling of the quadrilateral by the given tile extends by the standard cap, though that extension need not have a cancelling middle bank. At the even lift the surviving owners on the two straight runs contribute a and b tiles of opposite signs. The signed bank is −d at C or +d at T, with d=a−b, by the native cut equations.

## A constraint even when neither bank cancels

For each native gate let q be its signed B current and k its number of unmatched B owners. One always has k≥|q| and k≡q modulo two. If q is not divisible by three, **k≥7** on each target. This gives at least eight for an even such q. The two native costs are disjoint at B. If the canonical interface is absent and q=0, that bank costs at least two unmatched B owners, one of each sign.

Indeed the slant chord has integral displacement in the middle mesh, as does the opposite bank. Consequently the total displacement of the unmatched B bank belongs to that mesh. In its own edge units it is an axial integer w whose rotation by z or its conjugate is integral. Conjugation preserves step counts, so use (a+bρ)w=0 modulo c. Its least nonzero step distance is seven at both target moduli, as checked over all sums of up to seven unit directions. If the displacement vanishes, the three signed multiplicities along headings 0,2,4 are equal since those three units sum to zero and span the plane. Their sum is the heading character, equal up to a fixed sign to q on a fixed gate. This proves the divisibility assertion. It leaves physical cancellation strictly stronger than zero displacement.
