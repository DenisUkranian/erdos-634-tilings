# Native contacts with two unmatched middle owners

Use the reflected native chart and both two-owner hypotheses of the [native interface theorem](../TWO_OWNER.md). Each exceptional middle step is opposite to the other on its cut. The pure steps belong to the opposite bank. Counts below are necessary conditions on arbitrary complete tilings subject to these hypotheses. Retained words need not be realizable by an interior tiling.

## A length-independent restriction at 3431

Suppose both exceptional steps of each cut lie on the simple path from O to D. On the axial and slant cut let s=+1 and −1 respectively and let j be the heading of the first exceptional step as the path moves from O to D. If j+s is nonzero modulo three on **both** cuts, a complete tiling is impossible. The path constraints leave two pairs, each forcing 148 middle tiles on two adjacent lifts of 74 tiles each. Both residual regions fail the section test below. The words are in [UNBOUNDED.json](UNBOUNDED.json). The conclusion has no upper bound on the number of steps. The other headings and the case of a separate exceptional circuit are outside this enumeration.

To check finiteness, multiply all mesh coordinates by c. Each pure step is c times a unit. The first bridge is v=(a+bρ)ρʲ or its conjugate factor times ρʲ; the second is −v. The path visits only cR and v+cR, with R the axial lattice. Enumerate the points in both cosets inside the physical board and the undirected unit edges they support. The bounding box in the verifier allows the unit-length bridge shift on both ends. Number the pure edges and record their mod-two endpoint incidence.

Append rows for the endpoint incidence on each axis separately modulo a and modulo b in both coordinates. A pure edge of heading k contributes its endpoints on axis k modulo three. A bridge contributes on axis j+s modulo a and axis j modulo b, by reducing its two ordered short steps. Traversing the corresponding power of a generator gives exactly the endpoint difference by telescoping. Erasing a whole period or an inverse pair leaves these currents unchanged. Multiplication by c in the coordinates preserves the row comparisons since c is invertible in both residue rings. Hence the entire vector equals that of the canonical path by the [two boundary words](../../charge-variation/CANONICAL_SEAM.md). Physical subdivision at T-junctions preserves endpoint currents. Separate pure circuits are excluded by the supported-cycle theorem.

Reduce the two bridge columns modulo the binary span of all pure edge columns. They must have identical remainders, since the canonical vector already belongs to that span. For every pair with equal remainders obtain one support by adding the canonical mask and the two reduction masks. Add all elements of the explicitly computed binary kernel. Its dimension is at most eight for these headings. A simple path uses each pure edge at most once, so its mod-two support determines the available edges exactly. Trace each resulting graph, checking the endpoint path, both oriented bridges, absence of extra edges, both reduced words and the signed area. Place the actual owner triangles from their endpoints and hands. Verify physical containment, disjoint interiors, side membership and compatibility of the two cuts by winding numbers. The [verifier](unbounded.py) finds four pairs with area difference at least 74 tiles. The differences are 74,74,148,148. The one-lift moment and width test in [middle_pairs.py](../middle_pairs.py) excludes a sole B lift in all four. Every other occupied lift costs at least 74, B is occupied by its unmatched owners, and an occupied exterior-free descendant has occupied ancestors. This gives the two residual regions.

## The 148-tile residuals fail exact line sections

Remove the four prescribed unmatched middle owners from the 148 tiles. In each cut chain this replaces its two exceptional steps by the ordered short sides. Subtract the slant chain from the axial one with the signs of the middle region. Cancelling oriented unit steps gives one boundary of seven or eight corners with no repeated vertex. In middle short-mesh coordinates its doubled axial area is exactly 144ab. This calculation determines the region occupied by the remaining tiles: the oriented boundary determines its interior indicator by winding number. The [section record](SECTIONS.json) gives both polygons in full.

Seventy remaining tiles have short lift B; seventy-four have the unique occupied neighboring lift. Each is either in an actual half-turn pair or is a single whose long edge connects the two lifts. Indeed the four native singles have been removed, and an empty exterior-free side has zero boundary chain. On a fixed line a zero sum of oriented equal-length intervals forces opposite coincident copies by starting at the leftmost endpoint. Those copies are actual pairs at the fixed gate. This justifies the restriction on singles without banning T-junctions.

Write all coordinates in units of 1/37 of the middle short mesh. B has lattice cR with O as origin. The other short lattice has the form (ct,0)+mR for m=a+bρ or a+b−bρ, with 0≤t<c. To verify completeness, every adjacent translate meets cR. In unscaled coordinates the intersection of R and z or conjugate(z) times R has index c in R and is the kernel of a nonzero form x+wy modulo c; here w is 27 or 11. The horizontal unit maps to one, hence all cosets have these representatives. The replay even allows the representatives projecting to a native neighbor.

For a tile with obtuse vertex q and unit mesh vector m, the other vertices are u=q−amρʲ and v=q+bmρ^(j+h). A pair has fourth vertex u+v−q, with the mate heading shifted by three. Retain every pair whose four corners lie in the closed residual polygon. Retain every single with all corners inside and its long edge in the opposite lift: its hand must point toward that mesh and its endpoints belong to both lattices. The verifier tests the long-endpoint congruence directly. Candidate shapes can extend across a concavity and their interiors can overlap. Allowing them preserves every true tiling. All mesh origins for q are enumerated by transforming the polygon vertices into mesh coordinates; linear coordinate extrema occur at polygon corners.

Intersect the residual polygon with any of the eleven specified lines. In the indicated rotation of its coordinates each line has equation 2x=t with an odd integer t. Every shape vertex has integral coordinates, so the line misses all vertices. Each candidate that meets the line contributes one closed interval of positive length. If a tiling exists, its shapes partition each polygon section into intervals from its lower endpoint to its upper endpoint. Represent each interval by a directed edge from its smaller to its larger transverse coordinate. A complete chain must connect the section endpoints. A shape can be discarded if its edge belongs to no such chain on this line; intersect the lower forward-reachable set with upper backward reachability at the respective ends of the edge. Nothing is assumed about shapes missed by the line. Applying the test successively is valid by induction: all the shapes in any actual partition would survive every preceding test.

The [integer and rational replay](sections.py) excludes all 74 choices of neighbor lattice for **each** residual. Every rejection exhibits a line and a connected section with disconnected endpoints in the remaining interval graph. The section positions are fixed positive or negative half-integers in the scaled frame. In the first residual 70 choices already fail the first line, and all four others also fail. On both complete 143 tilings a separate [control](section_control.py) groups all actual whole pairs and singles, verifies directly that their intervals concatenate exactly, and independently checks that the pruning code keeps every true shape. It samples each gap between successive projected vertex coordinates in all three mesh directions (198 and 181 sections respectively). The numerical target conclusions require the native two-owner hypotheses, path attachment and heading restriction in the first paragraph.

## Opposite banks within two steps of minimum

Now allow any heading. On each cut suppose the opposite bank has at most a+b+2 steps. Abelianizing the two boundary words and cancelling the two opposite exceptional contributions fixes the signed pure counts on each axis modulo both a and b. Their discrepancy from the canonical count is less than ab, so equality holds as integers. Thus the bank consists of the a east and b southeast steps, with at most one additional inverse pair on an arbitrary axis.

A decomposition into a chord path and supported circuits could put the two exceptional steps together on a detached circuit. It cannot put only one there because the shift changes coset. In the detached case the horizontal displacement of the pure chord path already costs at least a+b pure steps. The circuit therefore has at most two pure steps. The exceptional pair by itself has zero area. With two pure steps it is a parallelogram with opposite unit steps, enclosing 2cL/(ab) tiles for L one of a,b,a+b. This follows by taking the determinant of the unit step with v/c in the three mesh directions. None of these counts is integral on either board. Thus both exceptional steps belong to the chord path and the enumeration covers the cap, including boundaries that pinch before decomposition.

For each position, prune a prefix only when a remaining axis syllable cannot be completed with the available pure steps and exceptional powers in the two boundary groups. The negative pure letters beyond the canonical counts are additional corrective letters in that suffix bound. Check the exact completed words, area and the positioned owner tests just described. The [replay](near.py) counts the resulting pairs by axial/slant excess and middle population. Each entry below is in blocks of c. A one in the column label means two extra pure steps on that bank.
