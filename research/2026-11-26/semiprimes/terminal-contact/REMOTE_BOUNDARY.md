# A contact reserve for every occupied exterior-free branch

Both original counts remain open. Work on the [positioned tree](../joint-cell-tree/TREE.md) of a complete tiling of either target. Root outward from its two-edge exterior path. Count actual whole-side half-turn mates before measuring unmatched owners. Let Z count occupied exterior-free branches with zero total signed population at each parity. Count branches by their entering edges, including nested branches. Let I count those among Z with an occupied strict descendant. Let M be the total number of unmatched tiles and V the sum of absolute odd short charges in blocks of c. Then

    M ≥ 16 Z + 2 I + max(A, (c+d)V),      d=a−b.

Here A=107 or 149 in board order. If any short lift off the exterior path has nonzero signed charge, A can be replaced by **149 or 171**. These estimates apply to arbitrary complete tilings with split contacts, either hand and any number of heights. The proof counts separate whole owners even at adjacent tree edges. Numerical caps on lifted populations and conditional two-height estimates need not be added to this inequality.

Every occupied exterior-free branch, charged or neutral, pays at least 16 unmatched owners on its entering edge. At 5063 equality requires a terminal branch of exactly 86 tiles and one of two boundary words. At 3431 equality permits 74 tiles, or 148 tiles split into two adjacent 74-tile lifts. In the latter case their common internal interface has at least **18** unmatched owners. In particular two consecutive exterior-free edges cannot both attain 16 if the outer one contains an occupied descendant. The [local control](patch5063.json) realizes one of the 5063 words within a partial packing.

## Small physical cuts without a terminal hypothesis

Use the standard physical frame and the [Jordan decomposition](../charged-contour/README.md). On a cut with at most 16 unmatched owners, every circuit has alternating charge zero since c+d>16. Its positive enclosed population n is a multiple of 2c and its length is even and at least ten or twelve respectively. Thus there is exactly one circuit on an occupied cut of this size. It encloses precisely the far tiles by the oriented physical boundary identity. Isoperimetry gives n ≤ (mc)²/(π√3 ab) for m owners. For m≤14 this is below 6c on both boards. For m=16 it is below 8c or 6c respectively. The lower rational estimates π>3.1 and √3>1.73 suffice.

Use the chart of the far root. Rotate and, if necessary, conjugate and orient the image CCW. A low step is a far owner with vector Dρʲ for D=a+b conjugate(ρ); a high step is the reversed near owner with vector cρʲ. Each bank has heading character zero: the two characters give equal bank characters up to the orientation convention and a nonzero one would use at least 2(c−d)>16 letters, as in the [terminal argument](TERMINAL.md). Each bank has even size. Its net displacement is zero as well. To check this last step for sixteen letters, let x,y be the two bank displacements in their own unit axes; then Dx+cy=0, and their norms agree. Reduction modulo the prime c gives c dividing the norm squared of x (an invertible x would force D=0 modulo c). Since its heading character vanishes, the axial coordinates u,v of x have u−v=0 modulo three: this holds on each unit heading with its character subtracted. Thus u²+uv+v² is divisible by three. A nonzero common norm squared is at least 3c, whereas the smaller bank has at most eight unit steps. This contradiction gives zero displacement. The three signed axis counts then vanish individually by combining displacement and character. Both banks occur, since a one-alphabet polygon is forbidden by the supported-cycle theorem.

The [word join](word_join.cpp) can now be used before knowing whether any descendant is occupied. Its directional torus test has a separate unrestricted justification. In the order-a boundary group a low step agrees with b copies of heading j−1, and in the order-b group it agrees with a copies of heading j. A high step agrees with c copies of heading j in both. These are the two [tile word identities](../charge-variation/CANONICAL_SEAM.md). Follow the word on the axial lattice modulo its order and record endpoint differences separately for each generator axis. Deleting a full generator period or an inverse pair preserves these three sums. The supported Jordan boundary is identity in each group, so all sums vanish. Replacing each low letter by lengths a,b in directions j,j−1 gives the same sums in both tests. This use of the torus requires no pairing of the tiles inside. The strip-width criterion for physical pairs is used only *after* establishing its population hypotheses.

Normalize a low step preceded by a high step to be the first letter, of heading zero. Join all two half-words of the required length, imposing signed axis counts, the two torus currents, and determinant sum abn. The join key is an integer fingerprint: collisions can only add candidates since all generated matching keys are examined. The [replay](remote_boundary.py) checks closure, area, simple vertices, both reduced group words and the exact endpoint data on the outputs. The results below count normalized words before dividing by the choice of first low step.

At n=2c the occupied root uses the entire population by the ordinary lift floor. It really is terminal. The 44 words at length 14 then all fail the established physical strip-width test. This proves the uniform 16 floor. At 5063 the two remaining words have bank splits (14,2) and (2,14). At 3431 every length-16 word of area 4c fails the terminal width test, so its branch must have two occupied adjacent lifts, each of population 2c by the no-empty-ancestor theorem. Both are balanced: a small boundary has zero ordinary and alternating characters and these lifts have opposite parity.

## Comparing consecutive physical interfaces at 3431

Suppose both the 4c outer branch and its 2c child have length 16. Up to cyclic choice of start and rotation there are 24 outer words. For the child the single-lift width test leaves two words. Keep track of both actual patterns of owners in the outer root chart.

* On the outer contour remove the far owners. This replaces each low step by its two short sides in order (a at j, b at j−1). The other steps stay.
* On the inner contour add the root's owners, which are near owners at this cut. In the child chart their apex relative to a high step starting at zero is a(a+b conjugate(ρ))ρʲ/c, as follows from the two short sides of the triangle on the reversed bank. Multiplication by z=(a+bρ)/c takes this apex to aρʲ, the high step to (a+bρ)ρʲ, and the low step to cρʲ. Thus in the root chart the enlarged contour replaces each high step by (a at j, b at j+1) and each low step by c at j. Include its six unit rotations and its mirror, reversing its boundary sign upon reflection. Its translation remains free.

The difference of these contours bounds exactly the root's remaining half-turn pairs. Indeed every unmatched root tile is on its parent or child edge: a gate with empty far side has zero bank chain, hence only whole mates by interval cancellation. The two sets of removed root owners are disjoint because each tile has one long marker. The number K of residual pairs is (74 − outer low count − inner high count)/2.

For each of the three root axes, form the two cumulative signed width functions on the normal coordinate t. Their difference after shifting the second must be a sum of active widths 33 and 7 from at most K pairs. Also its integral must be 231 times an integer in [0,K]. It equals the difference of first moments of jumps with reversed sign. Each contour has zero total jump on each axis, so the integrals are translation independent. Inner origins are vertices of the child-root intersection, hence have integral coordinates relative to the outer origin in the root chart. Normal shifts are integers. The [translation checker](nested_contacts.py) even permits each axis to choose its own shift. For two nonempty finite jump sets it tests all shifts whose supports overlap and one disjoint representative on either side. It tests widths immediately following every jump. Of 576 oriented comparisons, 32 pass the moments and **zero** pass all three one-dimensional tests. An independent scan of every integer level over shifts bounded by the outer perimeter gives the same exclusion. This argument retains the balanced child and its entire physical contact.

## Summing disjoint costs with the exterior sources

Neutral edges have zero signed current on both banks by the two cut characters. They have even contact size. At 5063 every nonterminal neutral edge pays at least 18. At 3431 a nonterminal edge paying only 16 forces the single terminal child just analyzed to pay at least 18. This child is neutral, has a unique parent, and is outside the set of nonterminal edges. Assign its extra two owners to that parent. Thus the total neutral contact cost is at least 16Z+2I on both boards, irrespective of nesting.

Remove these owners from the *signed count calculation*. Every parity sum on either side of a spine cut, and on either side of a charged leaf cut, stays unchanged: each removed bank has sum zero at its own short lift. No tiles have been removed from the geometric tiling. The [two-cut bound](../boundary-word/README.md) still charges its two full physical spine banks and the six absolute residuals to the remaining owners. Its arithmetic lower bounds are 107 and 149. With a charged off-spine leaf, the [three-cut bound](../charge-variation/CHARGED_SEAM.md) instead gives 149 and 171. Finite search ceilings in those arithmetic proofs may use the remaining unmatched count: its sign populations still account for every prescribed parity sum and all distinguished bank owners survive. At each odd vertex the short signed charge and the signed even owners directed to it likewise survive, giving the separate (c+d)V bound on the same remainder. This proves the stated maximum. It is not valid to remove a neutral branch physically.
