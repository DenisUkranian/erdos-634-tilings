# The exact finite-band lattice and what a direction bound does close

7 October 2026. Research for Denis Paliy; internal derivation, not an external review.

This note makes the positioned-current reduction quantitatively explicit.
It does **not** classify all tile counts or decide 154. The missing direction
bound in the supplied positional note has a finite, count-dependent answer;
turning that finite search into an all-count structural classification is a
separate problem.

## 1. Exact lattice, rather than a denominator overestimate

Write `O=Z[rho]`, with `rho=exp(i*pi/3)`, and let

    gcd(a,b)=1, a,b>0, c^2=a^2+ab+b^2,
    Z=a+b*rho, z=Z/c.

There are a unit epsilon of O and a primitive omega in O such that

    Z = epsilon * omega^2,  Norm(omega)=c,
    gcd((omega),(conjugate(omega)))=O.

For every L<=U, put k=U-L. Then the lattice in the finite-band vertex lemma is
exactly

    Lambda_[L,U] = sum_{h=L}^U z^h O
                 = z^L * conjugate(omega)^(-k) O.            (1)

Its fundamental area is `sqrt(3)/(2*c^k)`. In particular

    [Lambda_[L,U] : z^L O] = c^k.                            (2)

Thus the lattice is itself a rotated, uniformly scaled triangular lattice.
The rough containment in `z^L*c^(-k) O` has c^(2k) ambient lattice points
per original unit cell; the actual lattice has only c^k.

**Proof of the square factorization.** Primitivity implies
`gcd(a,c)=gcd(b,c)=1`. Also 3 does not divide c: if 3 divides c, reduction
modulo 3 forces a=b modulo 3; unless both are divisible by 3, the norm
has valuation exactly one at 3, contradicting its being c^2. Common
Eisenstein prime factors of Z and conjugate(Z) would divide both their
sum and difference. Away from the ramified prime above 3, this forces a
common rational divisor of a and b; the ramified prime is already excluded.
Hence their ideals are coprime. In the Euclidean ring O, every prime exponent
in Z is even, since the rational norm is c^2 and its conjugate prime is
absent. Unique factorization gives Z=epsilon*omega^2 and Norm(omega)=c.
The conjugate factors are coprime. Primitivity of omega follows, for a
rational integer dividing both its coordinates would divide a and b.

**Proof of (1).** We have `z=epsilon*omega/conjugate(omega)`. Multiplying
the sum by `conjugate(omega)^k` gives the ideal generated by

    epsilon^h * omega^h * conjugate(omega)^(k-h), 0<=h<=k.

This is `(omega,conjugate(omega))^k=O`. Rotation by z^L has absolute
value one; the squared absolute value of conjugate(omega)^(-k) is c^(-k).
This proves (1) and (2).

The use of unique factorization here is elementary: the hexagonal lattice
has covering radius less than one, so division with remainder reduces the
integer norm, proving that O is Euclidean. It is not an unproved arithmetic
hypothesis about the tile parameters.

## 2. Each new height introduces exactly c cosets

After normalizing L=0, the successive lattices are

    O subset conjugate(omega)^(-1)O subset ... .

The quotient between consecutive lattices is isomorphic to
`O/conjugate(omega)O`. It has c elements and, as an additive group, is
cyclic: multiplication by a primitive Eisenstein integer has a 2 by 2
integer matrix whose entries have gcd one and determinant c, so its Smith
normal form is diag(1,c).

This describes the nested grids exactly. It does not imply that choices
in different cosets are independent or that fractional current solutions
are integral.

For the 154 candidate,

    a=8, b=7, c=13, omega=3+rho,
    conjugate(omega)=4-rho,
    eta=1/(4-rho)=(3+rho)/13,
    Lambda_[L,U]=z^L * eta^(U-L) O.

The lattice for k=1 is `Z+eta Z`, agreeing with the complete two-level
certificate. At k=2 its index is 169, and at k=11 its index is
`13^11 = 1792160394037`. Even this exact lattice therefore grows
exponentially with the band width.

## 3. A universal finite direction bound already follows from contacts

The positive-length tile-contact graph of a tiling of a convex polygon is
connected: a generic path inside the target between tile interiors avoids
all vertices and crosses actual edges. A tile of short height h has edge
heights h and one of h-1,h+1. Two contacting tiles therefore have short
heights differing by at most two.

A simple path between a tile at minimum height L and one at maximum
height U uses at most N-1 contacts. Consequently

    U-L <= 2(N-1).                                         (3)

If the height of one boundary side is g, a tile incident to an open part
of that side has short height in {g-1,g,g+1}. Every tile then has height
in `[g-2N+1,g+2N-1]`. This is an absolute finite interval once N and the
boundary normalization are fixed. It does not require a conjectural
normal form or replacement by another tiling.

For I120, F1, equilateral, F2 and F3 targets, the previously proved
population divisibilities improve the number of occupied levels to at
most floor(N/c)+1 (with the stated distinguished level). Ordering occupied
levels and using connectivity gives adjacent gaps at most two; hence their
span is at most twice one less than their number. For I120 with N not
divisible by c, height zero occurs, so both extrema are explicitly bounded
by twice floor(N/c).

A separate note in this package proves the stronger no-gap statement for
the small I120 regime N<c^2. We do not duplicate its proof or claim its
unrestricted extension here.

## 4. Exact sufficiency is available, but is still an instance solver

For a fixed primitive tile and fixed target, combine (3), the finite-band
vertex lemma, and (1). Enumerate every triangle placement whose vertices
are in the resulting lattice and whose orientation lies in the finite
height range. Retain only placements contained in the target. This is a
finite and complete dictionary. The positioned boundary equation

    Bx=b_target, x in Z_{>=0}^J

is necessary and sufficient by the supplied current argument. Nonnegative
integer solutions are genuine nonoverlapping tilings. Thus there is no
remaining logical need for an *unbounded* direction search for fixed N.

However, this is not a new all-N classification: the repository already
had fixed-N decidability by complete incidence disks and rational
coordinate development. It substitutes an explicit finite placement
matrix for a finite list of incidence schemes. A criterion whose decisive
step is solving a new, exponentially large integer system for the given
input has not removed the geometric existence problem.

In particular, neither the lattice identity nor the direction bound
implies total unimodularity of B, equality of integer and fractional
feasibility, a parameter-independent height bound, or a finite family of
constructive recipes. None of these stronger assertions is used here.
The work still needed for a structural classification is a theorem
characterizing feasibility of these matrices in the classified target
families, or a different necessary-and-sufficient construction theorem.

## 5. Reproducible algebraic check

`check_exact_band_lattice.py` generates primitive plus-norm triples with
1<=a,b<=200 and checks k=0,...,7. It independently computes the gcd of
all 2 by 2 minors of the original rational-lattice generators after
clearing denominators. The resulting covolume equals c^(-k) in every case.
It also checks a square factorization up to all six units for each tile.
These are finite regressions for the algebraic proof, not a complete
check of tiling geometry.
