# Which rational coordinates can occur in a complete F2 tiling?

In axial coordinates $1,\rho=e^{i\pi/3}$ based at the 60-degree corner of the unit board, every vertex of every tile in a complete tiling belongs to $\mathbb Z[1/c]^2$. More specifically, if all edges in a walk from the corner to a vertex have heights of absolute value at most $h$, then its two coordinates belong to $c^{-h}\mathbb Z$. This is a necessary condition on proposed exact placements.

Indeed one outer side has direction 1. Adjacency across positive-length edge contacts propagates all the directions into the group $\rho^j z^k$ with $z=(a+b\rho)/c$. Traversing the connected union of tile sides gives a path from the emitting corner to any tile vertex. One can subdivide at crossings and T-junctions. At a turn the meeting point is a tile-side endpoint on both supporting lines, and the [integer-offset lemma](../first-cuts/CORNER_RAYS.md) gives an integer distance along the straight run. At the terminal tile vertex, the last run may continue on a side of that tile or on a different line. In the latter case the vertex has a transverse tile edge and is again an endpoint on the last line by the same two-bank argument. Since $z^{-1}=\bar z$ and $\rho^2=\rho-1$, multiplying any such direction vector by $c^{|k|}$ makes both coordinates integral. Summing the run displacements proves the claim. The [corner-path theorem](../first-cuts/CORNER_PATHS.md) gives further restrictions for one sign of $k$.

There is also a direct bound depending only on $N=pq$. Both axial board sides have height zero. The third has height two since $ap\rho-bq=\rho(a+b\rho)^2$. The [endpoint-connectivity theorem](../cuts/ENDPOINT_CONNECTIVITY.md) gives a path of at most $\lfloor(N+1)/2\rfloor$ endpoint contacts from the board to each tile. At each contact a side direction has already been fixed, and any other side of that tile differs from it by at most one in height: the two shorter sides are parallel modulo 60 degrees, and the long side adds or subtracts one copy of the angle of $z$. Thus all vertices of every hypothetical tiling lie on the exact axial grid $c^{-2-\lfloor(N+1)/2\rfloor}\mathbb Z^2$. This elementary bound does not decide a unit board by inspection.

For the concrete 3431 construction, the 3423 packing has two congruent convex pentagons of area 16 tile units. Each contains twelve retained tiles. In each pentagon, remove four and insert five, gaining two placements overall. A first exact coordinate choice put one new triangle per pentagon at a translation with denominator divisible by primes other than 37. That choice could never occur at those coordinates in a full tiling. The five translations can instead be adjusted together while keeping all the edge vectors fixed. The [final coordinates](round_3425.json) have a common denominator $37^5$. The [checker](check_boards.py) reconstructs the resulting 3425 tiles and verifies congruence, containment and all possible overlaps with exact integer arithmetic. The original 3423 packing was saturated, so this gain required removing tiles.

The resulting packing leaves area equal to three tiles inside each pentagon. A full dissection cannot keep either pentagon as a separately tiled region, since it has a side of length one while the shortest tile side has length seven. The coordinate condition by itself does not assert extendibility of a packing.
