# A phase bound at every height of an F2 tiling

Use the primitive odd F2 board $O=0$, $B=bq$, $C=ap\rho$ with $a>b$, $p=a+2b$, $q=2a+b$, $c^2=a^2+ab+b^2$, $N=pq$ and $\rho=e^{i\pi/3}$. Put $R=\mathbb Z[\rho]$, $K=\mathbb Q(\rho)$ and $z=(a+b\rho)/c$. An edge heading is $\rho^j z^h$. Each tile has both shorter sides at its short height $h$ and its long side at $h+1$ or $h-1$. The two axial board sides have height 0 and the other side has height 2 since $C-B=c^2\rho z^2$. Every tile vertex belongs to $K$ by the [integer-coordinate lemma](../packing/UNIT_COORDINATES.md).

Choose an Eisenstein prime $\pi$ such that $v_\pi(z)=e>0$. Normalize $v_\pi(\pi)=1$. Let $A=\{x\in K:v_\pi(x)\ge0\}$. At every height $h$ partition $K$ into all translates of $z^hA$. Regard such a translate as a node at level $h$. Each node has a unique parent at level $h-1$, since $z^hA\subset z^{h-1}A$. Only finitely many occupied nodes matter for a finite tiling. The two ends of a height-$h$ edge determine its single node. All three vertices of a short-height-$h$ tile determine the same short node, since its two shorter sides have integer lengths. Its long-edge node is either a child or the parent of its short node.

**Theorem.** For each $h\ne0$, each individual node of short height $h$ contains zero tiles or a positive multiple of $c$ of at least $2c$. The same holds at height zero outside $A$. At node $A$ of height zero the population is congruent to $N$ modulo $c$.

A subtree rooted at level $k$ is called exterior-free when it contains none of the three nodes of outer edges. If an exterior-free subtree contains tiles but its own root contains zero tiles, the subtree contains exactly $2c^2$ tiles. In the entire tree there is at most one exterior-free empty node with occupied descendants. Consequently two branches that separate at a shallow level have *separate* population costs even at equal heights.

**Proof of the cut formula.** All segments on a given supporting line have integral physical lengths. Include a collinear outer edge with its opposite orientation. The directed interval indicators sum to zero almost everywhere. After grouping by endpoint arclength modulo one on that line, each group still cancels: all jumps at a given coordinate are in one group and every group is compactly supported. At a generic point exactly two banks meet, hence overlapping side intervals give the same endpoint residue. At height $h$ an integer displacement along the line belongs to $z^hA$. The oriented edge sum thus cancels independently for each line *and* each of its height nodes. This uses the proof of the [integer-offset lemma](../first-cuts/CORNER_RAYS.md) and permits T-junctions.

Take a subtree $S$ at level $k$, and form the union $U$ of tiles whose short node belongs to $S$. Subtract from $\partial U$ the CCW outer sides with nodes in $S$. By the nodewise cancellation the resulting chain consists of whole long tile edges, with signed coefficient equal to the indicator of short-node membership minus that of long-node membership. Since the two nodes are adjacent, an edge crosses only the parent/root cut. It has height $k-1$ or $k$. If $S$ is exterior-free this is the entire boundary of $U$.

All its displacements belong to the rotated lattice $cz^{k-1}(R+zR)$, of axial covolume $c$. In fact the kernel of $(x+y\rho)\mapsto (a+b\rho)(x+y\rho)\bmod cR$ has index $c$: the first coordinate condition is $ax-by=0\bmod c$, and the second follows by the norm identity and $\gcd(b,c)=1$. Thus $R+zR$ contains $R$ with index $c$. A finite balanced directed endpoint graph splits into closed walks. Translating each walk independently to the origin shows its doubled axial area is an integer multiple of $c$. By addition of signed boundary integrals, $c$ divides $ab\,\#U$, hence $c\mid\#U$.

Subtract the corresponding subtree counts at children of an exterior-free node to get $c\mid n_{h,S}$ for its own short tiles. At each height at most one subtree contains an outer node: for $h\le0$ all board vertices lie in $A$, for $h=1,2$ the only eligible outer edge is the height-2 side, and higher up none remains. Subtract the exterior-free counts from the known [whole-board populations](../../FULL_BOARD.md) to obtain the core-node congruence. That source's heights equal $2-h$ in our frame (complex conjugation followed by a rotation and relabeling), with the exceptional height 2 there corresponding to 0 here.

To exclude population exactly $c$, apply the [line-count proof](../../LINE_COUNTS.md) to the selected node. All short edges of that node give full signed length equations on every support: long edges have length $c$, and cancellation split by node was just proved. Exterior contributions are zero or multiples of $c$ except in the height-zero core. The signed tile count is therefore a multiple of $c$ because $\gcd(a-b,c)=1$. If the node has $c$ tiles their signs are identical, as $c$ is odd. The incidence and coprime-parameter contradiction in that proof uses only the short edges of these $c$ tiles, the per-line congruences and distinctness of placements; it applies unchanged within the individual node.

If an exterior-free root is empty, the only surviving crossing edges in its boundary have long height $k$, from short nodes at $k-1$. The [one-height boundary argument](../../FULL_BOARD.md) gives $c^2\mid\#U$ and excludes $\#U=c^2$. It applies to boundaries represented as chains with multiplicities. Since $N<3c^2$, any nonempty such $U$ has size exactly $2c^2$. Two nested empty roots preceding an occupied node would have equal subtree mass, so no intervening node could contain a tile. A pair of consecutive empty roots with occupied descendants is impossible: across the lower edge of the tree joining the two empty nodes there are no crossing tiles of either type, and the inner subtree would have zero boundary despite its positive area. Two exterior-free empty roots with occupied descendants in disjoint branches would require $4c^2>N$ tiles in total. This proves the theorem.

**A joint restriction on direction and position.** If a tile at short height $h$ has an anchor $x$ with $v_\pi(x)=-m<0$, set $k=1+\lfloor-m/e\rfloor$. When $k\le h$, its ancestors from $k$ through $h$ are exterior-free. At least $h-k$ nodes are occupied, so this single remote subtree requires at least $2c(h-k)$ tiles. For $k\le0$ all tiles that cover any positive-length outer interval lie outside this subtree, since its eligible outer headings are outside it and all board headings exceed $k-1$. For multiple anchors one may count all the corresponding distinct ancestors. This bounds possible orientations by their position in every tiling, even for arithmetic parameters different from the controls.

The proof with $v_\pi(z)<0$ follows after reversing height and reversing the tree order, with exterior-free branches and exceptional nodes accounted for in the same way. No claim of a full tiling or of unrestricted nonexistence for the two control boards is made.

## Sparse height levels must occupy one Euclidean lattice

At any $h\ne0$ with $0<n_h<4c$, **all the tiles of that short height have their vertices in one translate of $z^h R$**. Thus a positive-length contact between two of their long sides must be their entire common side, and the two tiles are half-turn mates. In particular a partial contact between long sides of the same short height implies $n_h\ge4c$. The ordinary levels of size $2c$ or $3c$ therefore have an intrinsic mesh even when T-junctions and arbitrary directions occur elsewhere. More generally, if one can already distinguish $s$ different phases at a single chosen prime and height, at least $2cs$ tiles at that height are necessary.

For the reverse valuation tree take an Eisenstein prime with $v_\pi(z)<0$, and reverse the direction of descent ($h$ decreases down the tree). The two outer sides of height zero then give the only eligible outside headings at the intermediate level. Both side nodes belong to $A$ at height zero; all three vertices of the board are integral. At levels $h\ge0$ there is at most one subtree containing an exterior node, since every eligible exterior edge has endpoints in the single ball $z^h A$ containing $A$. At negative heights there is none. At a cut the two crossing height alphabets are again consecutive, so the lattice area calculation and the argument using the global height populations give the same per-node $2c$ lower bound.

For every prime over $c$, one of the two trees applies. In fact 3 does not divide $c$, and a rational prime inert in $R$ dividing $c$ would divide both $a$ and $b$ by the primitive norm equation. For a split prime dividing $c$, its two factors cannot both divide $a+b\rho$ by primitivity. Its norm is $c^2$, so the two valuations of $z$ are nonzero with opposite signs. If $n_h<4c$, there is only one occupied node at level $h$ at each of these primes. For any two tile vertices $x,y$ at this short height, $(x-y)/z^h$ is therefore integral at every prime over $c$. It is integral away from $c$ by $x,y\in R[1/c]$ and $z^{\pm1}\in R[1/c]$. Reducing numerator and denominator in the Euclidean ring $R$ now gives $(x-y)/z^h\in R$, as claimed.

In this lattice the long side has vector a sixth root of unity times $a+b\rho$ or $a+b\bar\rho$. Each of these is primitive as an integral axial vector since $\gcd(a,b)=1$. Along its supporting line, successive lattice points are separated by the full tile-side length $c$. Overlapping collinear long edges with lattice endpoints consequently coincide. The possible neighbors on the opposite side of this scalene tile edge are its half-turn mate and its reflection across the supporting line. Reflection sends its short height $h$ to $2(h\pm1)-h$, whereas the half-turn preserves $h$. This proves the geometric assertion.
