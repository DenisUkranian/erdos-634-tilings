# How close is the 3427 packing to a complete tiling?

There are 3427 complete congruent tiles on the 3431 board. Remove the twelve old tiles in each of the two pentagons of the [3423 packing](SATURATION.md) and place fourteen new tiles inside each one. The [coordinate table](pentagon14.json) specifies the fourteen triangles in one pentagon; the second copy uses the translation $(-4662,6105)/37$. The short-side orientations are powers of $z=(33+7\rho)/37$ times a 60-degree rotation, with both handednesses allowed. The listed anchor coordinates have denominator $37^5$. Coordinates of the other vertices can have higher powers of 37 in their denominators. Each pentagon now has two tile units of free area. The [full-board checker](check_3427.py) reconstructs all 3427 triangles and tests the metric, containment, area and pairwise interior disjointness. Run `python3 check_3427.py` from this directory.

**Every full 3431 tiling must omit at least twenty of these 3427 placements.** The uncovered area is four tile units, yet keeping even one of twenty specified triangles forces at least 38 entirely new placements. The result holds for the stated certificate. It permits arbitrary edge contacts, translations and orientations in a hypothetical tiling.

Write a unit side direction as $\rho^jz^h$, with $h$ called its height and $\rho=e^{i\pi/3}$. The height is unique because $z$ has angle irrational relative to $\pi$, as established in the [corner-ray proof](../first-cuts/CORNER_RAYS.md). All edge directions in a full tiling have this form. The three sides of a tile use exactly two consecutive heights: its short sides agree in height, and its long side differs by one. Say that such a tile bridges the integer $d$ when its heights are $d,d+1$.

The graph of all tiles joined along positive-length intervals is connected. To see this, join two interior points by a polygonal path in the board avoiding the finite set of tile vertices and crossing tile sides transversely. At a crossing the two tiles have a side in the same direction, hence the same height. Consequently, if the tiling has a height at most $d$ and a height above $d$, some tile bridges $d$. Otherwise all tiles fall on one side or the other and no edge of the contact graph connects them. A single tile can bridge just one integer. The board boundary supplies edges of height zero.

In the retained exterior of the two pentagons the 3399 tiles bridge $-1,0,1$ with multiplicities 540, 1670, 1189. The 28 new tiles inside the pentagons include twenty whose smaller height is at least 40. Across the whole 3427-tile packing, no tile bridges any of $2,3,\ldots,39$. If a true tiling retains any of those twenty, every one of the 38 integers needs its own new tile, by the preceding paragraph. Since $3431-3427=4$, at least $38-4=34$ placements of the packing must then be omitted. If no high tile is retained, at least twenty are already omitted. This proves the assertion in both cases. The [exact height checker](height_changes.py) recovers the bridge counts from the coordinates of the old tiles and the signed exponents of the new ones.

More generally, for a fixed packing of $N-k$ allowed placements, suppose $r$ of them are omitted in a complete tiling. The tiling has $k+r$ placements outside the packing. For each gap between consecutive heights that the tiling spans and that no tile of the packing bridges, at least one distinct such placement is required. This gives a lower bound on necessary changes to other proposed packings without constraining the geometry of their unfilled regions.
