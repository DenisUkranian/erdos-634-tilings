# Exclusion of the exceptional isosceles 120-degree family

**Manuscript theorem.** For each integer k≥3 set
$$
a=k(3k+2),\quad b=2k+1,\quad c=3k(k+1)+1,\quad q=a+2b.
$$
The isosceles triangle of sides (bc,bc,bq) cannot be dissected into congruent triangles of sides (a,b,c). This includes k=5, with target (1001,1001,1177) and tile (85,11,91). No primality hypothesis enters the proof. All tile orientations and T-junctions are allowed. The applicable published local row proofs are Beeson and Roşu, [§11, Lemmas 32–34](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23453v4#S11). The changes required for 120-degree tiles, moving interior banks and floating endpoints are given here and in the accompanying proof of the two-bank lemma. These new geometric arguments have not been formally checked in Lean or externally refereed.

## 1. Arithmetic and angles

Set h=3k+1. Then a+c=hb, c−a=k+1, c=kh+b, and gcd(c,b)=1 because 4c≡1 mod b. The identity c²=a²+ab+b² gives a tile with γ=120° opposite c and α+β=60° opposite a and b respectively. Twice the cosine of β is (2a+b)/c=(2c−1)/c, a nonintegral rational. Since twice the cosine of a rational multiple of π is an algebraic integer, β/π and α/β are irrational. Comparing the independent coefficients of α and β shows that each acute tile angle is indivisible into smaller tile angles. The target has base angles α because 2cos α=q/c. Its apex angle is α+3β. Its corners therefore contain no γ. The two base vertices have one α each. The apex contains one α and three β.

For every 1≤n≤k, each of na, nb and nc has only its homogeneous decomposition into the three lengths. To check nc, cancel the c terms and call the remaining multiplier m>0. The number X of a terms satisfies X≡−m mod b since a≡−c mod b. Hence m c≥(b−m)a, giving mh≥a, contrary to kh<a. Interchanging a and c gives mh≥c for a nonpure decomposition of na, likewise impossible. For nb, after cancelling b terms, write m b=Xa+Zc. Since m≤k, the bounds X,Z<b hold and reduction modulo b gives X=Z. If positive they give m≥h>k.

We also need an upper bound beyond the range of purity. For 1≤j≤b, every decomposition ja=Xa+Yb+Zc has Z≤k. If Z>0, write m=j−X. Then 0<Z<m≤b since c>a, and m+Z≡0 mod b, so m+Z=b. Substitution gives Y=hm−c. Thus m≥k+1 and Z≤k.

## 2. Local facts used for forced strips

Orient a line segment along a bank of whole tile edges. The region or exterior half-plane on its other side is already filled. Call a block of c edges stopped at an internal endpoint if the following non-c tile presents γ at that endpoint. In an ordered gap of non-c edges the γ ends cannot face one another because 2γ>π. Thus each inner gap stops at least one adjacent block. An end gap also stops its incident block when the endpoint wedge contains no γ. Some c block consequently has two stops, counting the ends of the bank as stops when a c reaches them.

A c tile displaying α at a start is protected if its b-ray has an a or c across it from that same point, or if the b-ray lies on a genuine continuing exterior side. The short-run induction of the cited §11 constructs a grid outward from this start. In its triangle step H(w) the exposed diagonal consists of w sides a. Its upper end is clamped by the through-edge on the starting wall of length wb, or by the continuing true exterior. The opposite bank of the diagonal must consist of w sides a by purity. Its upper residual sector is α+β, which excludes γ. Descending the diagonal, each tile must display β at its upper end and γ at its lower end since two γ cannot meet on one side. This gives the required inverted companions. At intervals where a proposed next tile displays its b side in the wrong direction the trapezoid step applies: such an anomalous edge forces a through-edge above a segment of nc, 1≤n≤w−1, by the purity of nc. The resulting diagonal of at most w sides a is again pure. The sectors α, β and α+β and the same upper-downward placement are exactly the ones used in Lemmas 32–33. The role of a and b can be interchanged when starting in β, provided the initial a wall has the corresponding continuation. A lower diagonal endpoint strictly inside a backed bank cannot overhang it transversely. At such an endpoint the same argument forces the terminal inverted companion even without a γ stop. This justifies a scan up to any strictly internal joint at which its companion is required. At a first reversal of a uniform row the two c tiles would show either α,α or β,β at that joint. The complementary sectors α+3β and 3α+β contain no γ, contradicting this terminal companion.

For an ordinary true side ≤k c tiles cannot form a doubly stopped run provided the initial α at an external endpoint is protected. At an interior orientation where two α meet, the intervening sector α+3β contains one α and three β, so a flanking β protects one of the two α starts. An interior α at a γ stop is likewise protected by the unique intervening β. A terminal β-only endpoint of a true board side is covered by the actual boundary alternative in the published trapezoid proof. These observations establish a run of at least k+1 consecutive c's in this exterior setting. On the initial I120 board the true wall at a base-angle α corner continues more than kb. At the apex an α at an endpoint of c is next to β, which places an a or c opposite its b-ray.

## 3. Selecting one protected corner

Each original leg has length bc and at least k+1 c sides. After removing all its c sides, any nonzero residual would be nc with n≤k and contain no c, contradicting purity. Both legs are therefore pure, with b=2k+1 c sides each. At each base corner the other side is b and hence lies on the true base. A scan from this corner orients the first k+1 leg tiles with α towards the base. If the tile at the apex also displayed α there, the next β tile of the apex fan would protect its b-ray and orient k+1 tiles oppositely from that end. These prefixes overlap on this leg with only 2k+1 edges. Both extreme apex tiles thus show β. The two remaining apex tiles have angles α and β. At least one extreme β is adjacent to the α tile. Select the corresponding leg. Its inward a-ray from the apex meets either b or c on the far side, with that edge starting at the apex itself.

Let O be the base corner at this leg. Let B be the vector of length b along the base towards the opposite corner, C the vector of length c up this leg, and A=bC the apex, taking O for origin. Put U_j=jB and V_j=jB+(b−j)C. Strip j consists of b−j standard upright tiles between the lines U_jV_j and U_(j+1)V_(j+1), and their b−j−1 inverted companions. Its diagonal edge at the top is parallel to B−C. The scans starting from the true base and the protected apex on the selected leg each give k inverses and k+1 consistently oriented tiles. Their union is the entire strip 0.

Suppose strips 0 through j−1 have been placed, for 1≤j≤k. There are j consecutive a sides on the diagonal from A to V_j. Its other bank starts with b or c at A. Purity of ja forces V_j to lie strictly inside an opposite-bank edge. This transverse through-edge clamps the segment U_jV_j at its upper endpoint. The lower endpoint is clamped by the true base. Its unfilled-side wedges are α at the bottom and β at the top. At the top the previous strip puts α+γ on one side, and the through-edge contributes a straight angle. A scan starting there with β extends the same a-wall by w more a sides. While j+w≤k the length (j+w)a still cannot have a decomposition beginning with the apex b or c. This guarantees continuation at the starting wall for the β-start scan, with the two side labels exchanged as explained above.

Consequently a doubly stopped block on this bank has at least k+1−j c sides. A block strictly inside uses the ordinary terminal-stop proof. A block reaching the bottom uses the true exterior base. A block reaching the top only is scanned FROM that β endpoint, terminating at a strict interior stop, valid for w≤k−j. The whole bank has (b−j)c≥(k+1−j)c of length. Subtracting all its c sides now leaves nc for n≤k, so it must be entirely c. The lower scan contributes k inverses and the upper scan k−j, precisely the b−j−1 internal joints. This proves strips 0 through k with both endpoints handled.

## 4. All subsequent strips and the contradiction

We invoke the **two-bank descent lemma**, whose proof is in the adjoining [TWO_BANK_DESCENT.md](TWO_BANK_DESCENT.md). If a clamped segment has c capacity K≤k and ends in a single β wedge at an internal transverse through-edge, its short terminal run can escape the ordinary proof only by exposing an auxiliary a-diagonal on the through-edge's line. Suppose this line also has a preceding fully backed prefix of length Lc, L≤k, aligned with that diagonal, whose opposite bank is anchored at its beginning. The lemma forbids the configuration under the protected start conditions in that file. In the triangle case the prolonged bank has an α+β terminal wedge. In the trapezoid case its c capacity is at most L and its transverse prefix has length dc with d<K. Both cases include a possible partial overhang across the intermediate joint. In the latter case switching the two lines reduces K+L and preserves the directed anchor. See [ENDPOINT_REVIEW.md](ENDPOINT_REVIEW.md) for the check of its use of the published terminal subcases.

Suppose k+1≤j≤b−1 and all preceding strips have been forced. The bank U_jV_j now has length Lc, L=b−j≤k, with a full c backing terminating at V_j. Its lower end is on the true base at a protected α wedge. If a tile on its other bank overhangs its upper end then the line U_jV_j has a through-edge across V_j. The diagonal AV_j is consequently clamped at both ends; its opposite bank anchors at A with the α apex tile described above and has total length ja. At A the free wedge is α+2β, and an α on a c-edge there has an adjacent β protecting its b-ray. At V_j the already placed strip and the assumed through-edge leave exactly β between V_jA and the continuation of U_jV_j. The upper bound in §1 gives c capacity K=k for AV_j. The transverse backed prefix is exactly the Lc bank just described and its start is the true base. All starting-side and terminal-sector requirements of the two-bank lemma therefore hold. It rules out the overhang.

Hence the free bank of U_jV_j is divided into whole sides. It is all c by purity of Lc. The lower genuine base scan runs to all L−1 internal joints, since L≤k, determining every upright tile and its inside companion. This completes every strip through b−1. The union is a standard b-fold triangle with boundary vertices O,A,bB; it has a side of length ba from A to bB. This cuts the original board into two subtriangles made of whole tiles. Here bB is strictly between the original base endpoints, as b²<bq.

In the other triangle the two ends of that side have angles α+2β at A and α+β at bB, since the standard cap used β and γ respectively. The third corner has angle α. Neither endpoint sector contains γ; whenever c shows α there, the neighboring β provides the required protection. The other ray is now an actual side of this triangular child, so the ordinary exterior-side theorem of §2 implies at least k+1 consecutive c's on its side ba. Section 1 for j=b says that the entire side can have at most k. This is a contradiction.

## 5. Implications for semiprimes and an additional residue filter

The [prime-pair reduction, §2](https://github.com/DenisUkranian/erdos-634-tilings/blob/c18097f56242dd43aebb11e54f5a56cf6eeca49b/research/2026-11-19/continuation/RESULTS.md) derives BQ, Paliy QP, I120 and F2 as the only nonclassical arithmetic candidates for two distinct primes above 3. For a nonclassical I120 pair p<q the prime equation is 4q=3p²+6p−1, with p≡q≡3 mod 4. Putting k=(p−1)/2 yields exactly the theorem above, starting with k=3. The separate adjacent-BQ proof is identified below. Hence all pairs above 3 of equal residue modulo 4 are classified. Pairs with two residues 1 are classical. If both are 3, the positive pairs are precisely those with 3p<2q<4p and both q−p and 2q−3p positive squares, by Paliy's construction.

For the mixed case the only candidate is F2 with p+q divisible by 3. In that case p=a+2b, q=2a+b for odd coprime a,b and c²=a²+ab+b², so ab≡7 mod 8 and pq≡7 mod 8. It follows pq≡23 mod 24. There is an additional restriction if 13∤pq. The [primitive normalization](https://github.com/DenisUkranian/erdos-634-tilings/blob/a837eb58e837132734342a53b889aade5cad723b/research/global-criterion-oct7/SHARP_NESTED_CONE.md) gives the unordered pair {a,b}={u²−v²,v(2u+v)} for coprime u>v>0, with no factor 3 denominator after swapping a,b when needed. Its symmetric F2 product is
$$
H(u,v)=(u^2+4uv+v^2)(2u^2+2uv-v^2).
$$
In the projective slopes (u/v)=0,1,…,12,∞ over F_13 the values of H on representatives (x,1) or (1,0) are respectively
$$
12,5,0,12,0,10,6,0,0,10,4,4,2,2\pmod{13}.
$$
Fourth powers of nonzero scalars are 1,3,9, giving nonzero product residues precisely 2,4,5,6,10,12. By CRT only 23,71,95,119,239,311 modulo 312 can contain unresolved pairs among the 96 invertible residue classes. Some of those pairs already admit constructions or are arithmetically excluded. This simple filter does not prove F2 realizability in the remaining classes.

## Adjacent BQ sources and reproducibility

For the BQ tile (k(k−1), 2k−1, k²) with scale one, k≥4, the previous package supplies OFFSET.md (successive interior banks, including purity and the count of companions) and MIXED_BRANCH_INPUTS.md (outer boundary and forced mixed corner). The latter's local run proof uses Beeson and Roşu §11 and treats the true exterior starting wall. The base upper bound 2k−1 and the k forced b-edges at both ends are sufficient. No non-general search at 517 is required. The small k=2,3 counts 21,70 use [Bonfioli's independent small-count work](https://github.com/ElVec1o/erdos634_primes_and_N200/tree/752005605ca4e607d2a08f57bdc2445dd5558dbb). The replays in BankArithmetic.lean cover the integer decompositions in the OFFSET argument; they contain no Lean version of the moving-bank or I120 two-bank theorem. Sources for the original angular classification and rationality are [Beeson and Zhang, Table 1 and Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01314v1). The complete pointwise decision for the residual F2 prime parameters is a separate problem.
