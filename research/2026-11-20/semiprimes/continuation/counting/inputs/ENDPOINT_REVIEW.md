# Review of the endpoint transfer and the semiprime consequence

## Applicable geometric inputs

The base proof is Beeson and Roşu, [§11, Lemmas 32–34](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23453v4#S11), especially lines 931–984 in arXiv HTML. Their hypotheses concern an exterior side. To use their argument in an internal strip, the proof needs to account explicitly for a tile edge crossing the end of that strip. The geometric arguments reviewed here are manuscript deductions. The existing Lean file in the adjacent BQ package covers the arithmetic of the side inventories.

## Adjacent BQ at unit scale

Write a=k(k−1), b=2k−1, c=k². Let a bank at offset j have endpoints U on the true base and V on the diagonal from the apex A. The j completed left strips expose exactly j sides of length a from A to V and exactly 2k−1−j sides of length c from U to V. The tile on the far side of AV starts at A with a side b or c. Purity of ja for j<k means that V lies in the relative interior of a far-bank edge. A candidate side through V transversely cannot cross that edge at a point interior to both. Thus the other bank of UV partitions it into whole sides.

In the scan from V the same argument is applied after w further a-edges on the apex line, now requiring the purity of (j+w)a for j+w≤k−1. It fixes the starting wall through the point at which the published row induction uses it. The other diagonals have lengths at most (k−1)b, and the switched horizontal anomaly would require a decomposition of nc containing a for n≤k−2. Each of these arithmetic exclusions holds. The terminal scan always ends at a strict interior joint of the backed bank. Its auxiliary diagonal cannot pass beyond the joint transversely, and the tiles on the other bank partition the diagonal into whole sides. Together the two scans prescribe (k−1)+(k−1−j) distinct companions, the full number of interior joints. The original board's true base extends beyond both corner patches. The contradiction in the number of b-edges follows. The proof uses no assumption that opposite banks subdivide their common line in the same way. At k=6 this excludes the (30,11,36) BQ board at 517.

## I120 strong shell beyond the short-purity range

Directly reusing the short-run lemma on AV fails if the terminal wedge is β bounded by an interior through-edge. At the equality case in lines 952 and 975 of the published proof, its auxiliary diagonal can lie on that edge's line although the edge need not extend to the far end of the auxiliary diagonal. This affects the new long-offset question. The earlier offset-purity argument above uses a different scan.

The replacement in TWO_BANK_DESCENT.md carries an auxiliary fully backed prefix of length Lc on the line bounding the weak β wedge. In the triangle collision H(n), the terminal a-diagonal continues that prefix by na, on the same side. The far end has free sector α+β against a continuing line. Its two indivisible incident corners ensure the α-start and terminal-clamp conditions for the new bank, including when the endpoint tile on that bank has angle β.

In a trapezoid collision s=n the continuation is ra with 1≤r<n. At the far end R a transverse edge passes through R, and the resulting free wedge is β. Behind R on that transverse line there are d=n−r−i₀ c-edges and a free-bank b-edge at W aimed toward R with α at W. The orientation matters. In Lemma 33 every new anomaly comes from a G tile in an α sector. The maximal-s proof of Lemma 32 can be restricted to such anomalies. Each inductive replacement keeps the old witness or substitutes another G tile with its α at the new start; hence the restriction is preserved. Since this b-edge starts at W, a tile on the same bank cannot overhang W. Any run selected using the forward γ of that edge starts strictly later, so this artificial start can never be a weak terminal endpoint.

The prolonged bank has length Lc+ra. It is anchored at its original start and clamped at R, and it includes the old overhang at the intermediate point. It can have at most L c-edges by purity of ra for r≤k. Its next transverse prefix has length dc with d<n≤K. In coordinates where the current run points east, R is the east end of the backed portion on the upper line and its b anomaly points east. At the next collision both the old prefix and the new a-diagonal are backed on the same side of that line. Changing which line is the current bank reflects coordinates and preserves these directed incidences. Thus the update (K,L)→(L,d) strictly decreases their sum. An initial true base at U is a valid α end because the exterior base extends farther than kb from U. This resolves the finite interior through-edge case treated inadequately by the direct application. ## Consequence for odd prime pairs

For distinct primes 3<p<q, the normalized arithmetic reduction leaves the classical two-square construction, the constructive Paliy row, the adjacent BQ row, the I120 prime curve and F2. The two geometric exclusions in this package remove the third and fourth rows. The mixed F2 pair requires both p+q≡0 modulo 3 and pq≡7 modulo 8, hence pq≡23 modulo 24. Thus all pairs outside that last residue have the complete classical or Paliy test. For p,q both 3 modulo 4 and p<q, the latter requires 3p<2q<4p and both q−p and 2q−3p to be positive squares. These two exclusions alone do not settle F2 realizability in its residual mixed sector.
