# A two-bank descent at a beta endpoint

This note treats the one endpoint at which the ordinary stopped-run argument on an internal clamped segment differs from a genuine side of the board. The tile has angles α,β,γ opposite a,b,c in the exceptional I120 family, γ=120°, α+β=60°, α/β irrational. The following short-length properties hold for k≥3: all decompositions of na, nb and nc into a,b,c are pure for 1≤n≤k. A bank is backed when a tiled region, or a genuine exterior half-plane, keeps tiles on its free side. The free side carries whole edges between its specified endpoints. A through-edge is an edge containing the prescribed point in its relative interior.

## The collision alternative in the stopped-run proof

Consider a backed bank oriented towards its right endpoint V. Its left end is either a protected corner, as in the established pure-leg proof, or the start of a prescribed b edge displaying α on the left, hence γ on its right. In the latter case the b edge is on the free bank. The right end has one of the protected sectors of the usual argument, or a single β sector between the backwards bank ray and a forward transverse ray e'. The left end, if a corner, has no β-only terminal sector. The forward ray at a weak right end has an edge through V on the far side of the sector. Let the total number of c edges on the whole bank be at most K, where K≤k.

There is a doubly stopped c-run of length at most K. With a b edge at the left, begin the gap counting at the γ on its right. In the ordered list of non-c signs, there is a transition from an edge having γ on its right to one having γ on its left, or to the end sentinel. The intervening c-run is nonempty, since two γ's cannot meet and V has no γ in its sector. Its left stop is strictly beyond the initial left endpoint. With a protected corner use the usual two-end counting. If both ends of the run show β, one of the adjacent α/α rays has a protecting β tile because the intervening π−2α sector contains one α and three β's. Thus one obtains a protected oriented α-start subrun of length n≤K, as in Lemma 34 of Beeson–Roşu. Its terminal stop can be interior, at a protected non-β endpoint, or at the weak V. All choices terminating away from V give the unaltered short-run contradiction. In particular a run facing away from the weak V does not create another exceptional case.

Use the two proofs of Beeson–Roşu §11, Lemmas 32 and 33 for the α-start. Every diagonal used there has coefficient ≤n≤k and all anomaly tests use pure short c multiples. The alteration at a terminal stop V is explicitly where Lemma 32 dismisses the endpoint angle equal to β by observing that its auxiliary diagonal would lie on a genuine board side (arXiv line 952), and likewise Lemma 33, line 975. On our weak bank this diagonal can lie along e' in the interior. All other steps of these two proofs persist as local statements — they use only the backed side, clamped transverse endpoints, indivisibility of acute sectors, obtuseness of γ and the short pure lengths. If this exceptional terminal case is reached, exactly one of the following witnesses results.

1. **Triangle case.** A full triangular array H(n) has its terminal a-bank extending from V to R along e', length n a. At R the initial b-wall has a through-edge (or continuing true exterior). The angular sector on the other side of this n a-bank is α+β by the angle calculation π−γ (line 974). The half-turn array supplies whole a edges up to R.
2. **Trapezoid case.** The upper trapezoid proof terminates with s=n, 1≤r<s, at V. Its terminal diagonal extends from V to R along e', with length r a and whole a edges on the current side. Its top transverse line ℓ_r through R is parallel to the original bank. The far sector at R between RV and the forward part of ℓ_r is exactly β (lines 944–947). On ℓ_r behind R there is a segment WR consisting of d=s−r−i₀ consecutive c edges on the grid side, so 1≤d<n. A tile on the opposite (free) bank of that line starts at W with a b edge aimed towards R, and displays α at W. To retain the last orientation, restrict Lemma 32's anomaly to a b edge starting with α. This holds for the G tile which first invokes the trapezoid proof in Lemma 33. Each replacement anomaly in the maximal-s induction is again one of the G tiles displaying α at its start (lines 958–960). Keeping an old anomaly leaves its orientation unchanged. The same short-purity argument as in line 946 ensures a through-edge at R on that line.

The strong right sector α+β cannot lead to the exceptional case: if the terminal tile presents β there, the remaining α has a vertex at that point, clamping the auxiliary diagonal from its other bank; if an initial c tile presents α there, the neighboring β protects its b-ray. The sector with an α on the left against the true exterior or α+2β at an apex also meets the analogous protected-start and terminal conditions.

## Why iterated trapezoid collisions stop

Suppose that a backed segment ending at V with c capacity K as above is weak. On its transverse line behind V suppose in addition that there is a known segment from U to V of length Lc with an occupied side continuous with the grid that would be built out on e', where L≤k. Assume either an actual exterior α start at U with a continuing outer b-wall, or an anomaly b edge at U with γ towards V on the free bank. The transverse prefix may already have mismatched, even overhanging, edges on its free bank. Its free-bank subdivision is anchored at U.

On either collision, continue the transverse prefix from U through V to R. Now UR has length Lc+t a, with 1≤t≤n≤k. Its known bank consists of the old c edges followed by the auxiliary a edges on the same side: the latter lie inside the sector between the backwards original bank and the forward e' ray. The free opposite side cannot cross U (the exterior or known b start) and cannot cross R (the end wall passes transversely through R). Hence this side really partitions UR into whole edges; in particular the possible overhang at V has been retained. It has at most L copies of c, since subtracting Lc from any larger such inventory would be a decomposition of t a containing a c. The α+β end in the triangle case immediately gives the ordinary contradiction by the collision alternative on UR. If the terminal sector is β we are in the trapezoid case. On the transverse line now behind R, that same trapezoid has supplied the prefix WR of length dc, with the forward-pointing b anomaly just proved, and d<n≤K. Both lines and their directions exchange roles. Geometrically, in coordinates where the current ray runs to the right and the trapezoid is above it, WR lies on a line parallel to the current bank and R is its right-hand end. The newly filled terminal a edges have the appropriate orientation relative to that line to continue its backing at the next collision.

Thus the new problem has the nonnegative capacities (L,d) in place of (K,L), with d<K. Their sum strictly decreases. For zero first capacity there is already a contradiction to the nonempty doubly stopped run. Therefore no weak segment with such a transverse prefix is possible.

## Application at the large offsets of one I120 corner

After the first k+1 shells at the strong corner, take any j with k+1≤j≤2k and L=2k+1−j. If a free edge overhangs at the upper endpoint V of the offset c-bank, the apex diagonal AV of length j a is wholly backed and clamped by this through-edge. Its free bank starts at the apex: the α tile there, immediately adjacent to the extreme β, puts b or c on AV. A c showing α is protected by the adjoining β in the remaining fan. The available apex sector is α+2β. At V the free wedge of AV is the weak β sector against the through-edge. The transverse prefix U_jV has length Lc, with the true base as a protected start at U_j. The arithmetic c capacity of AV is K=k: from j a=Xa+Yb+Zc, Z>0, put m=j−X. Then Z<m≤j≤2k+1 and m+Z≡0 modulo 2k+1, hence m+Z=2k+1 and Z≤k. All descent hypotheses are met. The overhang is impossible. This is an argument about actual tiles on either free bank, including internal T-junctions. It does not replace the old proof for the initial k+1 forced shells.
