# A uniform unit-scale obstruction on the v=1 Eisenstein family

Let u>1 be an integer and g=gcd(3,u−1), with a=(u²−1)/g, b=(2u+1)/g, c=(u²+u+1)/g and a>b. Thus a,b,c are pairwise coprime, c²=a²+ab+b² and a+c=ub. Put K=floor(a/u), so K<a/u<u and K<b. When u=10, g=3 these lengths are 33,7,37 with K=3. When u=6, g=1 they are 35,13,43 with K=5.

For every n≤K, the lengths na,nb,nc can only be written as their homogeneous sums of whole tile sides. For nc, first strip out identical c summands, so that a putative collision reads m c=Xa+Yb for 1≤m≤n. Reduction modulo b gives X≡−m and hence X≥b−m, since c≡−a modulo b. Then m(a+c)≥ab, so mu≥a, contrary to K<a/u. Exchanging a and c instead gives mu≥c for a collision on na, also impossible. For nb remove matching b summands and write m b=Xa+Zc. Since m≤K<a<c, both X and Z are less than b. They are equal modulo b, hence equal, and any positive count would give m≥u. This proves simultaneous purity.

On the other hand in ab=Xa+Yb+Zc one has X≤b, Z<b and X≡Z modulo b. Apart from X=b, Z=0, this gives X=Z and Y=a−uZ. Thus **no decomposition of ab has more than K copies of c**.

The ordinary exterior stopped-run argument in the I120 theorem, Section 2, uses precisely simultaneous purity through K, γ=120°, independence of the two acute angles, and a genuine continuing exterior side or a protected starting corner. These conditions apply here with no primeness assumptions. In particular on an exterior supporting side ab with 60° angles at both endpoints, a tile showing α on its c edge must meet a β along its b-ray; its neighbour starts with a or c along that ray. An interior α–α orientation switch is protected on at least one side because the gap contains one α and three β. The stop count forces a run of at least K+1 sides c, contrary to the preceding arithmetic. The same proof applies when the endpoint angles are 60° and α+2β: each contains just one α and at least one β. The relevant other exterior segment in each application extends more than Kb.

Consequently this entire v=1 family has no unit equilateral tiling by its own tile, no unit direct F1 tiling (side triple ab,bc,b(a+b)), and no filling of the three-direction pentagon described in F2_SMALLER_FILL.md. The pentagon has a supporting side WD of length ab with 60° endpoints. Its adjacent exterior sides are b² and 2ab, both longer than Kb; nonconvexity at a different vertex does not alter the backed segment or its endpoint wedges. This includes the pentagon of 1027 tiles on the (35,13,43) board. These are obstructions to particular fixed shapes. Neither F2 board with 3431 or 5063 tiles is thereby excluded.

The geometric stopped-run part is a manuscript adaptation of Beeson and Roşu, Section 11, Lemmas 32–34, as explained in I120_THEOREM.md. It has not undergone external review. The elementary purity and upper-bound arguments above are independent of that geometry.

## Both F1 orders and all straight caps on the unit F2 board

The stopped-run test can use β as the designated start angle, with a and b interchanged. More explicitly, an endpoint containing at most one β and otherwise α protects a β-start: its adjoining α tile provides a b- or c-edge on its a-wall from that vertex. This protection holds even when the first opposite b is shorter than a. Across any subsequently supplied prefix of j whole a-edges, j≤K, the other bank starts with a non-a edge. Purity prevents that bank from terminating exactly at j a; it must continue through the end, with intervening joints dealt with on the backed line. The lower diagonal purity required after interchange is that of j b. At an internal β–β junction the intervening sector is 3α+β; an α flanks at least one of the two β starts. At an interior γ-stop the unoccupied sector beside β is precisely α. The other local run and endpoint subcases are unchanged under the exchange in the two acute labels. An endpoint containing no β cannot need β-start protection. Refer to the starting-wall and trapezoid arguments of Beeson and Roşu, Section 11, Lemmas 32–34 and the 120-degree adaptation in I120_THEOREM.md.

This rules out the exchanged F1 unit target with sides ab,ac,a(a+b) as well, since the endpoints of its side ab have angles α+β and 2α+β. More generally every true supporting side with γ-free endpoint sectors of at most one β each, and with the stated neighbouring α whenever β starts on c, obeys the same c lower bound.

Combining the straight-cut table of F2_STRAIGHT_CUTS.md with this result gives a single possible cap at all v=1 parameters: the **scale-two equilateral cap at the 60° vertex C**. No cevian is possible. Two additional checks extend the k-family calculations to g=1.

1. For any putative cut about B, let d=c−a=(u+2)/g and w=b−d=(u−1)/g. The required positive side residual ap−c(a+b)=wa−bd is less than wa. A decomposition would give 0≤X,Z<w, and X−Z≡w mod b. But b=2w+3/g and no integer in [−w+1,w−1] is in this class. Both w and d are integral since g divides u−1 and 3.
2. A direct F1 cap of scale two at C leaves a boundary AD=b²; the cap side 2ab is forced along the shorter outer side bq. In a decomposition of b², the counts X of a and Z of c are strictly less than b since a,c>b. They agree modulo b, so X=Z and Y=b−uZ. Thus Z≤floor(b/u), which is 2 for g=1 and 0 for g=3. The angles of the remaining quadrilateral at the two ends of AD are 2α and 120°−β=2α+β. There is no β at the first endpoint and a β on c at the second is protected by an adjacent α. The swapped stopped-run test forces Z≥K+1, incompatible with this count: for g=1, K=u−1≥2 and for g=3, K≥1.

After just the two c-fold kite pieces of F2_SMALLER_FILL.md, the smaller convex quadrilateral cannot be completed by the first extra b-fold corner piece either. Such a piece at A would already create a supporting side WD=ab with remaining endpoint angles 60° and 120°−β=2α+β, to which the very same exchanged test applies. This excludes a specific corner arrangement in both the 693- and 1365-tile reduced positive subproblems.

## Exact equivalence for tilings admitting a straight crosscut

At v=1, a unit F2 board admits a tiling with at least one full straight cut between distinct exterior side interiors if and only if the equilateral triangle of side 2ab admits a tiling by the same tile. Necessity is the complete cap obstruction just proved. For sufficiency use the pre-existing F2 incenter dissection in [square-class-tails.md, Appendix A](https://github.com/DenisUkranian/erdos-634-tilings/blob/a837eb58e837132734342a53b889aade5cad723b/docs/square-class-tails.md). It places three standard tile-similar triangles of integer scales a, b, c about the incenter and leaves the equilateral cap of side 2ab at C. Its far edge is the required straight crosscut. This positive direction is prior geometry attributed there to Harries's incenter partition, in the context of Zhang's transfers. All straight cuts from a board vertex to the opposite side are already excluded. A filling with no straight crosscut remains a separate possibility.
