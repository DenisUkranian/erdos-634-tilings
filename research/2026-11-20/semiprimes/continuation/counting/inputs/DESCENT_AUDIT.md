# Two-wall descent audit for the beta-terminal collision

Consider the setting of NO_CEVIAN.md and the earlier explicit full-shell construction. The notation for a trapezoid, H(w), the anomaly ℓ_r and its index i0 is from [Beeson–Roşu §11, Lemmas 32–34](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23453v4#S11). This is a diagram-free check of the descent for its application to the two upper walls.

**Bad bank.** Let S be a segment filled on one side by tiles (the backed bank). On the free side S is partitioned into whole tile edges. Suppose there are at most k occurrences of c on the free bank. The left endpoint is a real exterior clean start with the protection required for a c tile showing α, or it is a tile vertex with a first free-bank b edge whose α occurs at the start and γ points towards the other endpoint. At the right endpoint the free wedge is β or α+β, bounded by a ray continuing rightwards on a previously marked line S'. Its opposite direction is the final portion of S', already backed to that right endpoint. Reversal of all orientations is allowed. If S' passes through the endpoint, it clamps the bank there. Along S' from an earlier fixed start to the crossing there is a backed segment containing d copies of c (or, in the first step, a known initial segment of length Lc). The free side of this prefix, relative to a prospective successor bank along S', is the same side on which the b-start was marked or the corresponding clean start was available. Full backing may continue past either end.

The initial situation is the alleged overhang through the upper tip of a new shell: S is the apex diagonal of length ja and S' goes from the base along the new shell of length Lc, L=b−j≤k. The diagonal bank is clamped at the apex and by S'. Its apex start has adjacent α then β in the selected apex fan, and its other wedge is β. It has at most k copies c by the length lemma. The start of S' on the actual base supplies the clean α-start, protected by the real base. The true apex start needs only have no γ and protect whenever it presents c and α.

**Local outcomes.** On S choose a doubly stopped run of c tiles on the free bank by the count of Lemma 34. For a b-start, there is such a run strictly beyond the starting point. Indeed the first gap contributes a stop because its first non-c tile faces γ forward. For α+β at a right endpoint an α-c tile is protected by the adjacent β at that endpoint. At a β wedge the endpoint cannot show α. At an interior meeting with α on the two adjoining c tiles the intermediate sector has one α and three β angles, so an extremal β protects a possible starting tile. Reducing to one of the two subruns when needed, this gives an α-start protected run of n≤k tiles heading to a stop. Non-c endpoints are interior stops presenting γ; problem endpoints can therefore occur only when the run ends at a virtual β wedge. Each step uses only purity of wa, wb, and of ℓ c for 1≤w,ℓ≤k, which holds for the exceptional I120 tiles.

Through the proof of Lemma 33 including the trapezoid contradiction of Lemma 32, distinguish the two terminal equality events. Away from equality, the proof gives its contradiction on a backed bank: transverse edges cannot overhang into the occupied backing at an interior base point. At a clamped wedge with angle larger than β the remaining positive sector requires a tile vertex and the cross wall terminates, exactly as on a whole side of the board. For an interior γ stop two such angles cannot fit. The same argument applies at a true exterior corner of wedge larger than β. At a real exterior b wall in a start the existing outer line is the persistent wall after each wb. If equality occurs, the terminal endpoint is the bad β endpoint and the line through the terminal auxiliary diagonal is exactly S'. The upright tiles in the trapezoid or H(w) have wa or ra successive a edges there on the SAME side already supplying the backed prefix to the terminal corner. The new bank, the union of the old prefix and this a-segment, is clamped at the start (or its first tile on the free side is the specified b-start) and at the new far endpoint by the transverse through edge furnished by Lemma 32/33. Real boundary starts preclude extension beyond the board.

1. In an H(w) equality the new wedge is π−γ=α+β by the starting-wall proof. It is a strong terminal. If the prefix length was dc, any decomposition of dc+wa contains at most d copies of c: removing d+1 or more c would give a nontrivial decomposition of wa. Accordingly the new bank contradicts the usual long-run lemma directly: with no β-only wedge, neither exceptional equality can occur.
2. In a Lemma 32 trapezoid equality, the new far endpoint is R=x(s−r,r), where the upper-bank through edge on ℓ_r has already been found. The sector on the free side of the auxiliary diagonal at R is exactly β. The new bank is therefore again a bad bank. Its new transverse prefix starts at x(i0,r) and continues to R along ℓ_r through d'c, d'=s−r−i0. It is backed throughout on the trapezoid side by c edges, and on the far side the b edge beginning at x(i0,r) presents α there and γ forward. This orientation is part of the anomaly hypothesis, maintained in the proof of Lemma 32: it holds for the defect G_j in the H(w) extension, and at lines 958–960 every replacement witness is again a G_j with the same orientation. The strict bound 1≤d'<s≤n follows from 1≤r<s and i0<s−r. To check the bank sides, choose coordinates with the original run east and the free side north. From its eastern endpoint V to R the diagonal goes northwest and its free bank is on its right, opposite the original trapezoid's tiles. At R, the ray on ℓ_r that bounds the free β wedge goes east, extending the same c prefix from x(i0,r). Upon reversing the orientation of handedness in a successor diagram the exact same relative orientation applies. The eastward successor a-segment is backed on its south side just as the old c prefix; the α-start b anomaly lies on its north free side.

Both collision types require a bank with at most k copies c. Each successor has that bound. More precisely, if n_i is a chosen short-run length on bank i and H events have been excluded, the new transverse prefix has d_{i+1}<n_i. Thus the bank two steps later has at most d_{i+1} c edges and n_{i+2}<n_i. A nonnegative strictly descending chain is impossible. All applications are to realized, finite banks, with no convergence argument. The argument proves that the alleged initial overhang is impossible.

At each late offset j=k+1,...,b−1 the no-overhang conclusion gives a free partition of (b−j)c≤kc, which must consist solely of c by the short-multiple lemma. The protected lower α scan now supplies every upright and each of the L−1 interior half-turn companions, since L=b−j≤k and all those joins are strict interior bank points. This is the earlier bottom-start scan in the offset proof and does not require the new artificial upper endpoint to serve as a stopping vertex. Induction yields the complete b-fold tile-similar corner, giving the impossible apex cevian of NO_CEVIAN.md.

**Dependencies.** This is a working geometric proof. It needs the earlier complete k+1 strips at the strongly protected apex, the I120 adaptation of exterior stopped runs and each local use with solid backing recorded above. Neither a complete Lean proof nor external refereeing is asserted.
