# Successive parallel banks at a pure leg

Fix k≥4, a=k(k−1), b=2k−1, c=k². We consider the isosceles triangle with equal legs bc, base b(b+c), filled by congruent (a,b,c) tiles. Its base angles are α; the angles of the tile opposite a,b,c are α,β,γ, respectively. All tiles may be reflected; every T-junction is allowed. We use the preceding boundary reduction in MIXED_BRANCH_INPUTS.md, and make explicit the one additional local version of the short-run induction below. The boundary reduction gives one pure or mixed leg at each corner. A mixed leg already forces a k-by-k standard corner patch, and hence k consecutive exterior b edges on the base at that corner. A pure leg consists of 2k−1 edges c with α facing the base corner. In the apex fan β,α,β, the α tile lies next to that leg tile. The total number of b edges on the base is k−1 or 2k−1.

The only outside-side counting input needed below can be recovered directly from the arithmetic of the inner banks. On each outer side the stopped-run bound gives ≥k c edges: at an endpoint where an α corner sits on c, the b-ray either borders β at the apex, or is the other exterior side for more than (k−1)b at a base corner. These are precisely the two initial-wall alternatives of the run argument. Apply the arithmetic in “Arithmetic forces the whole bank” below at j=0 to either leg of length bc. It gives only the all-c inventory or k copies each of a and c, both with zero b edges. Thus each base-corner α tile puts c along the leg. In the mixed inventory the run of length k uses all the c tiles and begins at that corner. Finally, if the base of length b(b+c) had more than b edges b, subtract b of them from its *length equation*. This leaves an inventory of length bc still containing ≥k edges c and at least one b, which the leg calculation forbids. This proves the exterior upper bound directly. No enumeration of the four complete base inventories is required.

The boundary prerequisites use only the outer three sides. The leg decomposition and stopped-run bound first force the pure/mixed alternatives. A mixed leg ends at the apex with β (its last a side cannot put γ there), and its k initial c sides force its **own** k-by-k corner. For a pure leg the base end faces α. Were the apex end also α, its neighboring apex β tile would protect its interior b-ray with a or c. The two protected α starts would force overlapping k-tile prefixes on a leg with 2k−1 tiles. Thus every pure leg ends with β. In both a pure/pure and a pure/mixed board, therefore, the two extreme apex tiles show β. The angle count at the apex gives the central α tile, whose b or c side starts on the internal a-ray of *each incident pure leg*. This seed is available before any interior strip is inferred. No base ordering, certificate for 517, interior seam, or alleged impossibility of a mixed board is a prerequisite.

**Pure-leg theorem.** A pure leg also forces k consecutive b edges on the base, starting at its corner. In particular neither combination of the two leg types can tile the triangle. This is a unit-scale result for the entire adjacent BQ family.

## Coordinates and the next bank

Put the base corner at O=0; take the horizontal vector B toward the opposite base corner with length b, and the vector C toward the apex with length c. Thus A=(2k−1)C is the apex. Write X(i,l)=iB+lC, U_j=X(j,0), V_j=X(j,2k−1−j), and S_j=[U_j,V_j]. One standard strip of index i consists of the upright tiles
(X(i,l),X(i+1,l),X(i,l+1)), 0≤l<2k−1−i, and the inverted tiles with vertices
(X(i+1,l),X(i,l+1),X(i+1,l+1)), 0≤l<2k−2−i. At l=0 the upright tile puts b on the exterior base. The first strip, i=0, is proved below by using the endpoint scans at zero offset. The pure-leg companion argument in PURE_ZERO.md gives the same strip.

Suppose strips 0 through j−1 are present, where 1≤j≤k−1. The exposed segment S_j is backed by exactly L=2k−1−j whole c edges. The line A V_j coincides with the diagonal direction B−C, and the completed strips give j contiguous whole edges a from A to V_j. The apex α tile puts a b or c side on the opposite bank, beginning exactly at A. Every decomposition of j a is all a for j<k. Thus an opposite-bank edge runs straight through V_j. This also precludes V_j lying on the exterior boundary. At U_j the exterior base continues well beyond the offsets used below. Neither a tile on S_j's free bank nor its side can overhang U_j downwards (exterior base) or V_j upwards (through edge, transverse to S_j). For precision, a side meeting the open S_j along an interval is collinear with S_j because the previously filled strip occupies the half-neighborhood on the other side at each such point. At V_j the through-edge on the apex line is transverse to S_j. An edge on the S_j line could extend over V_j only by crossing this through-edge in both their relative interiors. This is impossible for tiles with disjoint interiors. At U_j the exterior base supplies the same barrier. Thus the free bank partitions S_j into whole sides, including in the presence of T-junctions.

At U_j the free angle is α between the positive base ray and C. At V_j the last upright tile from strip j−1 contributes γ, the last inverted one contributes α, and the tile whose apex-line edge passes through V_j contributes a straight angle. The free angle is β, between −C and the continuing ray B−C. In particular a unique tile covers that β. All interior points on the opposite bank have an occupied half-neighborhood facing the completed strips. Consecutive edges may meet T-junctions on their open portions.

## A doubly stopped run

A maximal consecutive run of c edges is stopped at an endpoint if it is an endpoint of S_j or if the adjacent non-c tile along the bank presents γ there. Both endpoint sectors α and β are acute; no γ appears at either. The same elementary counting as in Beeson–Roşu, Lemma 34, now gives a doubly stopped run. For clarity, if there are u non-c and v c edges, the u non-c edges have u distinct γ endpoints among the u+v−1 interior joints, so v≥1. If there are p maximal c runs, an interior gap of d non-c tiles places d distinct γ among d+1 endpoints, hence hits at least one bordering c run. An end gap adjoining an actual endpoint cannot put γ there, so hits its bordering run. Including the convention about an endpoint when an end gap is empty, there are at least (p−1)+2=p+1 stops. Some run is stopped twice.

Such a run has length at least k−j. For one strictly inside S_j the original short-run proof gives the stronger lower bound k. Only a neighborhood of the run and a solid backing on the other side of S_j are required: every diagonal on the free side that hits the strict interior of S_j transversely is clamped by the backing. For a run starting at U_j and ending strictly inside S_j, if the initial c tile is present it necessarily shows α there; its b side is along the actual exterior base, which extends beyond (k−1)b. The exterior-wall α-start version gives the same lower bound k. A run equal to the entire segment already has L≥k. It remains to handle a run starting at V_j and ending strictly inside S_j. Its initial c tile shows β at V_j and its a-ray is the continuation of A V_j.

### Offset version of the short-run proof

For 1≤h≤k−1−j the β-start scan at V_j forces the terminal half-turn at the hth tile whenever the first h c tiles all face β towards V_j and their far joint is in the strict interior of S_j. This is the usual terminal-companion statement with the two acute labels interchanged; the only altered input is the initial wall. In the induction H(w), along this initial wall the known tiles run from A to V_j on j consecutive a sides, then continue from V_j towards P on w consecutive a sides in the same direction and on the same bank. The first side on the other bank starts exactly at A and is b or c. Since j+w≤k−1, purity of (j+w)a implies that P is interior to an edge of the other bank, and therefore there is a continuing wall beyond P. No tile is required to have a vertex on that other bank at V_j. This is precisely the conclusion needed from the starting wall by Beeson–Roşu, Lemma 33 (its paragraph “The line UP”). 

For the remaining steps of their Lemmas 32–33 the exposed diagonal opposite β-start has length w b or r b with w,r≤h≤k−1, hence pure b sides. The horizontal upper-bank anomaly would put an a in a decomposition of (s−r−i)c, with 1≤s−r−i≤h−1≤k−2, which is impossible. The separate first-deviation trapezoid inducts with those same length bounds and never uses the original offset wall. The upper diagonal endpoint is clamped by a through tile found as in the original proof. At every lower diagonal endpoint the prescribed backing across S_j prevents transverse overhang. When s=h or w=h in an argument by absence of the terminal half-turn, the lower endpoint is a strict interior joint on S_j. The exposed diagonal is transverse to S_j, and on its unused bank tiles cover the sector adjacent to it. From its upper endpoint, where an existing γ and the through edge leave α+β, purity forces successive half-turns down the diagonal, the last precisely the one assumed absent. This is the same terminal-substitution argument as in SHORT_GAPS.md; no lower endpoint at V_j is used. Nonterminal full-row and anomaly-extension steps keep the same proof as the two original lemmas. Acute sectors α,β are each indivisible, α+β has no γ, and no two γ fit into a half-plane.

Consequently a reversal before the kth-minus-j tile is impossible: after h≤k−1−j uniformly oriented c tiles, the compulsory terminal γ would have to fit between two outward α corners. The remaining angle π−2α=α+2β excludes γ=2α+β by independence of α and β. If instead a whole doubly stopped run had length h≤k−1−j, it would be uniform and the terminal γ would meet the non-c stop's γ on the same bank. Here the far terminal joint lies strictly inside S_j. Its known strip covers a whole half-neighborhood directly behind it, so the diagonal cannot extend through it. The descending terminal bank on the free side puts a γ at that joint. A non-c stop independently puts another γ there, and their tiles are distinct: the terminal half-turn meets S_j at the joint alone, whereas the stopping tile has a side along S_j. The two obtuse angles cannot fit the free half-plane. The proof starting at the upper endpoint therefore uses no stopping or support hypothesis beyond V_j. Thus the claimed bound k−j holds. For j=k−1 there is no scan needed: existence of at least one c already gives k−j=1.

## Arithmetic forces the whole bank

Write X,Y,Z for the numbers of a,b,c sides on S_j. Reduction modulo k gives Y=kt. Dividing the length equation by k, then reducing modulo k again, shows X+t=kq. Consequently

    2k−1−j=(k−1)q+t+Z.

Our run gives Z≥k−j. Therefore q≤1. If q=0 the bank is entirely c. If q=1 equality forces t=0, Z=k−j and X=k. The whole c count is exhausted by the doubly stopped run, so all the c tiles form that single block. At U_j only α fits. The first edge cannot be a because the incident angles of an a edge are β,γ; no b is present. Thus the single c block starts at U_j and ends strictly inside S_j. The bottom-start lower bound k applies, contradicting k−j<k. Only the all-c bank survives.

## Closing the induction

There are L=2k−1−j evenly spaced c tiles on S_j. From U_j the α-start scan with the true exterior b-wall forces the first k−1 terminal inverses, each at a strict interior joint of S_j (L≥k), and forces the first k tiles to face α towards U_j. From V_j the offset β-start scan forces the last k−1−j inverses and the last k−j tiles to face β toward V_j. The two orientation lists agree on their common tile; they contain all L tiles. Their inverse positions cover all (k−1)+(k−1−j)=L−1 distinct interior joints. In these uniformly oriented positions each required half-turn from either end is exactly the displayed inverted tile X(i+1,l),X(i,l+1),X(i+1,l+1) with i=j. From the U_j end it is the inverse across the preceding a edge; from the V_j end the same tile is the inverse across the next b edge. The shell j is complete.

For j=0 the length and orientations are already given by the input pure-leg result. The external lower wall and the genuine ambient apex α neighbor (offset zero) supply k−1 companions from each end by precisely these same scans, filling the first shell. Induction now gives shell 0 through k−1, so the k consecutive tiles on the exterior base from O put sides b there. The base has length b(b+c)>2kb, hence at the two distinct base corners the compulsory b-prefixes do not overlap. This conflicts with the boundary inventory bound of at most 2k−1 b sides.

## Inputs and scope

The imported arithmetic purity used above is elementary. A decomposition of nb, n<k, is all b, by reducing modulo k and nonnegativity. If na=Xa+Yb+Zc, then Y=kt and t+Z is divisible by k−1. A positive t+Z makes the part t(2k−1)+kZ at least k(k−1)>n(k−1), so na is pure. Similarly nc=Xa+ktb+Zc forces X+t to be a multiple of k; when n<k−1, a positive X+t makes X(k−1)+t(2k−1)≥k(k−1)>nk. Thus nc is pure for n≤k−2. At n=k−1 the only additional decomposition is (k−1)c=ka, which contains no b. All offset and endpoint arguments concern whole tile edges on one bank; partial contacts on the opposite bank are allowed.

The reference for the short-run induction is Beeson–Roşu, “Tiling a triangle into a prime number of congruent triangles”, arXiv:2607.23453v4, §11, Lemmas 30, 32–34, especially the proof of H(w) in Lemma 33. The offset modification and the successive use of the inner banks are the arguments in this note. This is a manuscript argument, not a Lean certificate.

For the separate small parameters, the geometric boards are (12,12,21) and (45,45,70). Bonfioli's repository already gives hypothesis-free exclusions of their respective tile counts in `Erdos634.Draft.SIXF21.chain_21_final'` and `Erdos634.Draft.AllN2.not_tile_70`, in `lean/Erdos634/Draft/SIXF21.lean` and `lean/Erdos634/Draft/N70.lean`. Combining these prior results with the present k≥4 proof covers every adjacent pair of positive coprime parameters. The manuscript argument above does not depend on either small-parameter certificate.
