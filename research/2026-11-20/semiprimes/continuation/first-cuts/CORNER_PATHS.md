# Arbitrarily bent paths between the three board corners

Use the [corner-ray normalization and integer-offset lemma](CORNER_RAYS.md). Write $O=0$, $B=t bq$, $C=t ap\rho$, where $p=a+2b,\ q=2a+b,\ c^2=a^2+ab+b^2$, and $t$ is the positive integer dilation. Set $w=a+b\rho$ and $z=w/c$. A direction of height $h$ is $\rho^j z^h$, up to reversing its sign. Here $z=e^{i\beta}$ and $\rho=e^{i\pi/3}$. To see that these heights cover every edge, start with a tile on the horizontal board side. Within one tile all directed edge changes lie in the group generated by $\rho,z$, since its angles are $\beta,\pi/3-\beta,2\pi/3$. Across a positive-length tile contact, the shared supporting line transfers a known direction up to reversal; reversal multiplies it by $\rho^3$. Every tile is reachable this way: join interior points by a polygonal arc in the interior of the board, perturbed to avoid the finitely many tile vertices and be transverse to every crossed edge. Thus T-junctions cause no failure of the propagation. The height is unique because $\beta/\pi$ is irrational, as proved in the corner-ray result.

**Theorem.** If $c\nmid t$, then in every ordinary tiling the following statements hold.

* No path from $O$ to $B$ or $C$ along tile edges has all its segments at strictly positive heights.
* No path between any two distinct outer corners along tile edges has all its segments at strictly negative heights.

Here a path means a finite connected polygonal walk lying in the union of tile sides, with changes of direction allowed wherever those sides meet, including T-junctions. It can be taken to change direction only at a change of supporting line: erase any immediate retracing along a single line, splitting a longer path if needed. The statements allow unrestricted orientations elsewhere in the tiling. They imply in particular that every edge path from $O$ to either other corner encounters a nonpositive height and a nonnegative height. A height-zero edge alone satisfies both required signs.

**Proof.** On each straight run between actual turns, both endpoints lie in the same component of the line union. At a genuine turn both incident lines have a tile-side endpoint by the two-bank argument in the corner-ray proof. A board corner is also a tile-side endpoint for every incident line of the path. Therefore the net displacement on any such run is an integer multiple of its unit direction. Consecutive collinear path segments, including backtracking steps, may be replaced by their net displacement since only the start and end enter this calculation.

Work with $\mathbb Z[\rho]$, where $\rho^2-\rho+1=0$ and $\bar w=a+b-b\rho$. Since $w\bar w=c^2$, we have $z=c/\bar w$ and $z^{-1}=c/w$. The integer $b$ is a unit modulo $c$. The substitution
$$
 \rho\longmapsto -a/b\pmod c
$$
respects $\rho^2-\rho+1=0$ by the norm equation. It takes $\bar w$ to $2a+b=q$, a unit modulo $c$. It therefore defines a homomorphism $\mathbb Z[\rho,1/\bar w]\to\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z$ taking $z$ to zero. Every positive-height displacement maps to zero. The corner images are $0,tbq,tbq$; equality of the last two uses $a^2p+b^2q=(a+b)c^2$. Since $bq$ is a unit, a positive-height walk from $O$ cannot reach the other corners if $c\nmid t$.

For negative heights use the conjugate substitution $\rho\mapsto(a+b)/b\bmod c$. This takes $w$ to the unit $q$, so it extends to $\mathbb Z[\rho,1/w]$ and kills $z^{-1}$. The values of $B$, $C$ and $C-B$ are now, respectively, the following units multiplied by $t$:
$$
 bq,\qquad ap(a+b)/b,\qquad 3a^2\pmod c .
$$
The last expression uses $a^3-b^3=(a-b)c^2$. For the second, $\gcd(c,a+b)=1$ because $(a+b)^2=c^2+ab$ and $\gcd(c,ab)=1$. The remaining coprimalities are in the corner-ray proof. All three differences are nonzero when $c\nmid t$, including when $c$ is composite. This proves the second assertion.

The theorem applies to paths with arbitrarily many internal turns. It gives no sufficiency claim about a path using both height signs or containing a height-zero edge. Nor does it rule out a tile chain whose junctions only share points and whose path passes through tile interiors.
