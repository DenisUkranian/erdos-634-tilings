# Independent audit of the global integer endpoint model

20 November 2026. Audited `global_integer/global_core_sat.py`, including the
single active tile-budget scope, empty-core confirmation, and optional exact
point-coverage constraints. No global nonexistence certificate has been
obtained by this audit.

## The projection theorem

Fix any finite set S of endpoint rows. A row is a point P together with an
unoriented line direction. Take every admissible placement of the fixed
scalene tile that has a side endpoint at P in that direction, for every row
in S. Also include every possible tile having an exterior edge, and any
other finite set of seed placements. Give each distinct placement one
Boolean variable.

The permanent necessary model consists of:

* Exact endpoint-current equality at every row of S.
* Exact onefold coverage of every exterior unit interval.
* At most N selected placements, where N is the exact area ratio.
* Optional exclusions of geometric overlaps, including the implemented
  shared-wedge groups and strict point-coverage constraints.

Every actual tiling of the complete board induces a solution of this
finite model: set a variable to one precisely when its placement occurs
in the tiling. A tile omitted from the catalogue cannot contribute to a
row in S, because that row's incident catalogue is complete. Omitted tiles
also cannot contribute to an exterior interval, because the entire
exterior catalogue is included. Omitting interior tiles can only decrease
the tile-count and point-coverage upper bounds. Thus UNSAT of this model
is an unrestricted obstruction, even if most interior placements have
never been generated.

This is a projection of a hypothetical full tiling, not an assumption that
the selected catalogue itself contains a complete tiling. It imposes no
height, denominator, translation-phase, or search-radius restriction on
the hypothetical tiling.

## Why the incident catalogue is complete

Specify one endpoint P and the unoriented axis of one tile side. Choose
the side's length (three choices), the length of the other side at P
(two), the direction along the axis (two), and the half-plane containing
the third vertex (two). These 24 choices give all metric possibilities.
The engine's inverse templates produce exactly these placements.

Consequently arbitrary real rotations are covered whenever they can
contribute to the specified row. It is unnecessary to assert a priori
that every tile of a hypothetical tiling belongs to a fixed finite
orientation palette. Tiles avoiding all selected endpoint rows contribute
zero there. An edge passing through P in its relative interior contributes
zero at P: subdivision at P introduces two cancelling endpoint terms.
This is why T-junctions do not invalidate the construction.

For these scalene primitive norm tiles, the obtuse vertex and the two
different short lengths determine a unique placement key. The angle of
z=(a+b rho)/c is not a root-of-unity angle: the only roots of unity in the
quadratic Eisenstein field have multiples of 60 degrees, while a>b>0
puts arg(z) strictly between zero and 30 degrees. Therefore the height and
phase keys do not duplicate the same geometric placement.

## Exterior completeness and boundary arithmetic

A contained triangle can cover a positive interval of an exterior side
only by one full edge. Every such edge has length a, b or c. The outside
edge partition therefore has integer offsets, with both distances to the
side's corners in the nonnegative semigroup generated by a,b,c.

Every tile vertex on a straight exterior side is an endpoint of that
partition. Otherwise another tile edge would pass through it and occupy
the entire available inward half-neighborhood, overlapping the first
tile. This justifies applying the arithmetic restrictions to all boundary
vertices of a proposed tile, not only to its boundary-edge endpoints.

The engine enumerates every eligible integral offset on each exterior
side and every one of the 24 incident metric placements. It retains each
placement having a full edge along that side, once at its smaller offset.
Exact unit-interval equations are then equivalent to full boundary
coverage because all these endpoints are integral. Later row expansion
cannot discover an omitted admissible exterior placement.

In axial coordinates the board is (0,0), (A,0), (0,B), where
A=b(2a+b), B=a(a+2b). The third side has length c² and unit direction
rho z²=(-A+B rho)/c². The implemented side coordinates and denominators
agree with this identity.

## Exclusions and the tile budget

At a shared side endpoint, each tile determines a ray along the axis and
a half-plane containing its interior. Two tiles with the same choices
have intersecting open sectors near the endpoint. They overlap in
positive area, so at most one can be selected. This proves the four
shared-wedge group constraints without an edge-to-edge assumption.

For a fixed rational point, at most one selected tile can contain it
strictly. The point-cut block uses exact common-denominator determinants;
points on edges are excluded. A partial catalogue suffices for this upper
bound, because omitted variables have nonnegative coverage coefficients.

The global cap must be <=N on the selected variables. An equality N would
be unsound for this projected necessary model: some actual interior tiles
can be omitted. The current code correctly keeps <=N. Its latest
implementation pushes just the current cap before each solver query and
pops it before adding columns. This is equivalent to all earlier subset
caps, which the latest cap implies. A final UNSAT confirmation and model
export retain the active current cap.

## Temporary rows and interpretation of solver answers

Rows outside S are enforced only under fresh assumption guards. Their
equations include the currently available columns and can be incomplete.
UNSAT with any such guard in its core proves nothing about unrestricted
tilings; it merely identifies rows whose incident catalogues should be
completed. Installing a row enumerates all its possible placements and
makes its equality permanent. No future placement can be missing from
this complete row.

Old guards remain as implications but are not assumed on later queries.
They can all be false and impose no restriction. The latest implementation
rejects malformed/error core output. An empty temporary core additionally
triggers `check-sat` with no assumptions and requires a literal `unsat`
before an obstruction is exported. This correctly distinguishes global
permanent constraints from temporary search restrictions.

Conversely SAT under all current guards provides a finite positive
endpoint identity, because every row arising from any selected tile has
either its complete equation or its latest guarded equation. The output
routine recomputes every endpoint balance and checks N distinct contained
tiles. For a final existence result, independently verify the exported
metric and currents. A finite positive polygon-current identity with the
outer boundary implies onefold coverage almost everywhere: on each
supporting line the zero endpoint derivative makes the compactly
supported edge current zero, and the resulting polygon-boundary identity
makes the indicator sum equal to the board's indicator.

A timeout, column limit, memory kill, guarded UNSAT, or unconfirmed empty
core is inconclusive. A solver-generated UNSAT proof still needs replay
and an encoding audit; this source audit is not itself such a proof.

## Independent executable controls

Run `python3 check_global_integer.py` in this directory. It checks:

* 305 exact incident catalogues, on all three triples (5,3,7), (33,7,37),
  (35,13,43), against a separate metric construction using rational
  projection and perpendicular altitude; actual polygon endpoint
  currents agree with all engine templates up to the known sign.
* 1,620 same-wedge pairs have positive-area intersection under exact
  polygon clipping.
* The exported 143-tile positive control has 143 distinct correct metric
  triangles, exact area, zero endpoint residual, onefold coverage of
  every exterior unit interval, and no overlap among 361 pairs whose
  bounding boxes permit intersection.
* 382 truth-table cases for the pseudo-Boolean equality and at-most
  encodings, including impossible and empty bounds.

All controls passed. No unsafe catalogue omission or mathematical
restriction was found in the current permanent model.

## Audit of the shared-guard and context-rebuild revision

The v3 implementation canonicalizes a zero-target equation by exchanging
its positive and negative incidence lists when this gives the smaller
key. Multiplying a zero equality by minus one preserves it. Rows with
literally identical canonical Boolean equations may safely share one
assumption guard. Unbinding a row retains the guard while any other row
still references it; expanding an UNSAT core selects the union of its
associated physical rows. Inactive implications remain optional.

Incidence lists contain no duplicates: each placement is inserted once,
each subsequent update processes exactly the newly inserted index range,
and a nondegenerate triangle has six distinct point/axis endpoint rows.

A rebuild discards the old solver context, regenerates every tile
declaration and every permanent clause, and regenerates precisely the
latest active guard implications. This also preserves changes that were
queued but not yet sent to the old context: the tile, clause and guard
collections already include them. The pending old budget pop is discarded
with the old context, and the loop installs the fresh current <=N cap.
No permanent restriction or required incident placement is lost.

The executable control additionally creates two physical rows sharing
one equation, changes only one row by adding a variable, removes the last
reference to the old shared guard, and rebuilds while the new declaration,
guard and permanent constraint are still pending. The expected satisfying
assignment survives exactly; assuming the original shared constraint also
correctly changes a deliberately inconsistent assignment from SAT to
UNSAT. This focused revision control passed.
