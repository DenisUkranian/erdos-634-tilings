# Prescribing prime divisors of actual squarefree tile counts

Let S(X) be the number of squarefree tile counts up to X in the [split sector](SPLIT_SECTOR.md): they are 23 modulo 24 and all their prime divisors are 1 or 11 modulo 12. For any two disjoint finite sets A and B of primes that are 1 or 11 modulo 12, the following limit exists and is strictly positive:
$$
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{n\text{ counted by } S(X):\ \ell\mid n\ (\ell\in A),\ \ell\nmid n\ (\ell\in B)\}}{S(X)}
=\prod_{\ell\in A}w_\ell\prod_{\ell\in B}(1-w_\ell),
\qquad w_\ell=\frac{4(\ell-1)}{\ell^2+\ell-4}.
$$
For example, the limiting proportion divisible by 11 is exactly 5/16. This describes actual positive counts for the original problem. Its inputs are the preceding [squarefree value theorem](F2_MASS.md) and [reduction of the sector](SPLIT_SECTOR.md).

For the polynomial H in the squarefree theorem, both quadratic factors have two simple projective roots modulo each such prime. The four roots are distinct and all lie in the affine unit chart. There are 4(ℓ−1) nonzero points on these lines modulo ℓ. Of the ℓ² lifts of each point, exactly ℓ vanish modulo ℓ². Each of the ℓ² lifts of the origin vanishes modulo ℓ² as well. Consequently the number of allowed classes is ℓ⁴−ℓ²−4ℓ(ℓ−1). Exactly 4ℓ(ℓ−1)² of them give valuation one. The ratio simplifies to the displayed weight. The Chinese remainder theorem imposes any fixed list of local conditions. The large-square tail and the estimate on repeated quartic values in the squarefree theorem remain valid on each such subset, being bounded by their totals. Both the unconstructed F2 counts and the contribution of other tiling shapes in this sector are o(√X). Thus the same relative limits hold for genuine tile counts.

One can prescribe the factor as well. On a staircase board with a>b, call a+2b the smaller factor and 2a+b the larger. Each set of two roots contributes wℓ/2. The two cone sectors in the quartic parametrization may interchange the factors, but have exactly the same local weights in either order. Counting across them is valid because the number of values with repeated positive-cone representations is o(√X). For disjoint finite lists of primes assigned to the smaller factor, the larger factor, or neither, the corresponding product of weights uses wℓ/2, wℓ/2 and 1−wℓ. In particular infinitely many genuine scale-one tilings realize each such prescription.

For every fixed r, a proportion tending to one of the integers counted by S(X) have constructive F2 representations in which **each** factor has more than r distinct prime divisors. Indeed for any fixed finite set of split primes, the indicators of divisibility of the smaller factor have the joint law of independent Bernoulli variables in the limit. Their total mean is the sum of wℓ/2 and the variance is at most that sum. The means grow without bound as the finite set expands, by divergence of the reciprocal prime sum in either split progression. Chebyshev's inequality bounds the limiting upper proportion with at most r divisors by M/(M−r)² for M>r. Apply this first with a fixed prime set, then let M tend to infinity, and repeat for the larger factor. This also explains why a positive-density statement for these tile counts cannot on its own establish infinitely many prime pairs.
