# Vertex lattices with non-edge-to-edge contacts

Consider a finite tiling of a convex polygon by nondegenerate triangles.
Join two tiles when *both* have the same point as a corner. This graph
is connected, including with arbitrary T-junctions. To see this, take
any positive-length contact of two tiles. On its supporting line take
the maximal connected closed segment made of tile sides containing the
contact. Each bank partitions this segment into whole tile sides. Away
from the finite set of corners, a tile side along an interior line must
have a tile side opposite; convexity separates interior lines from
exterior lines. At either end no collinear side can extend farther.
Consecutive tiles on one bank therefore share a corner, and the banks
share their first corner. Thus every pair with positive-length contact
is in the same corner-connected component. Positive-length contacts
connect all tiles, as follows by joining interior points by a polygonal
path in the polygon that avoids its finite set of tile corners.

In the primitive odd unit F2 setting, write $z=\lambda/\eta$ in lowest
Eisenstein terms, $N(\lambda)=N(\eta)=c$, and $R=\mathbb Z[\rho]$.
If all short heights are in $[l,r]$ and $D=r-l$, *all tile vertices*
are in the single positioned lattice
$$
   z^l\eta^{-D}R .
$$
In fact all short edge vectors belong to this lattice, since every
$z^{l+t}R$ for $0\le t\le D$ does. A long edge is the sum of its two
directed short vectors, regardless of hand. The graph lemma propagates
membership from the exterior vertex $O=0$ to all vertices. The
lattice is also the group generated by the *available* short vectors:
$a,b$ are coprime; after division by $z^l$ and multiplication by
$\eta^D$ the generated ideal contains $\lambda^D$ and $\eta^D$, which
are coprime. Its fundamental area is $\sqrt3/(2c^D)$ and its mesh,
measured by the shortest nonzero vector, is $c^{-D/2}$.

Explicit generators follow by squaring $u+v\rho$ in the usual
primitive parameterization. Let $e=1$ for $g=1$ and $e=1+\rho$ for
$g=3$. Then
$$
 \lambda=(u+v\rho)/e,\qquad\eta=(u+v-v\rho)/e .
$$
Both belong to $R$ (when $g=3$, $u\equiv v\pmod3$) and have norm $c$.
The index calculation of [TWO_PATHS.md](TWO_PATHS.md) proves they are
in lowest terms.

## Size of an exact placement catalogue

Write $j=N\bmod c$. The full-board results give height 2 and at most
$H=1+\lfloor(N-j-c)/(2c)\rfloor$ occupied heights, with at most one
vacancy inside their span. Hence $D\le H$. For each of the $O(H^2)$
possible intervals with $l\le2\le r$, $D\le H$, discard the interval
if its lattice misses an outer corner. Enumerate its lattice points in
the board and attach at each point the at most $12(D+1)$ placements
with the obtuse corner there. Keep tiles contained in the board and
connect two placements when their interiors are disjoint. A clique with
$N$ placements is equivalent to a tiling for that interval: all
placements have the required area, their closed union is contained
in the board and a proper closed union would leave positive relative
area. This tests the unit board including all reflected orientations
and T-junctions.

The board has diameter $O(c^2)$ and each grid is triangular of mesh
$c^{-D/2}$, hence it has $O(c^{D+4})$ points by disjoint disk packing.
Per interval the catalogue has $O((D+1)c^{D+4})$ tiles. The entire
catalogue is of size $\exp(O(\sqrt N\log N))$ since $N\asymp c^2$.
This is a bound on the number of individual candidate placements;
finding a clique can take exponentially longer. A positive certificate
lists $N$ lattice coordinates and orientations, each of
$O(\sqrt N\log N)$ bits, verifiable by elementary exact arithmetic
in time polynomial in $N$.

For the two-height case $[1,2]$ the bound is polynomial: $O(N^{5/2})$
candidates before the clique search. The number of potential anchor
*points*, prior to tile-containment tests, is exactly
$$
    1+(cNab+c+ap+bq)/2.
$$
Indeed the sublattice on each height-2 side has primitive step of
physical length one, and on the height-0 base the primitive step is
$c$ by the lattice tests in [TWO_PATHS.md](TWO_PATHS.md), with its
$cz$ frame rescaled by $c$. Hence the lattice boundary length is
$c+ap+bq$. Divide the board area $Nab\sqrt3/4$ by cell area
$\sqrt3/(2c)$ and use Pick's theorem. The count gives 14,663,429
possible anchor points for the 3431 board and 49,530,427 for the 5063
board before containment. It does not assert an actual tiling.
