# An obstruction to position-independent central calibrations

For both unit F2 boards, no bound obtained by assigning direction generators to a simply connected real nilpotent Lie group with central tile relator values, and applying a linear functional to their central logarithms, can show that positive tiles cannot fill the board. This includes the Heisenberg group and groups of any higher nilpotency class. It leaves open the minimum area of an actual van Kampen diagram.

## The representation modulo central relators

Write \(x_{h,j+3}=x_{h,j}^{-1}\). The tile relators compare the paths \(x_{h,j}^a x_{h,j+s}^b\) and \(x_{h+s,j}^c\), for both \(s=1,-1\). A half-turn gives the analogous equality for the powers with both exponents negated. If the relator values in a simply connected real nilpotent Lie group are central, pass to the quotient by its center, again a simply connected real nilpotent Lie group. In this quotient the two paths agree. Comparing the inverse of one equality with the half-turned one shows that \(x_{h,j}^a\) and \(x_{h,j+s}^b\) commute. In such a Lie group nonzero powers commute only if the elements commute. Indeed, if \(\exp(aX)\) commutes with \(\exp(bY)\), injectivity of the exponential map yields \(\exp(a\,\mathrm{ad} X)Y=Y\). As \(\mathrm{ad} X\) is nilpotent, \(\exp(a\,\mathrm{ad} X)-I\) is \(\mathrm{ad} X\) times an invertible polynomial, giving \([X,Y]=0\).

The three unoriented axes at any fixed height therefore commute. The relations put every \(x_{h+s,j}\) in the connected abelian subgroup generated by that layer, since it is the unique \(c\)-th root of a product in the subgroup. This propagates to every height. Writing the logarithms there as \(v_{h,j}\), they satisfy
\[
c v_{h+1,j}=a v_{h,j}+b v_{h,j+1},\qquad c v_{h-1,j}=a v_{h,j}+b v_{h,j-1}.
\]
Applying the two recurrences successively and using \(c^2=a^2+ab+b^2\) gives \(v_{h,j}=v_{h,j+1}+v_{h,j-1}\). The vectors are determined by \(v_{0,0},v_{0,1}\), and the physical unit direction vectors follow the identical recurrence. Hence the layer logarithm is a linear image of the physical unit displacement.

Back in the original group, all commutators of generator logarithms are central. Choose linear lifts of the two basic vectors. The logarithm of a generator is therefore the lifted planar displacement plus a central direction correction \(d_{h,j}\). For any physically closed polygonal word the class-two Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula gives its central logarithm as a fixed vector times the signed Euclidean area, plus the additive sum of the direction corrections weighted by edge length. This also holds when the two basic vectors are dependent. Higher nilpotent terms vanish on this subgroup.

## Positive rational populations

For any nonnegative real-linear inequality on positive tile relator values, the same inequality holds for every nonnegative rational combination of positive tiles. There is such a combination of total mass exactly \(N\) having the full board's signed directional lengths, so all of these inequalities are compatible with the board's area and boundary.

Use a type \((h,j,s)\) for the oriented directional current \(a x_{h,j}+b x_{h,j+s}-c x_{h+s,j}\). Every type occurs as the current of a counterclockwise tile. For \(s=-1\), take a half-turn of the clockwise geometric triangle and reverse the traversal to recover the stated current. The first six types and their masses are

| \((h,j,s)\) | 3431 | 5063 |
|---|---:|---:|
| \((1,1,-1)\) | 1551/37 | 2135/43 |
| \((1,3,-1)\) | 511/37 | 1079/43 |
| \((1,2,1)\) | 637/740 | 1859/1032 |
| \((1,4,1)\) | 13223/740 | 30901/1032 |
| \((2,1,-1)\) | 429/20 | 385/24 |
| \((2,3,-1)\) | 91/20 | 143/24 |

These give the full exterior current, with total masses \(3717/37\) and \(5525/43\), respectively. Add any positive orientation and its half-turn with equal weights to bring the mass to \(N\). Their directional currents cancel. All positive tiles have the same geometric area, so the resulting formal population also gives the board's area. ## A separate restriction on the single free-product cover

Consider the ordered-word cover with cyclic factors of order \(a\) used in the supported-cycle theorem. In the frame \(O=0, B=bq, C=ap\rho\), the three sides of a board scaled by \(m\) trace unit labels along the first cyclic factor, its inverse, then an inverse of the second factor. The first two lengths differ by \(m(bq-c^2)=-ma(a-b)\), returning the path to the identity. The last side length is divisible by \(a\). As a signed chain in this Cayley graph the full boundary is exactly \(m\) times the scale-one chain. For any 1-cochain on that graph, even depending on the starting group vertex, its boundary integral scales linearly in \(m\). Since a positive tiling exists at some larger integer scale, a 1-cochain nonnegative around every translated positive face cannot have a negative integral on the unit boundary. The same argument works separately on the order-\(b\) cover, where the first side contributes a full set of cyclic turns and the next two use one other factor. This last assertion concerns sign separation on one cover. It gives no analogous scaling claim on the product of the two covers or on a cover augmented by physical coordinates.
