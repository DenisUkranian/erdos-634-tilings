# Connectivity of tile corners on supporting lines

Let a nondegenerate closed triangle be tiled by finitely many nondegenerate closed triangles with disjoint interiors. Congruence is unnecessary. Make a graph whose vertices are the tiles and one additional copy of the outer triangle. Join two vertices precisely when the corresponding triangles have a common geometric vertex and each has a side on the same line through that vertex. The sides may extend in opposite directions from the vertex. A T-junction alone creates no such edge to the tile whose side contains the junction in its relative interior.

**Theorem.** Deleting any one vertex of the graph leaves a connected graph. Deleting any two vertices corresponding to triangles with disjoint geometric vertex sets also leaves a connected graph whenever vertices remain. In particular, any two-vertex separator corresponds to triangles that share a geometric vertex.

Orient tiles counterclockwise and the outer triangle clockwise. On every physical supporting line, add the oriented side indicators with respect to an arbitrary positive coordinate on that line. The result is zero away from endpoints: on any open subinterval between consecutive vertices, the two incident interior sides cancel, or the tile side cancels the exterior. The distributional derivative on the line is therefore zero. Equivalently the signed counts of side starts and ends agree at each point of the line.

After deleting some triangles, take a component of the remaining graph. At any point and line, all surviving triangles contributing a signed endpoint there lie in a single component. Thus the endpoint sum of the chosen component on that line can be nonzero only at endpoints of deleted sides lying on that same line. Call its sum of oriented sides the component boundary. It is a closed 1-chain, since it is the sum of triangle boundaries.

For one deleted triangle, the component boundary on each of its three supporting lines is a scalar multiple of its oriented side: a compactly supported step function whose derivative is supported at the two side endpoints is constant between them and zero outside. On every other line it vanishes. Conservation at the three vertices forces all three scalars to agree, with the sides oriented around the triangle. The component boundary is an integer multiple of the deleted boundary.

For two deleted triangles with disjoint vertex sets, they have at most one common supporting line. Indeed two distinct common lines intersect at a vertex of each triangle. If there is no common line, the preceding argument yields one multiple for each deleted triangle, because their vertices are disjoint. If there is a common line, use the two private sides of each triangle first. Conservation at their common vertex gives the same scalar on both: it is off the common line and is not a vertex of the other triangle. Lines of the other triangle that pass through the point in their relative interior have no endpoint there. Subtract these two multiples of whole triangle boundaries. The result is a compactly supported closed 1-chain on the single common line and hence vanishes.

In all cases, therefore, the signed indicator of the component is the same linear combination of deleted triangle indicators almost everywhere. To see this, subtract the two sides. The difference is constant on each open face of the finite arrangement of the supporting lines. Its jump across every edge is zero by boundary equality, and its value in the unbounded face is zero.

If the outer triangle survives, a component excluding it would be a nonempty union of tiles with interior disjoint from every deleted triangle, an impossibility. If the outer triangle was deleted, on the interiors of surviving tiles the indicator of any component is constant, equal to the coefficient of the outer triangle. A nonempty component therefore contains every remaining tile. With no deletions, a component excluding the board would have zero boundary and positive area. Thus the original graph is connected as well.

**Consequence for an exact search.** For at least two tiles, any tile can be reached from the board by a path of length at most the integer part of half the total number of graph vertices. Indeed there are two internally vertex-disjoint paths from the board to the tile, by the two-vertex form of Menger's theorem, and their lengths add to at most the number of vertices. For neighboring endpoints the distance bound is immediate. Each step pins one tile vertex to an already determined vertex and one of its incident sides to an already determined line. Given a tile's side lengths, only finitely many rigid placements satisfy those conditions, reflections included. If a finite orientation interval is known, all vertices belong to the single affine lattice or additive module generated by the board vertex differences and the allowed whole tile edge vectors. The statements apply to actual tilings, irrespective of whether their tile edges contain full straight chords. They place no sufficiency claim on local corner compatibility.
