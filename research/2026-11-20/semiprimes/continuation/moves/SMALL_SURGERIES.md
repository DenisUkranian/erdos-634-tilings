# Small exact replacements preserve short-side directions

Let the tile have primitive odd sides $a>b>0$, $c^2=a^2+ab+b^2$. Write $\rho=e^{i\pi/3}$, $z=(a+b\rho)/c$, $R=\mathbb Z[\rho]$. Describe all directions in a fixed orientation frame as $\rho^j z^h$, with the short sides of a tile at the same height $h$ and the long side at $h+1$ or $h-1$. Two tilings of the same triangle use a common frame by propagation from a common boundary side along positive-length contacts. Finite unions, disconnected changes and T-junctions are allowed. In comparing the tilings, delete every placement common to both. Each side then has $m$ changed tiles. The two finite lists have the same oriented boundary as geometric chains, including subdivisions.

**Replacement theorem.** At every height, the numbers of tiles with each of the two boundary-character signs are separately invariant modulo $c$. If $m<c$, all these counts are exactly the same. If $m<2c$, the sets of short-side heights among the changed tiles are exactly the same. At this budget the only possible nonzero change of the signed histogram transfers exactly $c$ tiles of a single sign between two already occupied heights of the same parity. This describes necessary conditions; realization of any proposed move needs its own proof.

**Area congruence for the difference.** For an integer $k$ take each tile's oriented boundary multiplied by the indicator of short height above $k$, and subtract just the tile sides above $k$. The result is zero or one directed whole long edge, in direction height $k$ or $k+1$. Form this signed sum for the new tiles minus the old tiles. The high sides subtracted from the two complete boundaries agree: internal contacts cancel on each bank, and exterior boundaries agree after subdividing. Thus the difference of the two high-region boundaries is a closed directed chain of whole long edges. It decomposes into closed walks, including walks of negative signed area. Each walk has all vertices in a translate of $z^k(cR+(a+b\rho)R)$.

The ideal $I=(c,a+b\rho)$ has index $c$ in $R$: modulo $c$, evaluation at $\rho=-a/b$ respects $\rho^2-\rho+1=0$ by the norm equation, and imposing $a+b\rho=0$ gives precisely $\mathbb Z/c$. Each closed walk in a translate of this index-$c$ lattice has signed area an integral multiple of $c\sqrt3/4$, by the determinant area formula. Each tile has area $ab\sqrt3/4$, with $\gcd(ab,c)=1$. It follows that the cumulative number of changed tiles above $k$ has difference divisible by $c$, for all $k$. Taking successive differences gives $c\mid\Delta n_h$ at every height.

**Signed congruence.** Weight a directed unit of edge by $(-1)^j$. The two directed short sides of a counterclockwise tile have opposite characters, so their total weighted length is $(a-b)\epsilon$ with $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$. Its long side has weight $-c\epsilon$. For the weight $(-1)^{j+h}$ the full tile contributes $(a-b+c)(-1)^h\epsilon$. All weights are additive under edge subdivision. At each fixed height the new-minus-old total weighted boundary is zero, and whole long edges contribute multiples of $c$. Thus $c\mid(a-b)\Delta G_h$, where $G_h=\sum\epsilon$. The norm and primitivity give $\gcd(c,a-b)=1$: a common prime would divide $3b^2$, and $3\mid c$ would give $a\equiv b\pmod3$, hence $a^2+ab+b^2\equiv3b^2\pmod9$, impossible. Consequently $c\mid\Delta G_h$. As $c$ is odd, $(\Delta n_h\pm\Delta G_h)/2$ are both multiples of $c$. If $m<2c$, the sum of the positive changes in these two-sign classes is less than $2c$. Any nonzero change must therefore be one gain and one loss of $c$. The total of each full boundary character is unchanged, so $\sum_hG_h$ and $\sum_h(-1)^hG_h$ are both unchanged. The transfer preserves the sign and the parity of its height.

**No new short height at the smaller budget.** Suppose the old difference list has no short sides at $h$ and the new list has some. If $m<2c$, exactly $c$ new tiles have that height, and $G_h$ is an odd multiple of $c$ between $-c$ and $c$. All have the same sign. On every supporting line of their short sides, denote by $M,L$ the numbers of their $a,b$ sides. The two tilings have the same signed length on that line, and all other contributions are whole long edges. Hence $(c-a)M+bL\equiv0\pmod c$. Across the $r$ used lines, $\sum M=\sum L=c$. Each $M,L\le r-1$: the other short side meets the given one at the obtuse vertex; naming its line fixes their intersection, and for a fixed common sign only one placement can use this ordered pair of lines. Distinct tiles cannot occupy that same placement. These are exactly the incidence conditions ruled out in the two parity cases of the [line-count proof](../../LINE_COUNTS.md), in the section **A bound from intersecting lines**. That proof requires only the displayed conditions and the primitive odd norm, so it applies to the signed comparison even when the common exterior has arbitrarily directed sides. Reversing old and new also prevents deletion.

For $(33,7,37)$ and $(35,13,43)$ a repair between two genuine fillings which introduces a short height absent among the removed tiles requires at least 74 and 86 removals, respectively. Comparisons with incomplete packings require extra boundary terms and are outside this inference.
