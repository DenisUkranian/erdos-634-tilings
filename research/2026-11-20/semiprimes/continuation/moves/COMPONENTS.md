# A spatial cost for branching into high directions

Use a common axial frame for the [unit F2 board](../../FULL_BOARD.md) with outer edge heights 0 and 2. The two short sides of a tile have a common height and distinct axes. For each integer $h>2$, form $F_h$ by retaining all closed tiles of short height at least $h$, together with all closed tile sides of direction height at least $h$, including lone long sides whose tile was discarded. Use ordinary connected components, so even a point contact joins components. The extra long sides are essential to the assertion.

For every connected component $K$ of $F_h$, the total number of retained tiles in $K$ is divisible by $c$. The number $n_h(K)$ of its tiles with short height exactly $h$ is likewise divisible by $c$, and it is either zero or at least $2c$. This strengthens a global count to a separate cost for every component. In particular each of $d_h$ components carrying at least one tile of short height $h$ costs a full $2c$ tiles at that level. Summing over $h$ bounds the total branching cost by $2c\sum_h d_h\le N$. Reflection of the height coordinate gives the identical conclusion below all exterior heights.

**Proof.** Apply the signed whole-edge construction at the threshold $h-1$ as in [SMALL_SURGERIES.md](SMALL_SURGERIES.md), now with one tiling. A retained tile belongs entirely to exactly one component; choose its term only if that component is $K$. An unretained tile with a high long side contributes only that side with reversed sign; choose it if that side belongs to $K$. Every high-edge contribution on a positive-length internal contact cancels, including contacts between two sides of unretained tiles, because the overlapping high sides are contained in the same component. There is no exterior high side. The resulting chain is exactly the boundary of the retained two-dimensional part in $K$. It is a sum of oriented whole long edges, now with directions of height $h-1$ or $h$. Decompose it into closed walks and take signed areas in the index-$c$ lattice as in the area proof. This gives the first divisibility, even if some long sides connect regions of positive area only at points.

The finite union $F_{h+1}$ lies in $F_h$, so each of its components lies in a unique parent component. Subtract from the parent count the counts of its children at the next threshold. This gives $c\mid n_h(K)$. On a fixed supporting line, all height-$h$ tile sides meeting in a positive-length contact belong to the same component. Summing the signed lengths for this component only therefore cancels as a line current, as there is no exterior at this height. The [same-sign line incidence obstruction](../../LINE_COUNTS.md) applies inside $K$ with its own short edges and whole long contributions: $n_h(K)=c$ would force all signs to agree, which its incidence estimate excludes. The claimed alternative follows. The argument allows unrestricted tile orientations and T-junctions.

These are necessary conditions on genuine tilings. They do not assert that geometrically separated regions in a partial packing will stay in separate components after further tiles are placed.
