# Absolute orientation bound for both remaining (6,5,9) targets

For the alpha target \((90,90,140)\), a tile in any tiling has its length-five side at undirected orientation \(h\) with \(|h|\le129\). For the auxiliary theta target \((90,90,60)\), it has \(|h|\le80\). These upper bounds control all geometries and T-junctions; they do not establish existence. The argument adapts [the independent height proof for 236](../cubes/AUDIT_236_HEIGHT.md).

Set \(d=i\sqrt2,\ z=(-1+2d)/3,\ w=3z=-1+2d,\ O=\mathbb Z[d]\). Every side is parallel to some \(z^j\), since the tile angles are multiples of \(\arg z\) modulo \(\pi\), and positive-length contacts connect all tiles to the horizontal base. The exponents are unique since the rational \(z+1/z=-2/3\) is not an algebraic integer. For short-edge height \(h\) the length-six side has height \(h+\epsilon\) and the length-nine side height \(h-2\epsilon\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\). This follows directly from the two positive-area prototypes \(0,5,-2+4d\) and \(0,5,7+4d\). Their doubled axial area is 20. The two outer legs have heights \(\pm2\) for the alpha target, \(\pm1\) for theta, and length 90 for both.

**Tails.** Let \(S_k\) count tiles at heights strictly above \(k\ge0\). The boundary of their oriented sum is the sum of the whole target edges of height \(j>k\), plus all oriented tile edges weighted by \(\mathbf1_{h>k}-\mathbf1_{j>k}\). This is an equality of subdivided line chains since all internal sides cancel in the sum over every tile. Rotate by \(z^{-k}\). The possible crossing displacements are, up to sign,
\[
6,\quad 6z,\quad 9z^{-1},\quad9,\quad9z,\quad9z^2.
\]
Each lies in the index-three sublattice \(I=\{x+yd\in O:x-y\equiv0\bmod3\}\); use \(6z=2w,\ 9z^{-1}=3\bar w,\ 9z^2=w^2\). The only possible exterior correction is the positive-height leg, giving \(90z^2=10w^2\) or \(90z=30w\), up to sign. They also lie in \(I\). Any finite balanced endpoint chain of full edges decomposes into closed walks by reversing the negatively weighted segments. The doubled axial area of each closed walk is a sum of determinants of lattice vectors, after translating its initial point to zero. The index therefore divides \(20S_k\), proving \(3\mid S_k\). In particular each occupied positive height costs at least three tiles.

If height \(k\ge1\) is vacant, the displacements \(6z,9z^2\) disappear and all remaining steps are in \(3O\), including any outer correction \(90z\). Thus \(9\mid S_k\). If \(k,k+1\) are vacant, the only crossing steps are \(9,9z=3w\); any exterior correction is an integer multiple of \(3w\). Their lattice index is 54, so \(54\mid20S_k\) implies \(27\mid S_k\). Three consecutive vacancies directly below a nonempty tail are impossible. Across the cut after the middle vacancy, all remaining tile steps are collinear. If the single possible outer edge is not parallel, vector closure fails; if it is parallel or absent, the signed enclosed area is zero. Distinct gap blocks below a nonempty tail have distinct positive tail counts.

**Terminal population.** If the largest occupied height \(H\ge2\) had three tiles, the signed count of length-five sides on each line of height \(H\) would be divisible by three, by oriented length balance modulo three. Other lengths are 6 and 9 and any outer leg has length 90. Three tiles cannot be partitioned entirely into opposite pairs. All three length-five sides must lie on one supporting line with the same sign. A side at height \(H+2\) can only be a nine-side of this terminal population, with fixed handedness. Both banks of an interval of such equal lengths induce the same division, and opposite orientations would require opposite signs on the five-sides. Hence there is no such side. All three tiles then have the same handedness and sign; they are distinct translates along the line of their five-sides. Their six-sides at \(H+1\) occupy three distinct lines. On each, a single length 6 must balance a signed sum of lengths 9 and possibly 90, impossible modulo nine. Therefore a terminal height \(\ge2\) costs at least six tiles.

For the alpha target at least ten tiles meet the leg of height \(-2\) in a whole side, since the longest side is nine. They have \(h\le0\). There are at most 270 tiles left at \(h\ge1\), giving at most \(270/3-1=89\) occupied heights if \(H\ge2\). There are no more than \(270/9=30\) separate gaps, and at most \(270/27=10\) of these can comprise two vacancies. Thus \(H\le89+30+10=129\). For theta at least ten tiles meet the leg of height \(-1\), giving \(h\le1\). The population at heights \(\ge2\) is at most 170 and a multiple of three, hence at most 168. The analogous count gives \(H\le1+(168/3-1)+\lfloor168/9\rfloor+\lfloor168/27\rfloor=80\). Vertical reflection of a tiling proves the same absolute bound for negative heights in both shapes.
