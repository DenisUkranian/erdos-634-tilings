# An independent height cap for the 236 candidate

Without any previously pruned boundary words or any search, every tile in the outstanding 236 candidate has short-side orientation height \(|h|\le52\). The additional terminal argument in the previous height note improves it to 51. This retraces the geometry of the bound and is independent of the 100 surviving word-orbit list.

Write \(d=i\sqrt{21}\), \(z=(-2+d)/5\), and identify points with their coordinates in the basis \((1,d)\). The target is \(0,472,236+18d\). The angle of \(z\) is irrational modulo \(\pi\) because \(z+1/z=-4/5\) cannot be the sum of a root of unity and its inverse. Positive-length edge contacts connect all tiles. Starting on the target base and following these contacts, every edge direction is therefore \(\pm z^j\). For a tile whose side of length 9 is at height \(h\), the 20 and 25 sides have heights \(h+\epsilon,h-2\epsilon\) for \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), directly from unit templates \(0,9,-8+4d\) and \(0,9,17+4d\). The target edge heights are \(-3,0,3\); the corresponding side lengths are 250,472,250. All tile doubled axial areas are 36. Physical rotations have axial determinant 1.

At a cut \(k\ge0\), collect the tiles at short height above \(k\) into \(A\). Its boundary, as signed interval chains, equals the sum of (i) the whole outer sides of edge height \(>k\) and (ii) the whole tile edges, each with coefficient
\[
     [h>k]-[j>k].
\]
This follows by canceling all original tile edges at each edge direction against the target boundary. It permits T-junctions of arbitrary offsets. After rotation by \(z^{-k}\), possible crossing steps for a 20-edge are \(20,20z\), and for a 25-edge they are \(25/z,25,25z,25z^2\), with either sign. The outer correction is absent for \(k\ge3\), and for \(k=0,1,2\) is one step \(250z^{3-k}\), up to sign. All lie in the index-five lattice \(x+2y\equiv0\pmod5\) in \(\mathbb Z^2\). A finite balanced signed sum of segments decomposes into closed endpoint walks even if different walks traverse different translations of that lattice. The determinant formula in each walk shows that its doubled area is a multiple of the lattice index. Therefore \(5\mid |A|\), where \(|A|\) counts tiles.

If \(k\ge1\) is vacant, meaning no tile of short height \(k\), the steps \(20z,25z^2\) are absent. All remaining crossing and outer steps lie in \(5\mathbb Z^2\). Thus \(25\mid|A|\). If both \(k,k+1\) are vacant, the only crossing steps are \(25,25z\). These span a lattice of index 125 because \(25z=-10+5d\). At \(k=1\) the only exterior correction is \(250z^2=-10(25)-8(25z)\). At \(k=2\) it is \(10(25z)\). Thus \(125\mid|A|\). If \(k,k+1,k+2\) are all vacant, no nonempty higher tail can occur. Across the cut after \(k+1\) the only surviving tile step has edge height \(k+1\) (from short height \(k+3\) and length 25). Any exterior correction is a single step. If not parallel to that direction it prevents vector closure, and otherwise every closed endpoint walk is collinear, hence has area zero.

Reflection across the target axis exchanges the two tails. The population at each nonzero height is a multiple of 5, by subtracting successive tails; \(n_0\equiv1\pmod5\). Directed length balance on all base-parallel lines gives \(9(P_0-M_0)\equiv472\pmod5\), where \(P_0,M_0\) are the positive and negative short counts. Along the exterior base, the number \(x\) of positive short edges satisfies \(9x\equiv472\pmod5\), so \(x\ge3\). Thus \(n_0=1\) is impossible. If \(n_0=6\), then \(P_0-M_0\) is even, between \(-6\) and 6, congruent to 3 modulo 5. Its only possible value is \(-2\), implying \(P_0=2\), impossible. Hence \(n_0\ge11\).

The opposite outer side of length 250 uses at least 10 tiles, because the longest unit side has length 25. All their short-side heights are in the opposite open half of the height axis because that outer height is \(-3\) (or 3) and a tile spans offsets of absolute value at most 2. The positive tail therefore has at most \(236-11-10=215\) tiles and at most 43 occupied heights. Distinct internal gaps among heights starting at 1 give distinct nonzero tail counts divisible by 25. There are at most eight gaps. At most one gap can have two successive vacant heights, as such a tail must equal 125. No gap has three. For extreme height \(H\), this proves \(H\le43+8+1=52\). The same applies to the other side.

The existing 51 proof uses one additional fact. If a terminal layer \(H\ge2\) had just five tiles, the signed count of short edges on each support is 0 modulo 5. Some support must have signed excess five, because the total number of short edges is odd. All five coincide in sign on the one support. Any 25-edge at \(H+2\) could meet only like 25-edges from the same terminal handedness. Equal-length chains on the two banks of an internal maximal interval coincide, pairing such tiles with opposite short signs, a contradiction. So all five have their 20-edge at \(H+1\). They are translates on their common short support and have distinct 20-edge supports. On each of these lines a single 20-edge would have to balance only 25-edges (and possibly a 250 outer edge for \(H=2\)), impossible modulo 25. Hence the terminal layer has at least ten and only 42 heights can be occupied. This yields \(H\le51\) unconditionally.

The absolute cap uses signed interval identities and does not depend on an integer offset assumption. For the separate finite vertex-star test, integer offsets along a whole edge at T-junctions follow from common clamped endpoints of the two complete bank partitions and integer whole lengths.
