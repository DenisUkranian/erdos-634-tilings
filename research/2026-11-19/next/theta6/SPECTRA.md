# Two fixed-tile classifications and one remaining scale

All lengths below are measured in the units of the stated tile. A tiling may use rotations, reflections, arbitrary real translations and T-junctions. The first two rows give the answer for every positive real scale of their respective target, including the question of which scales can occur.

| Tile | Target sides | Counts in a tiling of this target |
|---|---|---|
| \(6,5,9\) | \(15t,15t,10t\) | Exactly \(5t^2\) for integers \(t\ge7\). The least is 245. |
| \(3,8,9\) | \(72g,72g,136g\) | Exactly \(136g^2\) for integers \(g\ge2\). The least is 544. |
| \(6,5,9\) | \(45g,45g,70g\) | Every integer \(g\ge3\) works with \(70g^2\) tiles. The scale \(g=1\) is impossible. Only \(g=2\), i.e. 280, is undecided. A complete straight seam from a vertex exists for precisely the integer scales \(g\ge3\). |

## Theta target for the tile of sides 6, 5, 9

The outer sides are \(15t,15t,10t\). In any dissection its base is a concatenation of whole tile sides, so \(10t\) is integral and \(t\) is rational. The area ratio is \(5t^2\). If \(t=m/n\) in lowest terms, integrality of the area ratio gives \(n^2\mid5\), so \(t\) is an integer.

Bonfioli's notch construction supplies every \(t\ge7\). His November 18 paper identifies \(t=6\) as open. The unit extension applies to any existing tiling at scale \(n\), including one having no prescribed internal structure. Its hypothesis in this case is \(2\le(3-2)n\). Thus a tiling with \(2\le t\le5\) would extend, one unit at a time, to a tiling with \(t=6\). A tiling with \(t=1\) would also give one at \(t=6\) by dilating by six and subdividing each enlarged tile into 36 unit copies. The new certificate excludes \(t=6\). This proves necessity and sufficiency for every real \(t>0\). ## Two alpha targets with the same angle relation

Put \(b=f^2-e^2,\ c=f^2\). For the primitive tile \((ef,b,c)\), the triangle \(A_g(e,f)\) has equal sides \(gbc\), base \(gb(b+c)\), and area \(b(b+c)g^2\) in tile units. Bonfioli's necessary condition gives \(N=r^2b(b+c)\) with integral \(r\) for every tiling in this shape. Comparing areas forces \(g=|r|\), even when the original scale was an arbitrary positive real number. For a positive integer \(g\), the standard dissection of \(A_g\) into a tile scaled by \(gb\) and a theta target at \(t=gf\) gives a tiling whenever that theta target tiles. The two tile counts sum to \((gb)^2+b(gf)^2=b(b+c)g^2\). [Beeson §14.3, results table and theorem attributed to Bonfioli](https://arxiv.org/html/1206.2229v6#S14.SS3) · [Bonfioli's explicit construction](https://github.com/ElVec1o/erdos634_primes_and_N200/blob/752005605ca4e607d2a08f57bdc2445dd5558dbb/paper/erdos634_v6_main.tex#L419-L431).

For \((e,f)=(1,3)\), \(b=8\). Every \(g\ge2\) works because \(t=3g\ge6\). This uses only the constructive direction of Bonfioli's notch theorem. Its parameters are \(n_0=j_0=1,\ M_0=3,\ t_0=6\). The grid gives \(64g^2\) tiles and the theta part gives \(72g^2\). At \(g=1\) the independent integer weight checks exclude the \(72,72,136\) target. These facts prove the second row. As an unrestricted numerical count, 136 is possible with another shape, since \(136=10^2+6^2\). [Direct geometric certificate for the negative direction](../alpha136mesh/PROOF.md) · [Positive construction for \(e=1\)](https://github.com/ElVec1o/erdos634_primes_and_N200/blob/752005605ca4e607d2a08f57bdc2445dd5558dbb/paper/erdos634_v6_main.tex#L389-L425).

For \((e,f)=(2,3)\), \(b=5\). Every \(g\ge3\) works since \(3g\ge9\) and the first row applies. The two parts use \(25g^2\) and \(45g^2\) tiles. At \(g=1\), \(N=70\) was already excluded by Bonfioli. At \(g=2\) a seam from a vertex would force either the forbidden theta target at \(t=6\), or a triangular piece of area \(810/7\) in tile units. Thus existence of a vertex-seam dissection is decided at every scale, while general existence at \(g=2\) stays open.
