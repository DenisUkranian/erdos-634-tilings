# Review of the two (6,5,9) orientation bounds

The [height argument](../alpha280mesh/HEIGHT.md) applies to actual dissections with arbitrary translations. On a line of fixed exponent, subdividing at every edge endpoint gives the boundary cancellation identity on elementary intervals. Projecting onto the lines with exponent above the cut commutes with this subdivision. The resulting expression in whole tile and target edges therefore has zero total endpoint charge at every point. Reorient negatively weighted edges and decompose the resulting finite directed multigraph into closed walks. Each walk, independently translated to its first vertex and rotated by the prescribed power of the unit, has vertices in the lattice generated by its steps. Its doubled signed axial area is a sum of determinants in that lattice. The sum over the walks equals twenty times the number of tiles in the tail. This argument requires no common translation lattice for different walks or actual tiles.

The three lattice indices are 3, 9 and 54. In the third case their consequence is divisibility of the tail count by 27, since its doubled axial area is twenty times the count. The top-layer argument also survives partial edge contacts. At exponent two above the top height the only possible sides have length nine. On each side of a maximal connected segment the length-nine intervals start from the same end, so their opposite directed displacements must pair. Three identically signed short edges on the same line cannot support such a pair because this extremal nine-side determines the tile handedness. After removing that possibility, the three length-six sides are in distinct lines and have no possible length balance modulo nine. The occupied-height and vacancy-block counts then give the bounds 129 and 80.

For the mirror filter write the short-edge direction as \(u\), the third vertex relative to the anchor as \((c+4i\sqrt2)u\), and the target width as \(L\). The reflection sends the anchor to the reflected endpoint of the short edge after counterclockwise reordering. The new template has direction \(\bar u\) and parameter \(5-c\). Let \((t_x,t_y)\) be the reflected relative short-edge endpoint in mesh units. For an original anchor in cell \((i,j)\), the reflected horizontal index is either \(LQ-i+\lfloor t_x\rfloor\) or its predecessor. Both are possible even at integral \(t_x\) because reflection reverses a half-open interval. The vertical index is \(j+\lfloor t_y\rfloor\) or \(j+\lceil t_y\rceil\). The [mirror implementation](../cubes/beta_shapes/mirror_cells.hpp) admits all of these alternatives. Reflection sends a tiling to another tiling of the same isosceles triangle, so removing a cell whose reflected counterparts are all absent preserves every real placement.

An independent exact-rational enumeration at \(Q=3\) checked the reflected vertex triples and all combinations of fractional-coordinate breakpoint and open-interval representatives. It covered the 1,036 templates and 36,912 combinations at height bound 129, and the 644 templates and 22,800 combinations at height bound 80.

Fresh builds and runs of the stated arbitrary-precision C++ verifier, with clearance disabled and all heights active, reproduced both published-in-this-workspace traces byte for byte. Both reached fixed points on pass 19. The [280 trace](../alpha280mesh/global-q3-alpha.log) retains 1,950,559 shape/cell pairs with heights from −37 to 37. The [180 trace](../alpha280mesh/global-q3-theta.log) retains 921,492 with heights from −30 to 30. These are necessary bounds for arbitrary tilings. Positive cell counts do not imply existence. The replay reuses the C++ geometric filter, and the separate mirror check supplements it without constituting a second implementation of the full filter or external peer review.
