# Height cuts for the (9,20,25) tile

The target of the remaining 236 case has vertices 0, 472, 236+18d, where d²=−21 and d=i√21. Set z=(−2+d)/5. Multiplication by z has axial determinant one. Its argument γ has 2 cos γ=−4/5, so γ/π is irrational by the algebraic integrality of a root of unity and its inverse.

Choose the oriented base as the real unit. Every tile edge is parallel to exactly one z^h. Indeed the target base is covered by tiles, the three angle turns have the form kγ+jπ, and any two tiles are connected by a chain of positive-length edge contacts. For connectivity choose interior points and a generic polygonal path between them avoiding the finitely many tile vertices. The integer h is the edge height, distinct from its perpendicular distance to anything. Define a tile's height as the height of its side of length 9. Relative to h its length-20 and length-25 sides have heights h+ε and h−2ε, respectively, with ε=±1. This follows directly from α=2γ−π, β=2π−3γ. Exterior edge heights are 0,±3. Let n_h be the number of tiles with height h.

## Area divisibility across a cut

Let A_k be the union of tiles of height >k and E_k the sum of oriented whole outer sides of edge height >k. Give every tile and the target the same positive boundary orientation. Then ∂A_k−E_k is a signed sum of whole tile sides. A tile side with tile height h and edge height j has coefficient 1[h>k]−1[j>k]. To see this subtract, direction by direction, the whole target boundary from the sum of boundaries of all tiles. Overlaps of edges and T-junctions cancel as oriented intervals, with endpoints at arbitrary real positions allowed.

For k≥0, form ∂A_k by adding E_k. Its crossing displacements after rotation by z^(−k) have these possible values, up to sign.

| Side length | Normalized displacement |
|---|---|
| 9 | none |
| 20 | 20, 20z |
| 25 | 25/z, 25, 25z, 25z² |

All six are in the index-five lattice L={(x,y) in ℤ² : x+2y≡0 mod 5}, identifying (x,y) with x+yd. This can be checked using 25z²=−17−4d. The normalized exterior correction consists of just the height-3 side of length 250 when k=0,1,2 and is empty otherwise. The three displacements are, up to sign, 250z³=236−18d, 250z²=−170−40d, 250z=−100+50d, also in L.

A balanced finite sum of whole oriented segments splits into closed walks even if different walks have different translations. This follows by decomposing the finite balanced directed multigraph on their endpoints, reversing negative coefficients. Additivity of the line integral x dy−y dx under interval subdivision gives the doubled axial area of A_k. After translating each walk to the origin, each summand in its shoelace formula is a determinant of lattice vectors, hence a multiple of the lattice index. The normalized tile area is 36 in doubled axial units. Since 5 does not divide 36, the number of tiles above every k≥0 is divisible by 5. Reflection across the axis of the isosceles triangle changes the sign of every height and gives the same conclusion for lower tails. Consequently

- 5 divides n_h for all h≠0, and n_0≡236≡1 mod 5.
- If k≥1 and n_k=0, the positive tail above k has cardinality divisible by 25. The 20z and 25z² terms above can arise only from a tile at k. The remaining displacements belong to 5ℤ[d], of index 25. The exterior corrections for k=1,2 are in this lattice.
- If k≥1 and n_k=n_(k+1)=0, only 25 and 25z can cross the cut, from opposite sides of the gap. They generate the lattice with basis (25,0),(−10,5), of index 125. For k=1,2 the exterior corrections are respectively −10(25)−8(25z) and 10(25z). Therefore the positive tail above k has cardinality divisible by 125.
- Three consecutive vacant heights k,k+1,k+2 with k≥1 cannot precede a positive tail. Low tiles have heights ≤k−1 and high tiles have heights ≥k+3. Any common edge direction must be k+1. More explicitly take the cut after k+1, so the signed crossing terms from low or high tiles can include only low tiles at k−1, of edge height k+1, or high tiles at k+3, of edge height k+1. The latter are the only crossing terms for that cut; in either case this observation gives just one direction. The sole exterior correction, if present, is the whole side of height 3. A nonparallel single correction cannot yield a balanced chain. If absent or parallel, the whole boundary has signed area zero (each walk is collinear). A nonempty tail has positive area.

The reflected versions hold at negative heights. In particular at most one gap of two consecutive vacant heights can occur in the whole occupied range. Two distinct gaps on the same side would have different positive tails divisible by 125; opposite-side gaps would each require 125 tiles. Each separated internal gap corresponds to a distinct positive multiple of 25 on its own side, bounding their total number by nine.

A double gap far enough that the exterior gives no correction costs still more. If k≥3, n_k=n_(k+1)=0 and the tail is nonempty, its 125 tiles have axial area 2,250. Its two normalized step vectors 25,25z span a fundamental parallelogram of axial area 125. Each walk in that basis uses horizontal and vertical unit steps. With H horizontal steps and V vertical steps its absolute signed area in these units is at most HV/4: use the integral x dy, subtract the minimum x, and bound by the width (at most H/2) times its V/2 upward steps. Apply the same bound to every walk and sum; the total signed area is at most the product of the total step counts divided by four. The first direction comes only from height k+2 and the second only from k−1, and each of the two total step counts is even by displacement closure. Thus

    2 floor(n_(k−1)/2) · 2 floor(n_(k+2)/2) ≥ 72.

In particular the two adjacent occupied populations must total at least 20, and a neighbor of size five requires the opposite neighbor to have at least twenty. Reflection gives the identical conclusion below the base height. The simple span computations below do not use this additional constraint.

## Per-line balances and terminal levels

Use coherent counterclockwise orientation and direct the base along its positive target orientation. Let g_h be the signed count of the length-9 edges at h, using z^h as positive. Balancing physical lengths at each h gives 9g_h≡0 mod 5 off zero, and 9g_0≡472 mod 5. Therefore g_0≡3 mod 5. The base itself contains x positively oriented short edges, where 9x+20y+25z=472, hence x≡3 mod 5 and x≥3. If n_0=6, parity and |g_0|≤6 force g_0=−2, allowing only two positively oriented short edges. Combining these observations with n_0≡1 mod 5 proves n_0≥11. The same length congruence applies on *each supporting line*, with the correction 472 only on the outer base.

If the greatest occupied height is U≥2, then n_U≥10. Otherwise n_U=5. On each supporting line the signed count of short edges is divisible by five. If all there cancel pairwise in number, n_U is even, so one line has a signed excess of five. All five edges are therefore on this line with a common sign.

A length-25 edge of height U+2 meets only 25-edges of tiles of height U, with the same handedness. There is no target side at that height. Along a maximal connected straight chain of such edges, both banks partition the whole chain into edges of length 25; their divisions coincide. Opposing tiles on a matching 25-edge have opposite oriented 25-edges. For fixed handedness this forces their short edges to have opposite signs. Hence no such 25-edge occurs. All five tiles then have their 20-edge at U+1. They have the same handedness and short-edge sign, so are distinct translates along their common short-edge line. Their 20-edges thus lie on five distinct lines. On each such line there is exactly one side of length 20, and every other tile side there has length 25. Any coincident exterior side has length 250 (only U+1=3 is possible). This contradicts oriented length balance modulo 25. Reflection gives the lower extreme statement. In particular when an extreme has absolute height at least two, the terminal layer costs an extra five tiles beyond simple divisibility. Also each outer leg of length 250 is covered by at least ten tiles, and its edge height ±3 requires its covering tiles' short-edge heights to have that same sign. Thus each tail contains at least ten tiles. Each one-sided tail contains at most 236−11−10=215 tiles. If its outermost height has absolute value at least two, there can be at most 42 occupied heights on this side (its last height costs at least ten, and all others at least five). There can be at most eight separated gaps because their tail counts are distinct positive multiples of 25 bounded by 215. At most one gap uses a second vacant height. Therefore every tile's short-edge height satisfies |h|≤51.

## Local obstruction across five adjacent levels

For |h|≥4, if n_h=5 then at least one of n_(h−2), n_(h−1), n_(h+1), n_(h+2) is at least ten.

Suppose all four are zero or five. Let ℓ be the one short-edge support at height h. Exactly five short edges occur on ℓ, all of the same sign. Any non-short tile edge on ℓ has length 20 coming from h±1, or length 25 coming from h±2. In each nonempty neighbor layer, all five short edges have the same sign on a single support, whose direction differs from ℓ. Only one handedness there contributes an edge to the direction h. Within this handedness, all its tiles are translates of one another along the common short-edge line. Therefore at most one such edge from that layer lies on ℓ. The signed length equation on ℓ, which is interior because |h|≥4, is thus 45=20x+25y with |x|,|y|≤2. Its only integer solution is x=y=1. As at most two edges of either length can occur, this forces precisely one 20-edge and one 25-edge opposite the five short edges. Each connected component of this interval union equates a sum of short lengths with a sum of the facing long lengths. Neither 20 nor 25 is a multiple of nine. Hence it is one unbroken chain of five consecutive short edges.

The endpoint angles of a short edge are α and γ. In the chain, a transition from one order of endpoints to the reverse can go in only one direction, as the opposite transition would place two obtuse γ's on the same bank at their common endpoint. Thus the five tiles comprise at most two constant-handedness blocks, one of length at least three. At each of two successive internal junctions of that block, the gap between the two tiles has angle π−α−γ=β. This is the smallest angle, so a single tile with its β angle at that point is compulsory. One of its two placements is the half-turn mate fitting the two successive translates, with short edge parallel to ℓ and opposite in sign. This contradicts all five short edges of h having the same sign. In the other placement the 20- and 25-rays are exchanged, uniquely fixing the shape at the junction. If the block's 20-edge is at h+ε and its 25-edge at h−2ε, the new tile has short-edge height h−ε. The two forced tiles are translates by nine units along ℓ and their short edges are not parallel to ℓ. Their short edges consequently have distinct supporting lines, contradicting that that neighboring population is either zero or five.

This improves the bound on |h|. Divide each one-sided population by five and work inward from a provisional extreme U≤51 with a total budget of 43 units. A nonzero terminal population costs at least two units for U≥2. At each newly added height, a zero requires the accumulated outer tail divisible by five, a second consecutive zero requires divisibility by 25, and a third is impossible. When the fifth term of a window is available, a one-unit center of height ≥4 requires a term of at least two in the window. The finite dynamic program span.py enumerates these necessary conditions, retaining the full accumulated count and the last four entries truncated at two. It yields a maximum of 43. A 43-level population sequence satisfying this relaxation is printed by the script; it does not give geometric placements. Thus **|h|≤43 for every short edge, and every tile-edge height is at most 45 in absolute value.**

## A finite test on the ten leg words

For any fixed partial packing, restrict the signed length-9 intervals on each physical supporting line to their endpoint measures on ℝ/5ℤ. Edges of length 20, 25, or 250 contribute zero there. All 9-edges with the same lower-end arclength modulo one fall into a five-element orbit. Let v_j count the signed intervals at the five possible lower-end residues (the orientation sign is included). For a true tiling the five counts must be constant because a 9-step generates the orbit. On the outer base, first subtract a chain of m positively oriented short edges along the base with 9m≡472 mod 5. Its endpoint measure is precisely the projected outer boundary measure on that line. Thus the number of additional tiles for one orbit is at least ∑|v_j−median(v)|. Sum this over lines and orbits, then round each resulting fixed-plus-required height population up to its required residue modulo 5. Every new tile supplies exactly one short edge.

The script check.py expands the 1,475 actual tiles of the scaled reference construction, independently reconstructs the ten archived leg words and both middle apex tiles, and computes the bound using exact rational coordinates. On the positive control the observed nonzero short-edge height counts are 625 at −1, 450 at +1, and 400 at +2, with zero endpoint cost. Here the scale is t=5 and the base has length 1,180, so all height populations are divisible by five. The same lattice calculations apply to this scale using legs 625. On the 87-tile partial packing the endpoint cost is 16 additional tiles and the rounded population lower bound is 111. For the 110 archived two-leg/apex configurations the bounds range from 51 to 76. None crosses 236; no leg word has been excluded by this test alone. Feeding each job's height-by-height lower bounds into the span dynamic program bounds the largest absolute short-edge height by 35 in 6 jobs, 36 in 24, 37 in 56, 38 in 21, and 39 in the final 3. Thus all 110 remaining jobs satisfy |h|≤39 on every short edge and |j|≤41 on every tile edge. The computation deducts the minimum opposite-side and central budgets before testing each tail and also enforces its own per-height lower bounds. See results.json for multiplicities.

Run `python check.py` from this directory. This establishes necessary conditions for a hypothetical tiling, without deciding the remaining case or Erdős problem 634.

## A global obstruction for the 189-tile partial packing

Apply the line-residue bound to the later 189-tile packing in ../beta236/packing189.txt. The endpoint correction costs at least eighteen more short edges. Rounding up at each height and imposing n₀≥11 raises the total lower bound to 231. Its outermost positive height is 5, with five tiles required there. Introducing any still higher occupied height would add a terminal layer of size at least ten, exceeding the budget. Consequently the terminal-5 bound forces this complete population table.

| Short-edge height | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Compulsory population | 100 | 50 | 11 | 40 | 10 | 10 | 5 | 10 |

Their total is 236. One can reject the table by integer arithmetic without a tile-contact test. Orient each tile counterclockwise. Give a directed edge pointing along ±z^j the weight equal to its directed sign times (−1)^j, which is constant over all three sides of a tile. At short-edge height h use A_h for the signed count of tiles whose 20-edge is at h+1, and B_h for the others. Target signed lengths E_j are 250,472,250 at j=−3,0,3, and zero elsewhere. Then

    25 A_(j+2) + 20 B_(j+1) + 9(A_j+B_j) + 20 A_(j−1) + 25 B_(j−2) = E_j.

At step h insert j=h−2. The previously determined variables fix the integer A_h. If r is the total number of additional tiles at h, and a,b are the previously placed signed counts, the integer B_h must satisfy |A_h−a|+|B_h−b|≤r with the same parity as r. Enumerating this short interval gives respectively 1,6,4,12,10 partial states on entry to heights −2,−1,0,1,2; none proceeds past height two. See ledger189.py and ledger189.json.

A second calculation combines endpoint deficits and all signed-direction equations in one finite dynamic program. The signed Laurent relations are derived in ../beta236/SIGNED_LEDGER.md. Their integer solutions are parameterized by an arbitrary finite integer sequence H. In short-edge indexing,

    A_h = A*_h + 4 H_(h−2) + 5 H_(h−3),
    B_h = B*_h − 5 H_(h−1) − 4 H_(h−2).

Nonzero particular A* values at h=−1,0,1 are 10,4,−5; corresponding B* values are −5,4,10. The total variation inequality in that signed-ledger note gives 18 max|H_i|≤236+38 for any solution within the budget. Thus |H_i|≤15. The orientation bound above permits h=−43,…,43. The outer two H values at each end are zero: the smallest index i with nonzero H contributes to B_(i+1), and the greatest contributes to A_(i+3). For fixed three successive values, let n be the number of placed tiles there and a,b the two placed signed counts. A lower bound at height h is the larger of the line-residue bound and n+|A_h−a|+|B_h−b|. Round up to both the required residue modulo five and the parity A_h+B_h modulo two. Minimize the sum over successive H triples by retaining the last pair. Every completion by 236 tiles would supply a path costing at most 236.

The computed threshold is 246. It remains 246 after omitting both tiles 121 and 186 of the previously identified four-tile local obstruction. Greedy deletion reduced that example to 91 tiles while keeping the threshold 246. Their original one-based indices are in reduced-obstruction.json. The checker reloads those exact indices and derives the contradiction independently of the greedy discovery. The 91-tile subset omits both tiles 121 and 186 from the older four-tile pattern. Its endpoint cost alone is 64, and the separate height-population lower bound is only 186; the contradiction uses the signed equations and height information together. It leaves the ten initial leg words undetermined. For their 110 jobs the combined bounds range from 56 to 96. See polynomial_dp.py and polynomial_dp.json.
