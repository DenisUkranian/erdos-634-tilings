# Whole-edge cuts and area divisibility

4 October 2026. Denis Paliy, research with ChatGPT assistance.
This note proves a positive-region identity and an orientation bound
depending on the scale. It does not prove an absolute orientation bound,
a complete scale spectrum, or Erdős 634. The arguments were reconstructed
and checked by separate internal review passes; external acceptance is
not asserted.

## A median-cut identity for an actual tiling

Assign an integer height to every unoriented edge direction occurring in
a finite tiling of a polygon by triangles. Collinear overlapping edges
must have the same height. For a tile R, let q(R) be the median of its
three edge heights. For an integer k, let H_k be the union of the tiles
with q(R)>k. Let B_{>k} be the counterclockwise outer boundary chain,
retaining precisely the edges of height greater than k.

For each tile take its counterclockwise boundary and form

    1[q(R)>k] boundary(R)
      - sum over its edges e of 1[h(e)>k] e.

This is either zero or a single WHOLE tile edge with coefficient +1 or
-1. Indeed, the median is above the cut exactly when a majority of the
three edges are above it. Let C_k be the sum of these selected directed
whole edges, retaining multiplicities. Cancellation along actual contact
segments gives the identity

    C_k = boundary(H_k) - B_{>k}.                       (1)

This holds with arbitrary T-junctions. Atomization is used to compare
geometric chains, but each selected graph edge remains a whole tile edge.
Its incidence boundary consists of its two endpoints; merely passing
through another vertex contributes zero there. Consequently, if B_{>k}
is empty or is the entire outer boundary, C_k is a finite balanced
directed graph, and decomposes into closed whole-edge walks.

If all steps of such walks belong to a rank-two lattice M of fundamental
area A, each walk has signed area in (A/2)Z. Translate its first vertex
to zero and apply the shoelace formula in a lattice basis. This is valid
for self-intersecting walks as well. Summing signed areas respects the
chain identity (1), including coincident edges. Thus it measures an
actual tile union or its complement, not an arbitrary formal inventory.

## The exact Group-1 cut lattice

Let 0<u<v be coprime integers and put

    a=uv, b=v²-u², c=v², D=4v²-u².

The tile angles opposite these sides satisfy 3α+2β=π and
γ=(π+α)/2. Set z=exp(iγ). Then

    v z²+u z+v=0,       sin γ=√D/(2v).                 (2)

The angle α/π is irrational: 2cos α=2-u²/v² is rational and strictly
between 1 and 2, whereas a rational value of 2cos of a rational multiple
of π must be an integer. The same holds for γ/π.

After fixing one exterior edge horizontally, every directed tile edge
has direction ±z^h for a unique integer h. The angle differences of a
tile belong to this group, and positive-length contact connectivity
propagates it to every tile. A generic path through the target avoids
the finitely many vertices, so this argument does not require an
edge-to-edge tiling.

The c,b,a heights in one chirality are h,h+2,h+3; in the other they are
h,h-2,h-3. Hence the median is the b-edge height. At the cut k+1/2,
the selected edge is c or a. Its possible heights are respectively

    c: k-1,k,k+1,k+2;       a: k,k+1.

The lattice generated by all their allowed displacement vectors is

    M_k = c z^(k-1) <1,z,z²,z³>_Z + a z^k <1,z>_Z
        = v z^k (Z+z Z).                               (3)

Here the spans are over Z, not over Gaussian integers: arbitrary
quarter-turns at a fixed height are not available. To verify equality,
factor z^(k-1) and use the basis 1,z. Relation (2) reduces the generators
to the lattice generated by

    (v²,0), (0,v), (uv,u²).

The vector (0,v) follows from gcd(v²,uv)=v. The displayed lattice is
exactly {(vX,uX+vY): X,Y in Z}: the reverse inclusion follows from
Bezout for u,v and adjustment by (0,v). This is also the lattice
generated by v z and v z², proving (3).

Its fundamental area is v√D/2, while the unit tile area is bu√D/4.
Since gcd(v,bu)=1, (1) therefore implies:

* If every exterior side has height at most k, the number of tiles in
  H_k is divisible by v.
* If every exterior side has height greater than k, the number of tiles
  outside H_k is divisible by v.

Taking differences of successive unions, every median-height level
outside the interval [h_min,h_max] of exterior-side heights contains
a multiple of v tiles. Thus any such N-tile tiling has at most

    floor(N/v) + h_max-h_min+1

occupied median heights. A median height permits at most four rigid
orientations: two chiralities and two half-turn choices. This bound
depends on N and does not settle the absolute-bound question.

## A sharper theorem for every theta target

At integer scale t, place the theta target at

    O=0, B=bvt, C=-bvt z².

Its side lengths are bvt,bvt,but; its tile count is N=bt². The oriented
sides OB,BC,CO have heights 0,1,2 respectively. For k=0, add the edge
B→O to C_0. For k=1, add C→O to C_1. Formula (1) gives

    C_0+(B→O) = boundary(H_0)-boundary(target),
    C_1+(C→O) = boundary(H_1).

Both added steps belong to their respective lattices: B/(v)=bt and
C/(vz)=-btz. The closed-walk area argument now yields

    |H_0|-N = 0 (mod v),       |H_1| = 0 (mod v).

Together with the outer-cut congruences, this proves:

> In every theta tiling, every median-height level except q=1 contains
> a multiple of v tiles. The q=1 count is congruent to N modulo v.

For consecutive parameters u=v-1 we have N=(2v-1)t². The number of
occupied levels is therefore at most

    floor((2v-1)t²/v)+1 <= 2t².

Consequently **every such theta tiling uses at most 8t² rigid
orientations**, independently of u and v. This is conditional only on
the existence of the stated tiling; it supplies no tiling at an
arithmetically allowed scale.

## Why this does not complete the proposed approach

The endpoint conditions alone add no restriction on the integer theta
scale: the source-to-sink displacements above lie in M_0 and M_1 for
every integer t, including the impossible scale one. Area divisibility
uses more geometric information, through the positive unions H_k.
Nevertheless its orientation bound grows with t.

A proof of the [finite-scheme conjecture](finite-schemes-attack.md)
still requires a uniform constructive replacement or another global
argument. The fact that the cut chains bound nested positive regions
does not establish that those regions admit a different filling with
fewer orientations. No such replacement is asserted here.
