# Independent audit: eventual tilings in the five rational Group-1 shapes

29 September 2026. Internal mathematical audit by a separate ChatGPT review pass within this project. The two necessary isosceles count forms, the three gluing transfers, and the explicit eventual-construction bound below pass this review. This is a result for fixed rational Group-1 tiles in the stated parameter range; it is not a classification of all tilings or a solution of Erdős problem 634.

Companion statement and coordinate formulas: [eventual-rational-families.md](../eventual-rational-families.md).

## Verdict

**PASS.** For every primitive tile

$$
(a,b,c)=(uv,v^2-u^2,v^2),\qquad 0<u<v,\quad\gcd(u,v)=1,
$$

any tiling of a triangle with apex angle $\alpha$ and two base angles $\theta=\alpha+\beta$ has

$$
\boxed{N=bT^2,\qquad X=bvT,\qquad Y=buT,\qquad T\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.}
$$

Here $X$ denotes either equal side and $Y$ the base. No squarefreeness hypothesis on $b$ is required. All orientations, reflected tiles and arbitrary T-junctions are permitted.

The two characters used in this proof are already present in Michael Beeson's *Triangle Tiling: The Case $3\alpha+2\beta=\pi$*, [arXiv:1206.2229v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2229v4), Sections 7–8, Lemmas 19, 21–23 and Theorems 3–4, printed pages 25–28. I read the relevant source passage and independently reconstructed the proof below. This is a consequence of those published invariants and primitive normalization, not a new invariant or a priority claim.

## 1. The correct direction group

Put $\delta=\theta=\alpha+\beta$. The angle relation gives

$$
\alpha=\pi-2\delta,\qquad
\beta=3\delta-\pi,\qquad
\gamma=\pi-\delta.
$$

Also $\delta/\pi$ is irrational. Indeed,
$2\cos\alpha=2-u^2/v^2$ is rational strictly between 1 and 2. If $\alpha/\pi$ were rational, this quantity would be a rational algebraic integer and hence an integer, a contradiction. The formula $2\delta=\pi-\alpha$ proves the claim.

Fix the direction of one target side as zero. The group actually needed is

$$
G=\mathbb Z\delta+\mathbb Z\pi\pmod{2\pi},
$$

with unique coordinates $n\delta+\varepsilon\pi$, $n\in\mathbb Z$, $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$. Uniqueness follows at once from irrationality.

All tile directions lie in this group. Within a counterclockwise tile, the turns are

$$
\pi-\gamma=\delta,\qquad
\pi-\alpha=2\delta,\qquad
\pi-\beta=2\pi-3\delta.
$$

Across a shared positive-length segment the two counterclockwise orientations differ by $\pi$. The adjacency graph using positive-length contacts is connected: a path in the interior of the target between interior points of two tiles can be chosen to avoid the finite set of vertices and to cross edges transversely. Starting with a tile on the chosen external side and propagating through this graph proves the assertion even when the tiling has T-junctions.

It would be unsafe to assign real signs arbitrarily to the generators of the larger group generated by $\pi/2$ and $\alpha/2$: in that group an odd-under-reversal character must take a fourth-root value on $\pi/2$. That larger group is unnecessary. The actual tiling directions occupy the subgroup $G$, where the two real characters below are valid.

## 2. Two valid characters and the contribution of a tile

Define

$$
\chi_1(n\delta+\varepsilon\pi)=(-1)^\varepsilon,
\qquad
\chi_2(n\delta+\varepsilon\pi)=(-1)^{n+\varepsilon}.
$$

Both satisfy

$$
\chi_i(\varphi+\pi)=-\chi_i(\varphi),
\qquad \chi_i(-\varphi)=\chi_i(\varphi).
$$

For an oriented segment of length $L$, give it weight $L\chi_i(\varphi)$. Let $\Phi_i(R)$ be the sum on the counterclockwise boundary of a polygon $R$.

Start along the edge of length $a$ of a counterclockwise tile. In one orientation the directions of its $a,b,c$ edges have coordinates

$$
(n,\varepsilon),\quad(n+1,\varepsilon),\quad(n+3,\varepsilon).
$$

For a reflected placement the corresponding indices are $n,n-1,n-3$; equivalently, reflection reverses the boundary order while negating all relative directions. In either case, $b$ and $c$ have odd index differences from $a$, while all three edges have the same $\varepsilon$. Thus every placement has

$$
\Phi_1(\text{tile})=\sigma_1(a+b+c),\qquad
\Phi_2(\text{tile})=\sigma_2(a-b-c),
$$

where $\sigma_1,\sigma_2\in\{1,-1\}$. This explicitly includes reflected copies; no common orientation assumption is made.

## 3. Cancellation and the parity information

Subdivide all tile boundaries at every vertex lying on an edge. Every resulting interior segment appears exactly twice, with opposite directions. The weights cancel because reversal negates each character. On external sides, collinear subdivision adds lengths without changing the character. Therefore

$$
\Phi_1(\text{target})=M_1(a+b+c),\qquad
\Phi_2(\text{target})=M_2(a-b-c),
$$

where $M_i$ is the sum of the $N$ signs $\sigma_i$. In particular,

$$
M_1,M_2\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
M_1\equiv M_2\equiv N\pmod2.
$$

The common parity is essential; using only the integrality of the two quotients loses a possible factor of two.

## 4. The target's scale and boundary values

The sine law gives the side ratio $X:Y=v:u$:

$$
\frac{Y}{X}=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\delta}
=2\cos\delta=\frac uv.
$$

Each outer side has integer length because it is a sum of whole tile-edge lengths. Write $X=vL$ and $Y=uL$. Since $\gcd(u,v)=1$, Bézout's identity gives $L\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$.

Order the target's vertices counterclockwise so their angles are $(\delta,\alpha,\delta)$, with $AB$ and $BC$ the equal sides. Taking the direction of $AB$ to be zero, its successive side directions are

$$
0,\qquad 2\delta,\qquad \delta+\pi.
$$

Hence

$$
\Phi_1(\text{target})=2X-Y=(2v-u)L,
$$

$$
\Phi_2(\text{target})=2X+Y=(2v+u)L.
$$

For the tile,

$$
a+b+c=(v+u)(2v-u),\qquad
a-b-c=-(v-u)(2v+u).
$$

Dividing yields exactly

$$
M_1=\frac{L}{v+u},\qquad
M_2=-\frac{L}{v-u}.
$$

Since these integers have the same parity,

$$
\frac{M_1+M_2}{2}=-\frac{uL}{b}\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\frac{M_1-M_2}{2}=\frac{vL}{b}\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Bézout applied to $u,v$ now gives $b\mid L$. In fact, the first quotient alone suffices because $\gcd(u,b)=1$. Write $L=bT$, $T$ a positive integer.

The target's apex angle is $\alpha$; the tile sides enclosing $\alpha$ are $b,c$. Comparison of areas therefore gives

$$
N=\frac{X^2}{bc}=\frac{v^2L^2}{b v^2}
=\frac{L^2}{b}=bT^2,
$$

which proves the stated necessary form.

## 5. Relation to the earlier squarefree-kernel parametrization

If $b=q h^2$ with $q$ squarefree, the area-only argument gives

$$
N=qt^2,\quad X=vqht,\quad Y=uqht.
$$

Thus $L=qht$, and the new divisibility $b\mid L$ is exactly $h\mid t$. Writing $t=hT$ gives $N=bT^2$. Equivalently,

$$
M_1=(v-u)t/h,\qquad M_2=-(v+u)t/h.
$$

There is no further parity restriction on an integer $T$: after substitution,
$M_1=(v-u)T$, $M_2=-(v+u)T$, and both are automatically congruent to $bT^2$ modulo two.

The only subtle case in reducing the two divisibilities is when $u,v$ are both odd. Then $\gcd(v-u,v+u)=2$, so integrality alone permits $L=b/2$. The parity condition removes that possibility. For example, $u=1,v=3,b=8,L=4$ would give integral quotients $M_1=1$, $M_2=-2$, but they have opposite parity and cannot be sums of signs over the same number of tiles.

## 6. Alpha-isosceles arithmetic

Put $Q=b+c=2v^2-u^2$ and $P=b+2c=3v^2-u^2$. An isosceles target whose base angles are $\alpha$ has side proportions $(c,c,Q)$, since

$$
\frac{\sin(2\alpha)}{\sin\alpha}=2\cos\alpha=\frac Qc.
$$

The pair $c,Q$ is coprime. Integer outer-side lengths therefore make the sides $(cL,cL,QL)$ with a positive integer $L$.

Traverse this target counterclockwise, starting along an equal side with direction zero. Its corner angles are $(\alpha,\pi-2\alpha,\alpha)$. The successive edge directions in the $(n,\varepsilon)$ coordinates of Section 1 are

$$
(0,0),\quad(-4,0),\quad(-2,0).
$$

Indeed the first turn is $2\alpha=2\pi-4\delta$, and the second is $\pi-\alpha=2\delta$. Both characters take the value $+1$ on all three sides, so

$$
\Phi_1=\Phi_2=(2c+Q)L=(2v-u)(2v+u)L.
$$

The signed tile counts are consequently

$$
M_1=\frac{(2v+u)L}{v+u},\qquad
M_2=-\frac{(2v-u)L}{v-u}.
$$

Their common parity gives either of the integer identities

$$
\frac{M_1+M_2}{2}=-\frac{uvL}{b},\qquad
\frac{M_1-M_2}{2}=\frac{QL}{b}.
$$

Since $\gcd(Q,b)=\gcd(c,b)=1$, the second identity proves $b\mid L$. The first also works, since $\gcd(uv,b)=1$. Put $L=bK$. Comparing the target area to the tile area gives

$$
N=\frac{(cL)^2\sin(2\alpha)}{bc\sin\alpha}
=\frac{QL^2}{b}=bQK^2.
$$

Thus the complete necessary scale form in this branch is

$$
\boxed{N=bQK^2,\qquad (X,X,Y)=(bcK,bcK,bQK),
\qquad K\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.}
$$

This is a necessary form, not an assertion that every such scale is tileable. No squarefreeness of $b$ was used.

## 7. Three gluing transfers

These are existence constructions using whole tiled pieces. They neither require nor assert that an arbitrary tiling contains a removable block.

### 7.1 Theta at scale T gives W at scale T

Let $ABC$ be a tiled theta target, with angles $(\theta,\alpha,\theta)$, legs $AB=BC=bvT$, and base $AC=ubT$. Attach a copy of the tile enlarged by the integer factor $vT$ on the opposite side of $BC$, using its $b$-edge as the common side. Label its remaining vertex $D$, and match its $\alpha$ corner to $B$ and its $\gamma$ corner to $C$.

At $C$, $\theta+\gamma=\pi$, so $A,C,D$ are collinear with $C$ between $A,D$. At $B$, the union has angle $2\alpha<\pi$. Thus the union is the convex triangle $ABD$, with angles $(\theta,2\alpha,\beta)$ and sides

$$
AB=bvT,\qquad BD=cvT=v^3T,\qquad
AD=ubT+avT=uQT.
$$

The added block has $(vT)^2=cT^2$ tiles under its standard quadratic subdivision. The resulting count is $(b+c)T^2=QT^2$, exactly the W target at scale $T$.

### 7.2 W at scale T gives beta at scale T

In the resulting W triangle, attach another $vT$-fold tile along $AB=bvT$, with $\gamma$ at $A$ and $\alpha$ at $B$. The angle at $A$ becomes straight, while the angle at $B$ becomes $3\alpha<\pi$. The other two vertices both have angle $\beta$.

Both equal sides of the union have length $v^3T$. Its base has length

$$
uQT+avT=uPT.
$$

The count is $QT^2+cT^2=PT^2$, giving the beta-isosceles target at the same scale.

### 7.3 Theta at scale vK gives alpha at scale K

At theta scale $vK$, the sides are $(bcK,bcK,abK)$. Attach a $bK$-fold tile along its $a$-edge to the theta base $AC=abK$. Match its $\gamma$ corner to $A$ and $\beta$ corner to $C$; write $E$ for the new vertex.

At $A$, the sum $\theta+\gamma$ is $\pi$. At $C$, the sum is $\theta+\beta=\alpha+2\beta=\pi-2\alpha$. The other two corners, at the old apex $B$ and the new vertex $E$, are both $\alpha$. The resulting equal sides are

$$
BC=CE=bcK,
$$

and the base is

$$
BE=BA+AE=bcK+b^2K=bQK.
$$

The count is

$$
b(vK)^2+(bK)^2=b(c+b)K^2=bQK^2.
$$

All three attachments lie in opposite half-planes of their common side. Each has one straight combined endpoint and one combined angle strictly below $\pi$; consequently the union really is the stated convex triangle, with no overlap or hidden extra region. The two tilings may subdivide their common side differently: the resulting T-junctions are allowed.

## 8. Audit of the explicit eventual theta construction

Set

$$
\Delta=b(a^2+b^2)-a^2c=b^3-cu^4,\qquad
F=(a-1)(b-1),
$$

and suppose $\Delta>0$. Define the integer

$$
B=\left\lceil\frac{(a^2c+F)(a^2+b^2)}{u\Delta}\right\rceil.
$$

The proposed construction for every integer $T\ge B$ is a mixed-strip adaptation of the macrodissection in Beeson v4, Theorem 24, printed pages 88–89. I checked the source's definition of every piece and the following parameter substitutions.

### 8.1 An integer parameter always exists

Choose $J\in\mathbb Z$ in the closed interval

$$
\frac{uT}{a^2+b^2}\le J\le
\frac{ubT-F}{a^2c}.
$$

Its length is exactly

$$
\frac{uT\Delta-F(a^2+b^2)}{a^2c(a^2+b^2)}.
$$

The definition of $B$ makes this length at least one, so the interval contains an integer. Its lower endpoint is positive, hence $J\ge1$.

### 8.2 All triangular pieces are positive integer-scale tiles

Take the target parameter $\mu=bT/v$ and the source's green scale $p=a^2J$. Substitution in Theorem 24 gives the following five similar-triangle scales:

| Piece | Integer scale |
|---|---:|
| Green | $a^2J$ |
| Blue | $abJ$ |
| Red | $au^2J$ |
| Yellow | $vT-acJ$ |
| Pink | $uT-a^2J$ |

The source's auxiliary parameter $\mu$ need not be an integer. Each actual triangular scale in this table is an integer and therefore has a quadratic tiling by the original tile.

The upper interval bound gives

$$
ubT\ge a^2cJ+F>a^2cJ.
$$

Since $c>b$,

$$
\frac{a^2c}{ub}>av,
$$

so $T>avJ$. This makes both yellow and pink scales strictly positive. The first three are positive directly.

### 8.3 The two remaining parallelograms are tileable

The first has horizontal length

$$
L_0=ubT-a^2cJ\ge F
$$

and slanted length $H=abu^2J$. The positive integers $a,b$ are coprime. The Frobenius bound therefore supplies nonnegative integers $x,y$ with

$$
L_0=xa+yb.
$$

Split its horizontal length into $x$ strips of width $a$ and $y$ strips of width $b$. Its slanted length $H$ is divisible by both $a,b$. An $a$-strip uses $b$-steps along that length, and a $b$-strip uses $a$-steps. Every resulting cell has adjacent lengths $a,b$, included angle $\gamma$, and a difference diagonal of length $c$. It splits into two original tiles.

The final parallelogram has side lengths

$$
b(uT-a^2J),\qquad a\bigl[(a^2+b^2)J-uT\bigr].
$$

The first is a positive integer multiple of $b$. The second is a nonnegative integer multiple of $a$, by the lower interval bound. An ordinary grid of $a$-by-$b$ cells tiles it. If the second length is zero, this parallelogram is absent; the other pieces remain nondegenerate.

The macrodissection's interface identities are unchanged from Theorem 24. Its positivity conditions are precisely those verified above: the triangular scales are positive, the first parallelogram has positive base length, and the last has nonnegative width. Its pieces therefore have disjoint interiors and exhaust the theta target. No integrality of the source's auxiliary strip parameter $r$ is needed after the explicit mixed-strip subdivision. This is the step that enlarges the arithmetic coverage.

The target's equal side is $\mu c=bvT$, so its area is $bT^2$ tile areas. The construction therefore proves existence for **every integer $T\ge B$**, not merely an unspecified sequence of multiples.

As a numerical consistency check of the symbolic bound, $(u,v)=(1,2)$ gives $(a,b,c)=(2,3,4)$, $\Delta=23$, $F=2$, and $B=\lceil234/23\rceil=11$. This is a sufficient bound, not the optimal threshold for that tile.

## 9. Eventual theorem and its exact limits

For every fixed primitive tile with $\Delta>0$, combine Section 8 with the three whole-piece gluing transfers:

| Rational Group-1 target shape | Necessary count form | Scales proved tileable |
|---|---|---|
| W: $(2\alpha,\beta,\theta)$ | $QT^2$ | Every integer $T\ge B$ |
| Beta-isosceles | $PT^2$ | Every integer $T\ge B$ |
| Theta-isosceles | $bT^2$ | Every integer $T\ge B$ |
| Alpha-isosceles | $bQK^2$ | Every integer $K\ge\lceil B/v\rceil$ |
| Other scalene: $(2\alpha,\alpha,2\beta)$ | $QP K^2$ | Every integer $K\ge1$, by the separate two-piece construction |

For the alpha row, $K\ge\lceil B/v\rceil$ is exactly what ensures $vK\ge B$, so the theta input of Section 7.3 exists. The W and beta necessary forms come from their primitive integer side triples and the area ratio; this audit's new arithmetic work concerns the theta and alpha rows. The other-scalene exact spectrum is the separate result in [the two-piece construction](../two-piece-construction.md), independent of the hypothesis $\Delta>0$.

The eventual-gcd divisors for W and beta must therefore equal one in this range: their scale sets contain every integer beyond $B$, including consecutive integers. The result does not rely on the proposed scale-one impossibility proofs or the candidate prime classification.

The bound depends on the fixed tile. It leaves finitely many smaller scales for each tile, an unbounded collection of such finite ranges as $u,v$ vary, and the complementary $\Delta<0$ regime. There is no missing equality regime for a rational tile: $\Delta=0$ would make $x=b/c$ a rational root of $x^3-x^2+2x-1$, whose only possible rational roots $\pm1$ are not roots. The separate $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$ branches also remain outside this eventual theorem.

Thus the claim is a rigorous candidate for **cofinite scale coverage for each fixed tile in the stated range**, with the explicit bounds above. It is not a complete classification of every tile count in Erdős problem 634. The two direction characters and original macrogeometry are Beeson's published inputs; the divisibility deductions, mixed-strip construction and transfers must be attributed with that dependence. This review is internal, not external acceptance or formal verification.
